## Executive Summary

July 2026 showed rapid maturation of AI for fluid mechanics across three fronts: more trustworthy CFD surrogates, stronger physics-informed learning, and a widening push toward agentic and generative workflows. In CFD surrogates, papers moved beyond raw accuracy toward calibrated uncertainty, solver-agnostic training, and latent-space tradeoffs, while physics-informed methods tackled harder regimes such as shocks, inverse flow reconstruction, multiphase transport, and multiscale closure learning. Turbulence research was especially active, with new work spanning learned closures for LES/RANS, differentiable hybrid neural-CFD, generative modeling of turbulent fields and flames, and benchmark/dataset efforts for urban wind, multiphase flow, and control-oriented reduced models. A notable meta-trend is the integration of large language models, retrieval, and agentic systems into CFD tooling and discovery pipelines, suggesting the field is moving from isolated prediction models toward end-to-end scientific workflow augmentation.

## Field Progress

- Surrogate modeling is becoming more decision-ready through conformal prediction, residual-based trust scores, and uncertainty intervals.
- Physics-informed ML is extending to harder PDE regimes, especially shocks, multiphase flow, and inverse estimation from sparse data.
- Hybrid neural-CFD frameworks are increasingly differentiable end-to-end, enabling joint learning of closures and solver parameters.
- Benchmarking infrastructure is improving, with new datasets for urban wind, multiphase shock flows, and controlled wakes.
- Generative modeling is broadening from image-like synthesis to time-resolved flow and combustion trajectory generation.
- Interpretability is gaining importance, with operator-learning and turbulence-discovery methods explicitly designed to reveal mechanisms.
- LLM-based agents are starting to support practical CFD workflows, from OpenFOAM setup to physics-guided retrieval and analysis.

## Open Questions

- How can uncertainty estimates be made truly robust under distribution shift in CFD surrogate deployment?
- Which physics-informed strategies best handle shocks, discontinuities, and multiscale transport without sacrificing efficiency?
- Can differentiable hybrid solvers reliably learn closures that transfer across geometries, Reynolds numbers, and numerical discretizations?
- What benchmark protocols are needed to compare generative models for time-dependent flow and combustion realism, not just statistical similarity?
- How should agentic LLM systems be validated for correctness, reproducibility, and safety in scientific CFD workflows?
- Can interpretable operator-learning and discovery tools move from qualitative insight to actionable closure or design laws?

## Outlook

The July 2026 literature suggests AI fluid mechanics is transitioning from proof-of-concept prediction toward integrated scientific modeling systems that are reliable, interpretable, and workflow-aware. Near-term progress is likely to come from combining physics constraints, uncertainty quantification, and differentiable simulation with better benchmarks and richer datasets. The most impactful advances may be those that connect learned models to practical engineering decisions—design, control, certification, and automated solver setup—while preserving fidelity in the most difficult regimes such as shocks, turbulence closures, and multiphase dynamics.
