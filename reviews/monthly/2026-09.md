On the surrogate side, several papers pushed beyond pointwise prediction toward more operationally useful tools: benchmark creation for unsteady bluff-body flows, improved geometry-conditioned models for airfoils and turbomachinery, reduced-order sensing/assimilation, and methods that supervise models off-attractor to improve long-horizon stability. Turbulence research remained vibrant, with studies on dissipation anomalies, vorticity dynamics, wall-bounded flow control, roughness/thermal effects, and data-driven or theoretical advances in regularity and statistics. Overall, the month shows a field moving from isolated model demonstrations toward benchmarked, physics-aware, and deployment-oriented tools, while still grappling with stability, generalization, and faithful extrapolation to hard flow regimes.

## Field Progress

- Surrogates are increasingly judged by rollout stability, off-distribution behavior, and sensing/assimilation performance rather than only one-step accuracy.
- Benchmark datasets and challenge-style evaluations are becoming more common, signaling a move toward reproducibility and fair comparison.
- PINNs and physics-informed methods are broadening from toy problems to realistic elastic, biomedical, granular, and manufacturing flows, with more attention to stability tricks and representation design.
- Turbulence research is blending classical analysis with modern computation to isolate causal mechanisms such as vorticity amplification, dissipation scaling, and wall/roughness effects.
- There is growing convergence between CFD, reduced-order modeling, and machine learning in practical design loops for airfoils, turbomachinery, reactors, and biomedical flows.
- Solver modernization and HPC-aware implementations remain important infrastructure work that supports larger datasets, more simulations, and faster model development.

## Open Questions

- How can surrogate models be made reliably stable over very long rollouts in chaotic turbulent flows without repeated DNS supervision?
- What benchmarks and metrics best capture whether a learned model truly preserves physics, not just short-term accuracy?
- When should physics-informed methods prefer continuous automatic differentiation, and when are discretization-based constraints or hybrid formulations superior?
- Can generative data assimilation be made fully Bayesian and scalable enough for real-time turbulent flow inference?
- How can models generalize across geometry, Reynolds number, Mach number, multiphase state, and boundary-condition changes without retraining?
- Which numerical invariants and stability constraints are most important to preserve when learning constitutive laws or multi-species compressible fluxes?

## Outlook

The next stage of progress is likely to focus on trustworthy deployment: validated surrogates, calibrated physics-informed solvers, and assimilation methods that remain stable under distribution shift. Expect more benchmark datasets, more hybrid numerical-ML solvers that encode conservation and stability explicitly, and more application-specific methods targeting aerospace, energy, biomedical, and multiphase systems. The main bottleneck remains the same across subfields: achieving generalization and long-term fidelity while retaining computational efficiency.
