## Field Progress

- Surrogate modeling is shifting from generic function approximation toward physically structured architectures that respect geometry, boundaries, and multiscale flow features.
- Experimental datasets are increasingly being used as first-class benchmarks, not just synthetic DNS, which should improve external validity.
- Inverse and assimilation workflows are becoming more rigorous, with explicit treatment of sparsity, noise, observability, and parameter uncertainty.
- Generative modeling for turbulence is improving in spectral realism, especially at small scales, though fidelity-cost tradeoffs remain a major issue.
- Classical turbulence theory is still advancing: new results on transition thresholds, intermittency, adjoint chaos, and closure mechanisms continue to inform ML design.
- Control-oriented ML is starting to confront operational constraints, revealing that closed-loop stability can be a more important metric than reconstruction error.

## Open Questions

- How can surrogate models be trained to remain accurate under closed-loop distribution shift, not just offline validation?
- What is the best way to encode wall, boundary-layer, and anisotropic near-wall physics into neural operators without sacrificing generality?
- Can generative turbulence models reproduce both inertial-range and dissipation-range statistics while staying computationally efficient?
- How should inverse PINNs and data-assimilation methods quantify identifiability limits when observations are sparse, noisy, or partial?
- What benchmark suite would meaningfully compare models across DNS, experiments, moving boundaries, multiphase flows, and compressible regimes?
- How can ML-based closures be made interpretable enough to connect reliably with classical turbulence mechanisms?

## Outlook

The field is moving toward more disciplined and application-oriented AI for fluids, with a clear premium on physical structure, validation against real data, and robustness in operational settings. In the next few months, the most impactful work will likely be the models and benchmarks that can demonstrate not only improved accuracy, but also stability, uncertainty awareness, and transfer across geometries, regimes, and sensing conditions.
