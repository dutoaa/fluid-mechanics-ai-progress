## Major Themes

- Physics by construction: exact conservation projections, divergence-free representations, convex dissipation potentials, and boundary-compatible formulations aim to restrict learning to physically admissible solutions.
- Geometry-aware and task-specific surrogates: centerline attention, finite-volume convolutions, operator splitting, and coarse-mesh graph networks tailor computation to complex domains and required observables.
- Agentic scientific workflows: LLM agents are explored for proposing and evaluating turbulence closures, while other studies investigate agent-assisted inverse modeling and measurement selection.
- Physics-informed learning under scrutiny: filtering, coefficient-induced gradient imbalance, and regional solution errors challenge the assumption that a small residual ensures an accurate flow.
- Probabilistic turbulence reconstruction: generative approaches target distributions of plausible instantaneous fields, with growing emphasis on validation beyond visual realism and low-order statistics.
- Data and differentiable infrastructure: pore-scale datasets, physiologically consistent synthetic CFD, experimental assimilation, and CUDA differentiation support training and inverse analysis.
- State-aware control and sensing: enriched dynamic features, filtering, sparse-sensor reconstruction, and task-aware sampling connect learned models to actionable flow objectives.

## Field Progress

- Across the collection, physical structure is increasingly embedded in architectures, projections, and parameterizations rather than supplied only through residual penalties. The approaches address different guarantees and should not be treated as interchangeable.
- Surrogate design is becoming more selective about both representation and output: geometry-specific attention, mesh-compatible convolutions, and sparse-observable prediction avoid unnecessary full-field computation.
- Learning is being integrated more tightly with established scientific workflows, including multifidelity assimilation, fractional-step solvers, PDE-constrained inversion, and adjoint differentiation.
- Reported PINN failure mechanisms and coarse-graining analysis sharpen the distinction between satisfying a chosen training objective and accurately representing the intended physical system.
- Generative models and shared simulation datasets broaden the scope from single best-estimate fields toward turbulent variability and reproducible evaluation, although the supplied metadata do not establish mature common benchmarks.
- Conventional CFD and turbulence studies remain important context: entrance effects, boundary-layer scaling, numerical sensitivity, and multiphase modeling identify physical regimes and numerical biases that AI methods must respect.

## Open Questions

- How can learned closures remain valid when resolution, filtering, Reynolds number, geometry, or forcing changes, particularly when nonlinear governing terms are not closed on the training fields?
- Which hard constraints improve generalization, and what additional stability or error analysis is needed beyond conservation, divergence freedom, or a small PDE residual?
- Can agent-discovered turbulence models demonstrate reproducible gains on genuinely unseen flow families after accounting for human guidance, search cost, and candidate-selection bias?
- What benchmarks can jointly assess generative turbulence models for dynamics, intermittency, extreme events, calibrated uncertainty, and long-time statistics?
- How well do geometry-aware biomedical and engineering surrogates transfer from synthetic training domains to measured geometries, uncertain boundary conditions, and experimental observations?
- Do reported control and inference gains persist under sensor noise, actuation limits, model mismatch, and end-to-end computational or energy accounting?

## Outlook

The most promising direction suggested by this collection is tighter coupling among learned representations, admissible physics, and established numerical methods. Near-term evaluation should prioritize unseen geometries and flow regimes, resolution-aware constraints, regional and long-horizon accuracy, and complete accounting of training, search, inference, and control costs. Agentic modeling and generative turbulence methods merit attention, but their value will depend on reproducible workflows and stronger validation than selected test-case improvements. Because many supplied abstracts end before their results and limitations, the full papers are needed before drawing firm conclusions about comparative superiority or deployment readiness.
