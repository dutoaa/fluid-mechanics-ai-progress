A 3D PINN-Based Framework for Patient-Specific Pulmonary Hemodynamics Using Windkessel Models

Celia Taboada, Luis Nuñez, Angélica Casero, Pedro Navas

Cardiovascular fluid dynamics modeling relies heavily on mesh-based numerical methods such as Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD). While accurate, these methods entail significant computational costs and complex meshing workflows that limit their translation to clinical practice. Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) are a promising meshless alternative that embeds the governing equations directly into neural network training. In this work, we propose a PINN framework for pulsatile blood flow in patient-specific geometries with dynamic lumped-parameter Windkessel (RCR) outlet conditions representing distal vascular impedance. Coupling the Windkessel ODE to a 3D network raises two difficulties: the ODE alone does not fix the pressure level, and the local pressure variations that drive the flow are orders of magnitude smaller than the basal pressure imposed by the outlet. We address them by decomposing pressure into a time-dependent basal part and a spatially varying part, an initial condition and gauge for the Windkessel equation, a global mass-balance constraint, and a periodic Fourier embedding of time. Validated on 2D canonical channels (steady and pulsatile, with and without RCR outlet), the framework is then applied to a patient-specific right pulmonary artery, reproducing outlet flow, mean pressure, and the velocity and dynamic pressure fields with errors on the order of the data resolution. Finally, we solve the inverse problem of identifying the three Windkessel parameters of the outlet from synthetic data, recovering them with errors of about 0.3% from deliberately wrong initial values. Residual biases in the local pressure field and the need to test robustness to noisy clinical data remain open, but the results support PINNs as complementary tools for meshless hemodynamics and patient-specific digital twins.

Numerical Study of Flow and Cavitation in Single-Liquid Injectors with Sharp-Edged and Chamfered Orifice Inlets

Shreyash Gulati, Sven R. Walsberg, Timothy W. Nearhood, Jeffrey D. Moore

In the current work, the investigators used Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) to investigate water flow and cavitation in single-liquid injectors with sharp-edged and chamfered orifice inlets. The k-ω Shear Stress Transport (SST) turbulence model was used along with the Zwart-Gerber-Belamri (ZGB) cavitation model and the Volume of Fluid (VOF) method. The CFD predictions for the volumetric flow rate were validated against existing experimental data for the sharp-edged and chamfered orifice inlets. Pressure drops ranging between 0.07 MPa and 0.59 MPa were considered along with orifice length-to-diameter ratios of 2, 5, and 10. Strong cavitation was observed for the sharp-edged orifice inlet cases with a length-to-diameter ratio of 5 and a pressure drop larger than 0.2 MPa. A hydraulic flip was observed for the sharp-edged orifice inlet with a length-to-diameter ratio of 5 and a pressure drop of 0.59 MPa, which was consistent with the findings from the previous experimental study. Cavitation inception was observed for the chamfered orifice inlet cases with a length-to-diameter ratio of 5 and pressure drops larger than 0.2 MPa. For the sharp-edged orifice inlet with orifice length-to-diameter ratios of 2 and 10, a strong cavitation was observed inside the orifice.

Scalable Modal Analysis of the Imperial Front Wing Flow Field

Parv Khurana, Benet Eiximeno, Alexandra Liosi, Spencer Sherwin, Athanasios Chatzopoulos, Julien Hoessler

The aerodynamic performance of motorsport vehicles relies on understanding the unsteady flow dynamics around complex geometries at high Reynolds numbers. Extracting the dominant 3D coherent structures from high-fidelity simulations is often intractable due to the high dimensionality of the state-space, and standard modal decompositions, such as proper orthogonal decomposition (POD), do not give interpretable meaning to the temporal dynamics of the modes. This study demonstrates a scalable POD and Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) method, implemented in the open-source pyLOM framework, that can identify coherent structures and their temporal dynamics in high-fidelity simulations of Formula 1 aerodynamics, specifically the Imperial Front Wing (IFW), a publicly available benchmark based on the McLaren MP4-17D, at Reynolds number $Re = 2.2 \times 10^5$. DMD is applied to large eddy simulation (LES) results of the flow field that is resolved using spectral/hp element method via Nektar++, producing $49.72 \times 10^6$ degrees of freedom at polynomial order p=4. The simulation captures four dominant vortical structures (endplate, canard, main, and footplate), with time-averaged force coefficients showing 2.24% and 2.87% differences in $C_L$ and $C_D$ compared to the reference data. DMD extracts the frequencies, amplitudes and damping ratios of the coherent structures, and modes at chord-based Strouhal numbers of 5.1, 7.5 and 9.3 are associated, through point-probe spectra, with the main-element laminar separation bubble, canard vortex core pulsation and endplate vortex meander, respectively. The DMD allows for both a model reduction technique and a compression scheme, here reducing approximately 6 TB of snapshots to 200 GB of modal data.

Measuring and Improving Transfer of Neural Surrogates for Vehicle Aerodynamics

Favour Nerrise, Julian Busch, Gurpreet Singh Hora, Martin Sipka, Danielle C. Maddix, Georgia Olympia Brikis

Neural surrogates for external vehicle aerodynamics are fast alternatives to physics-based computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations. Their foundational limitation is a lack of zero-shot generalization across vehicle classes, which results in an expensive, custom training campaign for each new vehicle design. Similar to pretrained models in other fields, a pretrained surrogate should adapt to a new vehicle class using only a few simulations and stay accurate on its pretraining domain. No prior study has systematically tested this transfer behavior. We conduct a large-scale, controlled benchmarking study on five DrivAerML-pretrained surrogates across four independent target datasets. These datasets differ from the source in geometry, CFD solver, turbulence model, and flow conditions. We find that fine-tuning these surrogates outperforms training from scratch in fewer than half of the comparisons and lowers the accuracy of the surrogate on its source dataset. We introduce GeoReplay, a geometry- and gradient-aligned source replay that keeps source simulations resembling the target in training and applies their updates only when they agree with the target gradient. A risk bound separates geometric coverage from differences in the simulated physics, and we prove that every sufficiently small GeoReplay step decreases the target training objective. GeoReplay lowers the source error of fine-tuning in 77 of 80 settings and matches its target accuracy.

Failure Modes and Data-Efficient Remedies for Transformer-Based Prediction of Viscoplastic Flows

Maedeh Saberi, Hugo Franca, Maziyar Jalaal, Safa Jamali

Fast and accurate prediction of viscoplastic flows remains a challenge in computational rheology due to yield surfaces, discontinuous gradients, and strongly nonlinear transitions between yielded and unyielded regions. Transformer-based neural operators such as RheOFormer have recently shown strong potential for surrogate modeling of non-Newtonian flows. However, their applicability to viscoplastic systems and their efficacy in recovering realistic flows remain poorly understood. In this work, we present the first systematic investigation of RheOFormer applied to steady Bingham channel flow. By isolating the problem to a simple geometry and constitutive model, we examine how dataset structure, numerical value range, and input representation govern the model's ability to interpolate and extrapolate within the parameter space. The data regimes in which RheOFormer remains accurate and those in which errors emerge are identified, particularly for plug-dominated flows and for conditions outside the velocity scales or flow structures represented during training. Upon identifying these failure modes, targeted enhancements to the input representation and loss formulation are introduced to better predict low-gradient plug regions. Logarithmic velocity representations and gradient-aware loss formulations substantially improve accuracy in challenging regimes where the baseline formulation fails. In addition, extrapolation errors are reduced by augmenting the training set with only a few strategically selected cases spanning the missing physical regimes. Finally, a single partially developed snapshot suffices for an accurate steady-state prediction, and the model outperforms simply copying its input throughout flow development. These results highlight the importance of appropriate field representation and parameter-space coverage for data-efficient neural-operator prediction of viscoplastic flows.

Stable eigenmodes of the Large-scale circulation in Turbulent Convection

Yin Wang, Pik-Yin Lai, Penger Tong

Turbulent Rayleigh-Bénard convection (RBC) is a canonical system for studying buoyancy-driven turbulence. Its defining feature is a system-size large-scale circulation (LSC), whose dynamics reflect the coupling between coherent motion and turbulent fluctuations. Starting from the Boussinesq Navier-Stokes and temperature equations, we derive three coupled stochastic equations for the LSC angular velocity and its associated dipolar temperature field. The deterministic dynamics contain an unstable no-LSC set and two symmetry-related stable finite-LSC states. Linearization about either finite-LSC state yields one purely relaxational eigenmode and a pair of damped oscillatory eigenmodes whose frequency is set by the LSC turnover rate. Turbulent fluctuations continually excite the latter, providing a dynamical origin for the observed quasi-periodic oscillations. Experiments in a thin-disk cell directly resolve both eigenmodes and show their predicted Rayleigh-number scalings. The same stochastic dynamics also provide a route to rare cessations and reversals. This theory-experiment framework provides a tractable example of how coherent circulation states, intrinsic modes, and fluctuation-driven transitions emerge in turbulent convection, with broader relevance to geophysical and astrophysical flows.

A centered finite-volume method with explicit pressure relaxation for weakly compressible single- and two-phase flows

Shintaro Matsushita, Suhas S. Jain, Andrei G. Fedorov, Tetsuya Suekane

We present a non-dissipative centered finite-volume method for weakly compressible single- and two-phase flows. The method couples kinetic-energy-preserving advection to conservative diffuse-interface regularization and explicit pressure relaxation on structured collocated grids. To control excessive phase-mass outflow when neighboring cells have different intrinsic densities, we construct phase-mass fluxes using harmonic face densities. This connects phase-volume and phase-mass transport without altering the advective kinetic-energy identity, yielding a sufficient positivity condition and an inverse-density transport identity at finite velocity divergence. To retain the benefit of low-dissipation transport in the coupled calculation, a pressure-increment formulation incorporates the existing pressure gradient in the predictor. Compatible cell and face operators give a conditional energy estimate for additional pressure iterations, while fully discrete budgets separate advection, predictor pressure work, and the final correction. In inviscid homogeneous isotropic turbulence on $128^3$ cells, the relative kinetic-energy change remains below $1.2\times10^{-7}$ in magnitude at $t^*=8$ in both single-phase and density-ratio-$1000$ two-phase calculations. Matched upwind calculations retain only $3.60\%$ and $30.72\%$ of their initial energies, respectively. Shear layers, cavities, rising bubbles, and dam breaks demonstrate applicability to incompressible-regime flows. In a three-dimensional dam break, harmonic averaging retains positive phase masses where arithmetic averaging produces negative values. With centered advection, a prescribed subgrid-scale stress supplies the principal dissipation. The local pressure algorithm and collocated finite-volume form support future large-scale multiphase large-eddy simulation on GPUs with adaptive mesh refinement.

Compressibility effects on the flow-induced motions of bluff bodies: common features from transverse and rotary oscillations

Sawant Omkar Deepak, Swagata Bhaumik

Compressibility effects on flow-induced motions of bluff bodies are examined through two-dimensional direct numerical simulations of undamped transverse vortex-induced vibration (TVIV) of a circular cylinder ($Re=150$) and vortex-induced rotary oscillation (VIRO) of an elliptic cylinder of aspect ratio $0.5$ ($Re=200$) for free-stream Mach numbers $0.1\leq M_{\infty}\leq 0.6$. Response characteristics are analyzed across the first desynchronized zone ($DS\text{-}I$), the synchronous (lock-in) branch ($SB$), and the second desynchronized zone ($DS\text{-}II$). While compressibility effects are noted to stabilize the oscillations in $SB$ in terms of reductions in response amplitudes and fluctuating aerodynamic coefficients, as well as the extent and intensity of hysteretic jumps, in contrast, the oscillation amplitudes and aerodynamic coefficients increase with $M_{\infty}$ in $DS\text{-}II$, where the amplitude variation with $M_{\infty}$ is shown to follow the \textit{modified Prandtl-Glauert relationship} for both TVIV and VIRO cases. This indicates the role of the inviscid nature of compressibility in the $DS\text{-}II$ regime in increasing the oscillation response, as opposed to $SB$ where enhanced viscous stabilization with increasing $M_{\infty}$ leads to the moderation of response. Although the simulations are performed at low Reynolds numbers, the identified trends are consistent with fundamental compressibility mechanisms and may therefore persist at higher Reynolds numbers.

Discrete adjoint method for the general synthetic iterative scheme for optimization of rarefied and continuum gas flows

Jianan Zeng, Ruifeng Yuan, Wei Su

A discrete adjoint method is developed for the general synthetic iterative scheme (GSIS) to enable efficient gradient-based optimization of gas flows across rarefied and continuum regimes. The primal GSIS is expressed as a fixed-point system of kinetic and macroscopic synthetic equations, and both sets of residuals are incorporated into a discrete Lagrangian. Imposing stationarity of this Lagrangian then yields a coupled mesoscopic-macroscopic adjoint system that directly inherits the primal acceleration structure, without a separate Chapman-Enskog or asymptotic analysis. The resulting system is solved by alternating kinetic and macroscopic updates analogous to those in the primal GSIS, with the inner subproblems treated using established discrete adjoint techniques for the discrete velocity method and Navier-Stokes equations. Tests on the NACA0012 airfoil show that adjoint sensitivities closely agree with central finite-difference estimates, while the adjoint solver achieves convergence efficiency comparable to that of the primal GSIS, with both solvers converging within tens of iterations in rarefied and near-continuum flows. The method is further validated by multi-objective shape optimization and boundary-flow-parameter optimization. For a three-dimensional re-entry capsule, shape optimization reduces peak wall heat flux by 14.17%, 10.68%, and 6.54% while achieving respective lift-to-drag gains of 10%, 20%, and 25%. For a fixed-geometry showerhead chamber, optimization of inlet-flow allocation reduces the coefficient of variation of wafer pressure from 1.49% to 0.06% at fixed total gas supply, while maintaining the mean pressure near its initial value. Beyond these applications, the formulation provides a systematic and extensible framework for constructing multiscale adjoint solvers directly from the fixed-point representations of their primal counterparts.

Oceanic Surface Waves from Wind-Drift-Layer Instability: Exponential Growth to Finite-Amplitude States

Sayali N. Jadhav, S. Ravichandran, Ratul Dasgupta

Oceanic wind-wave genesis via shear-induced linear instability and the propagation and breaking of finite-amplitude waves have traditionally been studied as decoupled problems. While experimental evidence indicates connection between the two regimes, a first principles demonstration that the latter can emerge from the former remains challenging. Here, we bridge this gap by investigating gravity-dominated surface waves arising from an inviscid, shear-driven Holmboe instability. The background state comprises a uniform air current and a constant-vorticity water drift layer of finite thickness overlying deep irrotational water. In the high-Bond-number and low-Froude-number regime, this configuration supports Holmboe-unstable modes beyond a critical Froude number. Two-phase simulations of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, initialised with the fastest-growing eigenmode, exhibit initial exponential growth in excellent agreement with linear theory. In the subsequent nonlinear stage preceding breaking, finite-amplitude propagating waveforms develop. Spatio-temporal spectral analysis reveals bound harmonics locked to the primary carrier wave, coexisting with residual free-wave components. This indicates that an infinitesimal, linearly unstable mode can evolve into a nonlinear propagating waveform of approximately permanent form under background shear. To characterise these waveforms theoretically, we formulate an exact nonlinear boundary-value problem for steady-state propagating waves including background shear. The resulting wave profiles show very good agreement with time-averaged profiles extracted from the transient simulations in the nonlinear stage. These findings extend the existing theory and complement the wind-wave observations, demonstrating that sheared Stokes-like waves, can naturally emerge during the nonlinear evolution of a wind-drift-induced linear instability.

AI Meets Turbulence Modeling: Balancing Causation, Correlation, and User Intent

Sharath S. Girimaji

Turbulence states vary widely in modeling difficulty, requiring distinct paradigms; thus, no single AI or data-driven approach suits all flows. We propose an operational framework classifying data-driven turbulence modeling by balancing physical causation and statistical correlation via non-equilibrium flow memory and user intent. Using the Mori--Zwanzig projection formalism, we decompose unclosed turbulent stress into a local response ($τ_{ij}^L$) and a spatio-temporally non-local memory integral ($τ_{ij}^{NL}$). Near statistical equilibrium, rapid relaxation suppresses advective memory, letting local correlations capture physical causation via intrusive $τ_{ij}^L$ constitutive relations in Reynolds-Averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) equations. Because the elliptic Poisson equation for fluctuating pressure couples stress evolution to eddy dimensionality beyond single-point velocity-gradient componentiality, local closures are not strictly valid even at equilibrium, yet remain acceptable engineering approximations. In non-equilibrium turbulence, modeling bifurcates by user intent. For predicting unseen geometries, large coherent structures sustain non-local memory ($τ_{ij}^{NL}$), invalidating local total-stress closures and requiring first-principles causal scale resolution guided by Rules-Augmented AI (RAAI), with resolution set by a recent non-equilibrium theory. Conversely, for rapid design in a known, parameterized coherent flow class, a purely correlational strategy suffices: combining Non-Intrusive Reduced-Order Modeling (NIROM) with uncentered Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) across multi-realization ensembles yields zero-solver synthesis of stationary statistics and unsteady coherent evolution from governing dimensionless parameters. This framework aligns physical causation, statistical correlation, and user intent across flow regimes.
