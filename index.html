TurtLES: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Turbulent 3D Neural PDE Surrogates

Jarno Platenburg, Armand Kassaï Koupaï, Paola Cinnella, Patrick Gallinari

Machine-learning surrogates are increasingly used to accelerate computational fluid dynamics, yet progress is limited by the lack of benchmarks capturing realistic, time-dependent turbulent flows. We introduce TurtLES, a 14 TB dataset of 3D turbulent cylinder wakes in the shear-layer transition regime, computed via high-fidelity implicit large-eddy simulations. The dataset contains 400 trajectories of 400 time steps each, with 3-11 million points per frame, spanning variations in geometry, Reynolds number, and angle of attack. Unlike existing datasets, TurtLES combines (i) physically realistic turbulence with an active energy cascade driven by vortex stretching, (ii) dense irregular meshes, and (iii) long-horizon temporal dynamics. We benchmark state-of-the-art neural operators on autoregressive rollouts and identify key failure modes, including temporal instability, loss of small-scale energy, and challenges in learning on large irregular domains. TurtLES provides a new benchmark for evaluating surrogate models on 3D turbulent wakes at industrially relevant mesh resolution.

Projection-based segregated, iterative, and multi-stage physics-informed neural network for incompressible Navier-stokes equations

Zhenyu Zhang, Zhixiang Ling, Yinghao Huo, Huayong Liu, Yunde Su

Physics-informed neural networks(PINNs) offer a mesh-free and continuously differentiable approach to fluid simulation. However, standard monolithic PINNs for incompressible Navier-Stokes (NS) equations often suffer from severe constraint coupling and spectral biases, limiting both convergence and accuracy. To address this challenge, we propose a segregated, iterative, and multi-stage PINN (SIM-PINN) inspired by the classical projection method in computational fluid dynamics. In SIM-PINN, the nonlinear NS equations are decoupled into the linearized momentum and pressure Poisson equations. Each is addressed separately using dedicated multi-stage neural networks and coupled through projection-based inner iterations. Each stage of the multi-stage neural networks for the momentum equations corresponds to one iteration of the projection method, in which the velocity fields are progressively refined at targeted frequencies. Upon iteration convergence, solutions to the fully coupled and nonlinear NS equations are recovered. By integrating the segregated and iterative framework with the multi-stage training, SIM-PINN alleviates the optimization difficulties of the incompressible NS equations arising from nonlinearity, constraint coupling, and spectral biases. The improved convergence and accuracy are demonstrated by simulations of steady lid-driven cavity and unsteady Kolmogorov flows.

Elastic frontogenesis sets the spectra of smooth elastic turbulence

Marco Edoardo Rosti, Andrea Mazzino

Most turbulent flows are sustained by inertia, which transfers energy across scales and produces characteristic power-law spectra. Yet dilute polymer solutions also exhibit turbulence when inertia is negligible: in this state, known as elastic turbulence, the velocity field remains spatially smooth while both the velocity and the polymer conformation exhibit robust power-law spectra. How a smooth flow, lacking a hierarchy of eddies, generates such multiscale structure has remained unexplained. Here we show that the missing mechanism is elastic frontogenesis, whereby the flow spontaneously organizes polymer stress into thin sheets that are stretched within their plane and compressed across their thickness, causing their amplitude to grow as they become thinner. Because the fronts are sheet-like structures, this amplification-thinning law uniquely determines the polymer conformation spectrum, while the Stokes response transfers the same geometric scaling to the velocity field, reconciling broadband spectra with spatial smoothness. Being geometric rather than model-specific, elastic frontogenesis provides an organizing principle for viscoelastic flows and the viscoelastic analogue of vortex stretching in inertial turbulence.

Weiheng Li, Zhiyao Jiang, Bai Song

Embedded microfluidic cooling is a promising technology for dissipating the intense heat generated by high-performance electronic chips. However, the design and optimization of such coolers impose prohibitive computational costs when using conventional numerical solvers. Here, we present a parameterized physics-informed neural network (PINN) as a deep learning framework for rapidly and accurately predicting the thermohydraulic performance of embedded manifold microchannels. By incorporating four geometric dimensions along with three spatial coordinates as the network inputs, the proposed model enables multiphysics prediction across a continuous design space. Key to this framework is a composite strategy combining geometric ansatz embedding, the SOAP optimizer, and a thermal-conductivity-based curriculum learning scheme, which helps overcome the convergence challenges inherent in training PINNs for complex three-dimensional flows. Notably, by leveraging a highly optimized JAX backend, the network achieves convergence on a single consumer-grade GPU in less than four hours. Our approach offers a physics-consistent, real-time surrogate model that can substantially accelerate innovations in advanced microfluidic cooling of future electronics.

Why fluid relaxation modes matter: Nonlinear frequency response, dynamical integrity, and dynamic pull-in of electrostatic microbeams with squeeze-film damping

Fehmi Najar

The squeeze film between an electrostatically actuated microbeam can both induce damping and add stiffness to the structure. In addition, the dynamics of the film can interact with the nonlinear response of the microbeam. This paper extends a reduced-order model built on the complex modes of the coupled fluid-structure problem to the computation of periodic responses and their stability for clamped-clamped microbeams. Periodic orbits are continued in the frequency domain, and their stability is determined from their Floquet multipliers. The robustness of the response is assessed through basins of attraction, integrity measures, and dynamic pull-in maps in the plane of the excitation parameters. The model is validated against the measured and computed frequency response of a microswitch. Closed-form expressions are derived for the fluid relaxation modes, together with a correction for strongly coupled films, and define a parameter that characterizes the regime of the film. The approach is applied to different microbeams including cases where fluid modes relax on the timescale of the oscillation. For all simulated devices, a single fluid mode is necessary and sufficient, whereas adding coupled modes cannot replace it, whether the fluid modes respond quasi-statically or carry their own dynamics. Removing the fluid mode displaces both bifurcations that bound the pull-in band, changes the size of the basins of attraction and the position of the attractor within them, misses an abrupt erosion of the safe basin under increasing excitation, and shifts the whole region of dynamic pull-in in the excitation plane. The reduced model, with three real unknowns, makes these global analyses affordable for a fully coupled fluid-structure system.

Variational principles for dissipative continua: transport theorems, inertia, and the minimum-maximum duality

Wolfgang Flachberger, Lukas Einkemmer

Variational principles for dissipative processes come in two families: minimum principles at nonequilibrium stationary states (Helmholtz, Kirchhoff, Prigogine) and maximum principles for relaxation in continuum mechanics (Ziegler, Hackl, Fischer). We contribute to the understanding of the thermodynamic extremal principle (TEP) by investigating its extremal properties, and uncover the connection between the two families, whose distinction was recently made precise for diffusion: the extremal character is decided by the regime, which is either relaxation at frozen state, as in a closed system (maximum dissipation), or a driven stationary state in an open system (minimum dissipation), while the underlying Rayleighian functional is convex and minimized in both cases. We further examine the Reynolds transport theorem, a useful tool for fixing the energy rates of continua, and the role of inertia, recently argued to lie beyond the reach of Rayleighian principles. With inertia the driving force must be held fixed during the variation, which is less elegant than in the inertialess case, where it is a state function. Writing the energy rate as rates times driving forces still yields the correct equations, and an extremal statement survives whenever the frozen force does not depend on the varied rate. This holds directly for unsteady Stokes flow, and for Navier-Stokes flow after a change to material coordinates, in which the particle acceleration is the second time derivative of the state and the convective term, nonvariational in the Eulerian description, does not appear. The framework is tested on the Navier-Stokes family (Stokes, unsteady Stokes, and Navier-Stokes flow, in order of increasing complexity) and on dissipative Lagrangian mechanics.

Parametric spatio-temporal reduced-order modelling via Spectral Proper Orthogonal Decomposition and Gaussian Process Regression

Guglielmo Padula, Antonio Colanera, Gianluigi Rozza

We present SPOD-GPR, a non-intrusive, parametric, spatio-temporal reduced-order model (ROM) that combines Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD), Spectral POD (SPOD), and Gaussian Process Regression (GPR). A parametric POD first projects the high-dimensional state onto a low-dimensional subspace. The reduced trajectories are then divided into windowed sections, transformed into the frequency domain, and truncated to the most energetic frequencies. SPOD is then performed independently at each retained frequency, yielding temporally coherent modes and coefficients. The parametric dependence of the POD and SPOD bases is modelled by GPR on a local chart of the Grassmann manifold after Procrustes alignment. The SPOD modal coefficients are learned both as explicit functions of the parameters and through a parameter-conditioned autoregressive model that allows for temporal extrapolation beyond the training window. Gaussian processes are sampled jointly and pathwise via Matheron conditioning, ensuring that each predictive realization carries uncertainty quantification consistent with the posterior distribution. The method is tested on four parametric examples where the parameter values and the prediction horizon lie outside the training data: the two-dimensional anisotropic Kuramoto--Sivashinsky equation in a time-periodic and a travelling-wave regime, each parametrised by one of its two coefficients; flow past a cylinder parametrised by the Reynolds number; and flow past an elliptic obstacle parametrised by its semi-axes. In all cases, SPOD-GPR reduces the normalised mean absolute error compared to a POD-GPR baseline, and improves the calibration of the predictive uncertainty, especially in the fluid flow cases, where POD-GPR is severely overconfident. This opens the way to spectral, uncertainty-aware reduced-order models for many-query and real-time applications, such as digital twins.

Phase-resolved turbulence dynamics in cardiac-cycle-inspired pulsatile pipe flow

Xinyu Wang, Yan Bao, Benlong Wang

Direct numerical simulations examine the phase-dependent turbulence dynamics of pipe flow driven by cardiac-cycle-inspired waveforms. Four cases with and without a low-flow plateau span two Reynolds-number ranges. The response follows four stages of turbulent--turbulent reorganisation. Strong acceleration suppresses transverse fluctuations and vortical activity. During acceleration and early deceleration, streamwise-normal-stress production recovers ahead of pressure--strain redistribution and radial momentum transport, allowing energy to accumulate in elongated streaks. In early deceleration, near-wall high-speed streaks develop predominantly varicose disturbances associated with Q4 sweeps, whereas outer-buffer-layer low-speed streaks develop predominantly sinuous disturbances associated with Q2 ejections. The transition exhibits distinct radial sequences: Q2-based local onset occurs progressively later towards the wall, whereas Q4-based onset is nearly simultaneous across the viscous sublayer and buffer layer, with peaks occurring successively outwards. The two responses overlap between the drag minimum and recovery maximum as redistribution intensifies markedly and multiscale momentum transport recovers rapidly. Turbulence then decays and its scales readjust. Where present, the plateau prolongs this decay and weakens the turbulence inherited by the next acceleration stage. It further delays local instability and lengthens the onset-to-peak intervals of the shear contributions. An unsteady friction decomposition links these phase-dependent dynamics to the drag response, highlighting the role of delayed turbulent shear recovery in sustaining the low-drag interval.

Flash evaporation Riemann Problem: Thermodynamic Relaxation and Oscillation-Free Real Fluid Finite Volume Method

Haotong Bai, Ping Yi, Yixin Yang, Guoyan Zhao, Wenjia Xie, Mingbo Sun

The Flash evaporation Riemann problem (FeRP) formulated the wave structures of flash evaporation in the vapor-liquid homogeneous equilibrium limit. However, physical flash evaporation involves finite-rate interphase heat and mass transfer, inducing the wave structure to deviate from the equilibrium state. To solve such a non-equilibrium Riemann problem, this paper first derives the governing equation of mechanical equilibrium evolution in a relaxation system, based on which an oscillation-free Homogeneous Relaxation Real Fluid (HRRF) finite volume method is developed. In addition to the Euler equations with mass relaxation, the HRRF method evolves advection equations for the internal energy--pressure affine parameters and the vapor volume fraction, in order to eliminate numerical pressure oscillations inherent in conservative methods while preserving phase-transition-induced pressure changes, and consistently degenerate into a completely pressure-equilibrium method in supercritical flows. The HRRF method is then employed to comprehensively evaluate how thermodynamic relaxation affects the FeRP solution. Results indicate that the non-classical expansion shock of the FeRP loses its traveling-wave characteristics with increasing relaxation time, continuously degenerating into a self-similar rarefaction wave. Compared with the equilibrium limit, an increased mass transfer relaxation time smooths the expansion branch of the RP solution, lowers the intermediate pressure and velocity, and induces a pseudo-splitting of the rarefaction wave as well as a pseudo-advance of the phase transition onset. Meanwhile, the heat transfer relaxation time primarily dictates the temperature difference in the vapor-liquid mixture, thereby modulating the liquid superheat and in turn influencing the mass transfer rate.

Instantaneous SFS fields further reveal that local extrema arise from the combined contributions of multiple decomposed terms. Velocity gradient tensor reconstruction from tracer orientations via Jeffery dynamics

Isshin Arai, Tomoaki Itano

The velocity gradient tensor is a local quantity, but methods that obtain it by spatial differencing of the velocities of spherical tracers amplify measurement noise. We propose a method that reconstructs the velocity gradient tensor from the orientations of non-spherical tracers such as flakes and rods. Based on the Jeffery-type equation, we show that, for incompressible flow, all components of the velocity gradient tensor are uniquely determined from the orientations of four tracers and their time derivatives in the neighbourhood of a reference point (orientation-based method). The method uses neither tracer positions nor spatial differencing. It therefore requires orientation measurements only near the reference point, without requiring measurements throughout a three-dimensional region, and measurement noise is amplified only through temporal differencing. Consequently, the minimum achievable error scales as $σ^{1/2}$ with the noise level $σ$, compared with $σ^{1/3}$ for the positional PTV-plus-differencing approach (position-based method). Numerical tests further show that the vorticity is reconstructed more accurately than by the position-based method, whereas the strain rate is reconstructed with comparable accuracy. Finally, applying the method to spherical Couette flow, we find that the reconstruction fails where the neighbouring tracers align, which renders the inversion ill-conditioned, and that this degeneracy appears as the characteristic light-and-dark pattern familiar from flow visualization.

Oleg N. Kirillov

When heat and solute both destabilise a rapidly rotating fluid, do their buoyancies add? Codensity models assume so, giving a straight onset diagonal; spherical shells show curved and kinked thresholds. We study the Busse--Simitev rotating annulus, whose normal modes obey an exact complex cubic dispersion relation. Combining the Bilharz criterion with the neutral-curve envelope yields, in the rapid-rotation limit, an explicit swallowtail wave front, in Arnold's sense, that bends the codensity diagonal into smooth and then kinked thresholds as the diffusivity ratio decreases. Its cusp edges lie inside the unstable domain and its exposed kink ends at the swallowtail singularity; these are singularities of wave-number selection, without coalescence of temporal eigenvalues, and the swallowtail diffusivity ratio has analytical small- and large-Prandtl-number limits. At a kink, broad, fast thermal and narrow, slow compositional Rossby waves become neutral together; hidden arcs mark the birth and merger of unstable wave-number bands. The same geometry enters nonlinear selection: mean-scalar feedback factors through neutral-arc normals and scalar responses, so the mean-profile coexistence margin vanishes with the kink angle as the waves merge. Complete cubic coefficients predict robust coexistence at a reference corner and a narrow bistable interval near merger. Exact finite-rotation corners and periodic-cell simulations confirm the coexisting waves and their invasion thresholds.

Steady states are found by Newton's method with an exact sparse Jacobian obtained by graph colouring, and their gradients follow from the implicit function theorem at the cost of one linear solve. In an unsteady roughness calibration, a singular value decomposition analysis of the sensitivity matrix showed that the stage records at the gauges are 70 to 160 times more sensitive to the total resistance of the reach than to its distribution among the roughness zones, which explains why only a gauge layout with gauges in every zone recovered all five roughness values. Quantitative global regularity for Navier--Stokes equations with odd viscosity on the cubic three-torus

Jinzheng Li, Yuqi Li, Hao-Yu Sun

We study the three-dimensional incompressible Navier--Stokes equation on the cubic torus with a constant odd-viscosity operator given by the vertical derivative of the curl. Arbitrary real, mean-zero, divergence-free initial data and an independent steady force, both in the second-order Sobolev space, admit a unique global strong solution when the magnitude of one fixed odd-viscosity coefficient is at least a norm-uniform sufficient threshold. In the unforced case the solution is smooth at positive times. The qualitative argument adapts the resonant-averaging framework for rapidly rotating fluids to the different odd-viscous dispersion. Weighted lattice counts and signed corrected-energy estimates yield a sufficient bound for the odd-to-ordinary viscosity ratio that is polynomial in the normalized initial-data radius at each fixed normalized force radius, together with a separate bound in a specified force-dominated regime. No optimality is claimed for the polynomial exponents or prefactors; determining the sharp viscosity threshold for the original equation remains open. Direct kinetic and weighted enstrophy barriers also give regularity for every coefficient on explicit bounded data and force regions, including the closed unforced ball of homogeneous second-order Fourier Sobolev radius ninety-seven fortieths times the viscosity and seven closed forced rectangles. We specify how finite certificates enter the analytic argument and distinguish minimization of a sufficient formula from sharpness for the original equation. All three main theorems are formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

Tuning fluid frictional drag through flow-induced aggregation of wormlike micelles

Lucas Warwaruk, Amanda Tiano, Berith Isaac, Gareth H. McKinley

Charged amphiphilic surfactants are versatile polymorphic molecules which can form supramolecular structures, such as wormlike micelles. We study the rheology and non-Newtonian fluid dynamics of the cationic surfactant trimethyltetradecylammonium chloride (TTAC) combined with the aromatic counter-ion sodium salicylate (NaSal). In the non-dilute regime, this solution exhibits a linear viscoelastic response consistent with a single-mode Jeffreys model, and in capillary pinch-off experiments, fluid filaments form extended cylindrical threads, indicating strong strain-stiffening. In the dilute regime the linear viscoelasticity and extensional rheology become immeasurably small, but the TTAC/NaSal mixture develops flow-induced structures at high shear rates, as the result of an end-to-end concatenation of micelles produced by shear-induced flow alignment. These flow-induced structures augment both the measured viscosity and the viscous drag in laminar flows at high shear rates (by as much as 50 %). In turbulent Taylor-Couette flow, the flow-induced structures produce a sudden and dramatic destabilizing influence on the stable toroidal Taylor vortices, resulting in upwards of a 200 % enhancement in turbulent drag. However, with increasing Reynolds number, the turbulent flow transitions smoothly to a state of drag reduction, generating up to a 30 % reduction in fluid frictional drag but without the shear-induced degradation that plagues drag reduction strategies based on solutions of high molecular weight polymer, such as polyacrylamide. These results show that surfactants are a unique and effective modifier of fluid frictional drag in both laminar and turbulent flow regimes, capable of producing both significant levels of drag enhancement and drag reduction by tuning the composition and operating conditions.

The DNS set-up is verified against reference cylinder data at Re = 3900, and DNS and PIV at Re = 10,000 are compared field by field. The dataset supports the development and testing of data-driven turbulence closures, reduced-order models, and wake-stability analyses across geometries with distinct separation behavior.
