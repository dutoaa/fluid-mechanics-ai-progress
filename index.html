Assisted Turbulence Modeling via Data Assimilation and Machine Learning for Turbomachinery Flows

Tom Moussie, Pierric Joseph, Marcello Meldi

This work presents the development and assessment of a hybrid framework coupling numerical simulation, experimental samples, data assimilation (DA) and machine learning (ML) to investigate the flow within an axial compressor stage. The methodology first implements a Multi-Fidelity Ensemble Kalman Filter (MFEnKF) which integrates experimental PIV data with a two-fidelity numerical simulation setup, combining steady-state RANS on a fixed grid with unsteady hybrid RANS/LES simulations on a moving grid. This data assimilation step successfully reduces discrepancies between experiments and CFD realizations. Besides, it provides additional high fidelity samples when compared with the available PIV data. This enriched database is used to train machine learning (ML) tools, with the objective to obtain black-box forcing terms to be included in the discretized form of the Navier-Stokes equations. These models are expected to preserve the accuracy of the DA results while not longer requiring the input of experimental data. The neural network architecture used here maps local physical features such as the velocity, the velocity gradients and the turbulent viscosity, to obtain the volume forcing terms. The analysis of the performance of such ML models shows that global accuracy is preserved but localized increased errors in specific regions of the compressor, such as close to the blade tips, can exceed those observed from the baseline RANS simulations. To resolve this issue, a Mixture of Experts (MoE) method is employed, which classifies and combines the trained ML tools for different flow regions. The discrepancy with experimental data of this method is systematically lower than the one observed for the baseline RANS solution and very similar to the one obtained with the MFEnKF.

Synthetic Dataset Generation, Physical Consistency and Active Learning for Shape-Informed Surrogate Modeling of Hemodynamics

Francesco Romor, Richard Schussnig, Charles A. Taylor, Gerhard A. Holzapfel, Alfonso Caiazzo

Model order reduction has the potential to enable real-time patient-specific assessment of cardiovascular flows. However, it still faces significant challenges stemming from the geometric variability of vascular anatomies. Building upon the SynthAorta dataset (Bosnjak et al., IEEE Trans. Med. Imag. 45(1), 2026) of physiological aortic geometries, we generate an extensive dataset of physiologically consistent computational fluid dynamics solutions through an automatic boundary-condition adjustment procedure. A common reference geometry allows all patient-specific anatomies to be represented by displacement fields, providing point-to-point correspondence without the need for geometric registration. Based on this dataset, we develop Graph U-Net and Transformer U-Net surrogate models to predict velocity and pressure fields at peak systole. We further investigate two strategies to enhance data efficiency and prediction accuracy: active learning and differentiable physical consistency constraints based on the finite element residual. The results show that active learning is strongly regime-dependent: while it offers little benefit over random sampling during early training, it improves performance during fine-tuning through gradient- and subspace-based acquisition strategies. Similarly, physical consistency constraints prove to be substantially more effective as a fine-tuning objective than as a regularizer applied throughout training. Finally, out-of-distribution studies demonstrate that the primary limitation of surrogate models lies in the geometric support of the underlying parametrization rather than in the neural network architectures themselves. The proposed framework combines synthetic dataset generation, geometry-aware surrogate modeling, physics-informed optimization, and active learning into a scalable workflow for inter-patient hemodynamic prediction.

Neural Operator Splitting for Fluid Dynamics in Complex Geometry

Jianxiang Li, Xi Deng, Hong Chul Nam, Anima Anandkumar

Solving dynamical systems in complex and diverse geometries remains challenging for both numerical solvers and learning-based surrogate models. We propose Neural Operator Splitting, a framework for learning fluid dynamics while preserving the physical structure of fractional-step numerical schemes. Instead of learning the full-step evolution monolithically, we decompose each time step into physically interpretable steps, and learn each operator separately using intermediate states from the underlying numerical solver. Guided by the linearity properties of the fractional steps, we introduce a conditional-linear Fourier neural operator (CL-FNO) whose linear state mappings are modulated by nonlinear conditioning on inputs such as boundary geometry. We further introduce two complementary strategies, symmetry sampling and corrective cycling, to mitigate error accumulation during autoregressive rollout. Experiments on 2D and 3D fluid flows with varying geometries and initial conditions show that our full model reduces mean relative $L^2$ rollout error by 68\% in 2D and 40\% in 3D compared with a single full-step neural operator. At comparable error levels, neural-operator inference is approximately $10^3$ times faster per step than the Monte Carlo solver.

DeHNSSo-v2: A compressible, curvilinear, and efficient extension of the Delft Harmonic Navier-Stokes Solver

Pietro Carlo Boldini, Yifu Chen, Marios Kotsonis

We present DeHNSSo-v2, a major update of DeHNSSo-v1 (Westerbeek et al., Comput. Phys. Commun. 302, 2024), the open-source Delft Harmonic Navier-Stokes Solver for nonlinear stability analysis of shear flows. The new release adds a compressible formulation on general two-dimensional body-fitted geometries, automatic grid generation, linear-cost sparse assembly, reusable per-mode factorizations, and Newton pseudo-arclength continuation for high-amplitude nonlinear solutions. The curvilinear formulation preserves the fourth-order accuracy of the discretization on strongly distorted grids, and the compressible solver is confirmed to reproduce DeHNSSo-v1 in the incompressible limit. Validation spans published benchmarks for linear instability of compressible flat-plate boundary layers from $\mathrm{Ma}=0.5$ to $\mathrm{Ma}=4.5$, nonlinear oblique-mode breakdown at $\mathrm{Ma}=1.6$, nonlinear swept-wing crossflow compared with direct numerical simulations, and linear Tollmien-Schlichting wave interaction with a modeled surface-embedded Helmholtz resonator compared with experimentally validated linearized Navier-Stokes simulations. On the nonlinear test cases of DeHNSSo-v1, the computational developments result in a speed-up of approximately one order of magnitude. By broadening the scope and reducing the cost of DeHNSSo-v1, DeHNSSo-v2 provides the boundary-layer-transition and flow-control community with an accessible tool for studying receptivity, nonlinear disturbance evolution, and instability interactions with complex surface geometry and metamaterials across compressibility regimes.

Predicting trigger in streak breakdown via Picard-Chebyshev approach in plane-Poiseuille flow

Nobutaka Taniguchi, Aiko Yakeno

The evolution of finite-amplitude disturbances is critical in the prediction of laminar-to-turbulent transition. To analyze the transient growth of disturbances, Pralits et al. (J. Fluid Mechanics, 2015) proposed a weakly nonlinear optimal perturbation (WNLOP) analysis that accounts for the effect of mean-flow distortion on the fundamental wave. The present study formulates a WNLOP analysis via Picard--Chebyshev iteration for compressible parallel flows to predict streak breakdown. The formulation incorporates the non-modal amplification of disturbances and their nonlinear interactions for polychromatic waves, considering all the possible nonlinear interactions starting from a finite set of initial wavenumber components. The nonlinear interaction is tracked as a monotonically increasing set of wavenumber components; specifically, the first-order approximation recovers the linear disturbance equation, the third-order approximation corresponds to the amplitude equation of weakly nonlinear theory, and further iterations systematically approach DNS results in the weakly nonlinear regime. By applying the proposed formulation to a subcritical plane Poiseuille flow at Mach number $\mathrm{M}=0.3$ and Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}=3000$, the proposed formulation demonstrates high predictive accuracy in comparison with DNS calculations and succeeds in identifying the optimal perturbation restricted to a planar wave that causes streak breakdown in full nonlinear simulation. The proposed method predicts the nonlinear interaction between the streak and the planar wave, which generates an oblique wave and subsequent harmonic waves characterized by an alternating streamwise vortex structure, whose amplitude determines whether further nonlinear interactions are induced to trigger the turbulent transition.

CuAD: Source-to-Source Automatic Differentiation of CUDA Kernels for Discrete Adjoints in Computational Fluid Dynamics

Seunghyun Joo

Discrete adjoint methods give the sensitivities that aerodynamic design and data assimilation require, but they remain hard to obtain for flow solvers that run on graphics processors. This paper presents CuAD, a source-to-source automatic differentiation tool that reads CUDA kernels and writes their tangent and adjoint versions as ordinary CUDA source. Its reverse mode is organised around the GPU thread. It adopts the convention of CUDA kernels that inputs and outputs do not alias, recomputes intermediate values instead of storing them, differentiates pure accumulations without a tape and keeps any remaining tape in a fixed thread-local array. We evaluate CuAD on four kernels from simple flow models and on a two-dimensional Euler solver. All generated derivatives pass dot-product tests to round-off, and the adjoint kernels cost two to six times the primal kernels. On a Biot-Savart summation compiled by one compiler, the CuAD adjoint costs about four times the primal at every size, as does Enzyme when the source declares the absence of aliasing with __restrict__. On the unchanged source Enzyme caches the loop in the device heap and costs 9 to 240 times the primal, while the heap tape of Clad costs 60 to 4400 times the primal and fails beyond 8192 vortices. Exact Jacobians assembled from the generated tangents also matter for accuracy. In single precision, the adjoint gradient of a finite-difference Jacobian reaches errors of 45 to 75 per cent on a stiff Burgers problem and of $5\times10^{-4}$ to $4\times10^{-2}$ on a transonic airfoil, whereas the generated Jacobian keeps them below $5\times10^{-5}$ and $3\times10^{-6}$. The applications include the shape sensitivities of lift and drag of a transonic NACA 0012, for which the residual, the boundary conditions and the wall force are differentiated by CuAD and the adjoint agrees with the tangent to $10^{-13}$.

Spatially developing drag reduction across a finite oscillating wall

Marco Galli, Nicolò Scaiola, Ferdinand F. J. Schrijer, Fulvio Scarano

The spatial evolution of a turbulent boundary layer at $Re_τ= 652$ developing across a finite spanwise oscillating wall is investigated with normalised oscillation period of $T^+ \approx 101$ and amplitude $A^+ \approx 7$, yielding a drag reduction of 32\%. The spatial distribution of skin friction is estimated adopting a recently introduced near-wall data nonlinear regression method based on the arctangent function, which circumvents the requirement of high wall-normal resolution to capture the viscous sublayer. The method viability is assessed against reference DNS data, and the trends of measurement errors from experimental data are well reproduced. The local impact of the control action is examined by evaluating the spatial development of scalar fields of turbulence statistics as well as conditional quadrant analysis to link the onset and recovery transients to turbulence events. The boundary layer response is observed to follow an exponential decay law for the skin friction spatial evolution. The decay length-scale corresponds to $0.6$ boundary layer thicknesses $δ$ for the onset transient and $0.9δ$ for the recovery one. The same exponential fit applied to the Reynolds stresses yields a response within $0.5δ$, with the exception of the shear stress peak, that requires a significantly longer distance.

Reynolds-number-dependent entrance bias in temperature polarization of lab-scale membrane distillation

Soyoung Kim, Tiezheng Tong, Yinuo Noah Yao

Lab-scale membrane distillation (MD) modules are commonly used for membrane characterization and developing empirical heat-transfer correlations. Entrance-induced flow structures can introduce geometry-specific effects into measured temperature polarization (TP) and heat transfer. However, the dependence of these effects on module geometry and flow condition remains unclear. We performed three-dimensional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations of direct contact membrane distillation (DCMD) over inlet Reynolds numbers of $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}=10$-$1000$, focusing on side-chamber length and main-channel height. We discovered that counter-rotating streamwise vortices formed near the main-channel entrance across all geometries, but their downstream persistence increased with $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$. At lower $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$, the vortices mostly decayed before mid-channel, while at higher $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$ they persisted farther downstream and produced pronounced spatial variations in TP. Increasing side-chamber length reduced the secondary-flow kinetic energy entering the main channel, and generally strengthened TP, whereas increasing main-channel height reduced the bulk velocity and streamwise kinetic energy, resulting in a thicker thermal boundary layer and stronger TP. For most geometries, the deviation from the straight-channel baseline changed from stronger TP at lower $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$ to weaker TP at higher $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$. Entrance effects therefore vary in both magnitude and direction with operating condition and cannot be represented by a constant correction across the Reynolds-number range.

Exponential Growth of Turbulent Cross Helicity in Shear-Flow-Driven Turbulence

B. Tripathi, A. E. Fraser, P. W. Terry, E. G. Zweibel, M. J. Pueschel, A. M. Beloborodov

Cross helicity, the velocity--magnetic-field correlation, is an ideal invariant of incompressible magnetohydrodynamics and constrains energy cascades and magnetic-field generation. Although cross helicity typically decays via visco-resistive effects, we analytically demonstrate that it grows exponentially in sheared inhomogeneous turbulence. This cross-helical shear instability is confirmed using three-dimensional numerical simulations. Growth requires a nonzero gradient of the Reynolds--Maxwell stress to produce turbulent cross helicity; this production overcomes effective losses when shear exceeds a threshold set by the ratio of turbulent magnetic to momentum diffusivities.

Fourier-Feature-Embedded Complex-Valued Physics-Informed Convolutional Residual Networks for Sparse and Gappy Noisy Flow-Field Reconstruction

Biswanath Barman, Rajendra K. Ray

Reconstructing flow fields from sparse and noisy measurements becomes particularly challenging when data are completely absent from dynamically critical regions, such as the vortex-formation region of a wake. Conventional interpolation and numerical reconstruction methods generally require sufficient spatial or boundary information, while physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) can also face difficulties when sparse and noisy observations provide little or no supervision in the region of interest. To address this gap, we propose a Fourier-feature-embedded complex-valued physics-informed convolutional residual network (FCV-PICRN) for sparse, gappy, and noisy flow-field reconstruction. The framework integrates Fourier features for oscillatory and multiscale structures, complex-valued representations for coupled amplitude--phase learning, and convolutional residual blocks constrained by the Navier--Stokes equations. The method is first verified using the Taylor--Green vortex at $Re=100$ and then evaluated for the cylinder wake at $Re=100$. Three severe missing-data configurations are imposed in the dynamically sensitive wake: a large hole, a large truncation, and a plus-shaped gap extending in both streamwise and transverse directions. These gaps are further combined with additive Gaussian and multiplicative speckle noise. Despite having no supervised velocity data inside the excluded regions, FCV-PICRN reconstructs velocity, pressure, and vorticity with high structural similarity index (SSIM) and peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), and remains robust under Gaussian noise up to $20\%$. Finally, for forced two-dimensional Kolmogorov turbulence with $5\%$ Gaussian noise, FCV-PICRN yields lower reconstruction errors than a conventional PINN. These results demonstrate robust physics-informed reconstruction of severely incomplete and noisy flows across laminar and turbulent regimes.

A graph neural network (GNN)-based autoencoder is leveraged to produce reduced latent graph representations of detonation-containing flows. In a two-dimensional hydrogen-air channel configuration with a uniform fuel-air mixture, it was found that latent graphs with effective resolutions of twice the induction length are capable of reconstructing both the wavefront and the post-shock region of the cellular detonation wave structure. Extrapolation studies using stratified and three-dimensional detonation configurations revealed an intrinsic property of GNN-based reconstruction from reduced latent graphs -- a representational hierarchy, rooted in the local nature of message passing, that tends to prioritize features at the detonation front (i.e., the wavefront profile and triple points) over the downstream deflagrative regions. In particular, it was found that at a fixed ambient condition, the information contained in uniform two-dimensional detonations was sufficient to reconstruct the wavefront structure of both stratified and three-dimensional detonations. Overall, this work demonstrates the utility of GNNs for both reduction and information overlap extraction in detonation-containing flows, opening promising pathways for accelerated simulations of these flows.

Fully resolved particle dynamics over long time scales in acoustofluidic flows: Theory and numerical simulations

Khemraj Gautam Kshetri, Amneet Pal Singh Bhalla, Nitesh Nama

We present a perturbation-based theoretical framework, together with its immersed boundary/volume penalization implementation, for long-time simulations of fully resolved rigid-particle dynamics in acoustically actuated flows. A perturbation formulation separates the fast acoustic time scale from the slow mean dynamics, avoiding long-time direct integration of the fully compressible Navier-Stokes equations. The immersed rigid body is represented through a Brinkman penalization term in equations posed over a unified fluid-solid domain. The coupled fluid-particle system is decomposed into a time-harmonic first-order problem governing the acoustic response and a time-averaged second-order problem. A central challenge in this decomposition is the treatment of the moving fluid-solid interface. We derive order-consistent expressions for the structural velocities, comprising rigid-body and Stokes-drift contributions, and formulate the corresponding first- and second-order rigid-body equations of motion. For a circular particle in two dimensions, the simulations reveal substantial differences in the magnitude and structure of acoustic streaming around fixed and freely moving particles, highlighting the importance of two-way fluid-structure coupling. The results also show that particle-density effects are more complex than suggested by simplified descriptions based solely on the acoustic contrast factor. The framework captures the mean particle dynamics using time steps associated with the slow particle-motion scale rather than the much smaller acoustic time scale. Finally, simulations of an elliptical particle demonstrate the ability of the method to capture coupled translational and rotational motion. The proposed approach therefore provides an efficient route to fully resolved, long-time simulations of acoustically driven rigid-particle motion while retaining two-way fluid-structure coupling.

Load-Balanced Neural-Network-Aided Adaptive Tabulation of Phase Equilibria for Transcritical Multiphase Flows

Navneeth Srinivasan, Po-Yu Lai, Suo Yang

Simulating high-pressure transcritical flows with phase change is critical for advanced propulsion, yet traditional first-principles approaches, vapor-liquid equilibrium (VLE) calculations, consume most of the runtime and frequently fail to converge. Standalone machine learning models introduce heavy evaluation overhead across large grids, while dynamic tabulation still relies on fragile iterative solvers. To resolve these limitations, this study introduces a hybrid In-Situ Adaptive Tabulation and Artificial Neural Network (ISAT-ANN) framework. A pre-trained ANN replaces the iterative solver during table expansion to guarantee crash-free operation, while ISAT enables rapid data retrieval via local linear interpolation. To eliminate parallel load imbalances, an O(N) greedy load-balancing algorithm redistributes computational tasks across cores. Coupled with Adaptive Mesh Refinement (AMR), the framework was validated on a Mach 5 shock-droplet interaction benchmark. It accurately captured shock-interface dynamics without instability, achieving a 2.30 times speedup in thermodynamic calculations on a single core and 2.62 times in parallel over standalone ANNs, with AMR providing an additional 2.92 times overall efficiency boost. This plug-and-play framework offers a stable, scalable path for high-fidelity multiphase flow simulations.

Dakshaka Vishwajith Amarathunga, Lanre M. Oshinowo, Ji-Woong Park, Tarek Echekki

We present a novel framework for reconstructing fine-resolution scalar fields from coarse-resolution data in 2D unsteady flames using an extension of neural implicit flow (NIF). The framework leverages the novel architecture of NIF to develop an efficient representation of scalar fields in spatio-temporal reacting flows. Different strategies are adopted within the proposed framework to establish super-resolution for multiscalar fields in combustion. They include 1) a reconfiguration of the input and output fields, 2) the use of a latent composition space based on nonlinear principal component analysis (NLPCA) to reconstruct the fine-resolution fields, and the conversion from nonlinear principal components (NLPCs) to species and temperature contours, and 3) the adoption of multiple ParameterNets in the NIF architecture, each accommodating one of the NLPCs. The framework was validated on a 2D unsteady Bunsen-stabilized lean premixed methane-air flame with forcing at the inlet. The results, using a coarsening ratio of 8 in a given direction, show that the adoption of multiple ParameterNets yields more accurate super-resolution than a single ParameterNet without any additional cost in the hyperparameters.

Dramatic pressure-induced slowdown and the soft glassy rheology of water at 2D material interface

Guilhem Mariette, Kenji Watanabe, Takashi Taniguchi, Jean Comtet

The flow of molecularly confined water has attracted considerable interest in recent years. Yet despite their exquisite character, current nanofluidic devices cannot probe molecular water flow beyond the linear-response regime, while nanomechanical force- based approaches have produced markedly inconsistent results. Here, using tuning- fork-based atomic force microscopy, we tackle these limitations by measuring the full linear-to-nonlinear, shear-dependent viscoelastic rheology of molecularly confined water at various two-dimensional material interfaces -- demonstrating pronounced rate effects in water flow response. The observed rheological behaviors are remarkably analogous to that of macroscopic soft glassy yielding materials and fully captured by a non-linear model accounting for the shear-induced acceleration of the dynamics -- a hallmark of disordered supercooled and metastable systems. We further unveil a pronounced pressure-induced slowdown in nanoconfined water dynamics, with relaxation times extending into the microsecond range and marked material dependence. These results reveal non-linear, glassy rheology as a general feature of interfacial water and offer a route to tunable, non-linear nanofluidic elements.

How stagnation lines move, persist and break up in fluid flows

Prabakaran Rajamanickam

Stagnation curves of arbitrary shape (zeros of $\mathbf v(\mathbf x,t)$) are non-generic yet common; we derive their motion. Differentiating $\mathbf v=\mathbf0$ gives $\mathbf L\mathbf U=-\mathbf a$ ($\mathbf U$ curve velocity, $\mathbf a=\mathbf v_t$, $\mathbf L=\nabla\mathbf v$ kills tangent, acts on normal plane via $2\times2$ block $\mathbf K$); dynamics enters only via $\mathbf a$. Normal velocity: $-\mathbf K^{-1}$ times normal $\mathbf a$; tangential solvability: scalar condition $Σ=0$ per point ($Σ$: part of $\mathbf a$ outside range $\mathbf L$). Potential flow: $\mathbf K$ is strain rate, normal velocity pressure gradient over principal strain rate $ε$, persistence needs uniform pressure on it. Stokes flow: tangential vorticity enters $\mathbf K$ (centre lines), $Σ$ involves normal vorticity. Navier-Stokes: $Σ$ set by instantaneous flow via nonlocal pressure, viscosity. If $Σ\ne0$, line becomes, to first order in $t$, isolated points at zeros of $Σ$ (critical points of $\varphi_t$ if potential; maybe none in Stokes flow: loops vanish). Symmetry (line an orbit of rigid-motion group, no velocity along it) suffices for persistence, not necessary: flows steady in rigidly moving frame have $Σ\equiv0$. Incompressibility enters via $\mathrm{tr}\mathbf K=0$; for inviscid irrotational homentropic compressible flow, line dilatation is $\varphi_{tt}/c^2$, so lines can be nodes, persistence needs uniform enthalpy, singular $\mathbf K$ arises at ordinary points, acoustic standing waves give exact examples (node line breaking into alternating sinks, saddles); also degenerate $\mathbf K$ (pairs born at distance $O(\sqrt t)$), curvature, torsion evolution, closure for moving-singularity-driven flows, helical and elliptically conical stagnation-point flow, exact Navier-Stokes example: saddle, centre lines split into four points per period.

Current industrial practice has largely relied on empirical fatigue models to predict damage progression and guide maintenance. From these findings, we propose a $\textit{universal scaling law}$ that relates all key impact loadings to the simple parameter of the droplet $\textit{projection area}$, which can be determined solely from the initial droplet geometry and orientation. This law provides a predictive framework for impact loading dynamics of non-ideal shapes, offering new insights into the mechanisms of LEE and improving the accuracy of erosion predictions for wind turbine blades.

Filipe Ramos do Amaral, Mateus Peixoto Avanci, Jean-Christophe Robinet, Peter Jordan, Jérôme Huber, Grégoire Pont

We study coherent structures extracted from time-resolved particle image velocimetry (TR-PIV) measurements of isothermal elliptical jets with aspect ratio of 1:2, at Mach numbers 0.4 and 0.7. The velocity snapshots are decomposed according to the D$_2$ symmetry. Spectral proper orthogonal decomposition (SPOD) is then used to identify coherent structures. Modelling is performed using three-dimensional parabolised stability equations (3D-PSE) based on linearisation about the time-averaged mean flows. A comparison between SPOD and 3D-PSE results shows alignment of SPOD and single-mode PSE structures of up to 70\%. A linear combination of PSE solutions, obtained from the family of initially unstable local modes, leads to SPOD-PSE alignment of up to 90\%. Limitations are observed at low frequencies and near the nozzle and are attributed to the existence of non-modal amplification mechanisms. The study shows that coherent structures in turbulent elliptical jets can be educed using the D$_2$-SPOD approach, and modelled using 3D PSE.

Their relative importance, however, remains less well established when aerodynamic and hydrodynamic low-frequency excitations coexist under operating conditions. This study develops an efficient time-domain formulation for full difference-frequency quadratic transfer function (QTF) loading using bandwidth truncation and integrates it into a fully coupled model of the IEA 15 MW turbine on the VolturnUS-S semi-submersible platform. Numerical predictions are compared with 1:70-scale real-time hybrid wave-basin experiments covering both irregular wave-only and combined turbulent wind-wave conditions. Under wave-only conditions, second-order difference-frequency loading substantially improves the prediction of surge and the associated mooring response, particularly in energetic sea states, with the full-QTF model generally providing the closest agreement with measured low-frequency spectra and peak-amplitude distributions. Under combined wind-wave conditions, low-frequency thrust fluctuations dominate near rated wind speed, reducing the relative importance of difference-frequency wave excitation, whereas its contribution becomes more apparent at higher wind speeds as thrust fluctuations diminish. Overall, the full-QTF model provides the most consistent improvement over the linear and Newman models, with maximum surge standard deviation discrepancy from measurement under the most energetic wave conditions less than 18%, with smaller discrepancies for heave, pitch, and mooring tensions.

NeRF-Inspired Neural Fields for 3D Super-resolution of Reacting-Flows

Dakshaka Vishwajith Amarathunga, Shang-Ru Li, Tarek Echekki

The multiscale nature of turbulent reacting flows and the importance of fine-scale structures pose challenges for super-resolution. We investigate a 3D super-resolution framework inspired by Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), called Neural Field Super-Resolution (NFSR), to reconstruct fine-resolution fields from coarsened reacting-flow fields. The proposed formulation adapts the continuous coordinate-conditioned representation of NeRF to physical flow-field reconstruction by conditioning spatial coordinates with local coarse-grid thermochemical information, while eliminating radiance prediction and volume rendering. Principal component analysis is used to provide a compact and low-dimensional representation of the thermochemical state, and a hierarchical extension, H-NFSR, progressively reconstructs the solution through intermediate resolution levels. The framework is evaluated using three-dimensional direct numerical simulation data for a turbulent premixed hydrogen-air flame, with an overall spatial coarsening factor of eight in each coordinate direction. The results demonstrate consistently better prediction measures and a higher ability to reconstruct fine-scale information than tricubic interpolation on the unseen test realization. The hierarchical formulation reduces visible coarse-cell imprinting. In contrast, biased sampling mainly benefits the most localized structures. Three-dimensional isosurface comparisons further show that H-NFSR recovers much of the small and intermediate features of the flow, including surface wrinkling and local flame curvature effects. These results highlight the potential of coordinate-conditioned neural fields as a flexible framework for three-dimensional coarse-to-fine reconstruction of reacting-flow simulations.
