Spatial symmetry invariance of three-dimensional Navier-Stokes turbulence

Shijun Liao

Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS) of the deterministic Navier-Stokes (NS) equations is widely used to provide benchmark solutions of turbulent flow, but its accuracy and reliability have not been rigorously inspected from a mathematical viewpoint. Here we prove a spatial symmetry invariance theorem (SSIT) of three-dimensional NS equations whereby an exact solution of deterministic NS equations under periodic boundary conditions maintains the same spatial symmetry as its symmetric initial flow, forever. SSIT provides an absolute benchmark test for computational fluid dynamics (CFD). It is shown herein that traditional DNS of NS turbulence quickly violates SSIT, revealing that the spatiotemporal trajectory of NS turbulence predicted by DNS soon becomes badly polluted by numerical noise. Importantly, it is found that even the statistical results of DNS might be unstable, i.e., exhibit sensitivity dependence on the initial flow conditions. In other words, the loss of spatial symmetry in traditional DNS of NS turbulence is not a physical instability but instead a numerical artifact caused by the chaotic nature of the flow. Using the SSIT, it is straightforward to deduce several key properties of NS turbulence, including `ultra-chaos', the so-called `noise-expansion cascade', `permanent memory of symmetric initial flow conditions', and so on. Note that permanent memory of symmetric initial flow conditions suggests that NS turbulence governed by deterministic NS equations subject to symmetric initial flow should have no `mixing', corresponding to non-ergodicity, although ergodicity is one of the most fundamental assumptions of turbulence theory.

Generative AI in Turbulence: Foundations, Applications, Validations and Benchmarks

Luca Biferale, Michele Buzzicotti

Generative AI offers new approaches to turbulence problems in which incomplete observations or unresolved d.o.f. admit multiple physically plausible solutions. Potential applications include stochastic closures, super-resolution, data assimilation, uncertainty quantification, and the generation of Eulerian and Lagrangian flow ensembles. However, turbulence is not a conventional image-generation problem. Its multiscale dynamics, intermittency, extreme events, rough velocity fields and intrinsically stochastic transport make visual realism, pointwise error, and low-order spectral agreement insufficient measures of physical fidelity. We examine where generative AI provide novel capabilities with emphasis on diffusion models, score-based methods, and related probability-transport formulations. We discuss their use in unconditional generation, probabilistic reconstruction, reduced-order modeling, temporal evolution, and mesh-free representations. We argue that validation must address turbulence properties: energy transfer, structure functions, anomalous scaling, pdf tails, coherent structures, conservation laws, temporal consistency, ensemble diversity and generalization. Finally, we discuss potential limitations in validation, baseline, data availability and reproducibility, and the need for a benchmark ecosystem combining open data, standardized challenges, hidden tests, physics-aware metrics, and documented failures. This paper serves as a bridge between two communities. For AI practitioners, we provide a fair and accessible introduction to turbulence, essential for appreciating the challenges posed by the field and for assessing the physical relevance of generative models. For turbulent practitioners, we provide an introduction to the key technological aspects of Generative AI, clarifying what these tools actually do, and where their potential and current limitations lie.

Free-vortex-wake dynamics of airborne wind energy kites in crosswind flight

Yinong Tian, Shan Wang

The wake of an airborne wind energy kite is shaped by its crosswind path and, for on-board generation, by the slipstreams of its rotors, and in farms it reaches downstream units. It is computed here by a vortex step method with a time-marching free vortex wake, implemented by the authors in their open-source code OpenFAWE with a lumped-mass tether, actuator-disc rotors releasing vortex rings and several kites. It reproduces an independent vortex step code to 10^-9 on the rigid-wing Makani M600 and within 0.3% in lift and 0.9% in drag on the TU Delft V3 leading-edge inflatable kite, matching its Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes lift within 3.1%. For fixed kites the lift deficit of a finite wake is the far field of its starting vortex, which fixes the wake length before a run, and the vortex core must stay below the narrowest panel width. With these settings the helical tip-vortex pair of the M600 on its loop lowers its lift by about 0.5% from 5 to 13 m/s. Free rotor rings do not converge with the time step, whereas an undeformed slipstream tube converges once its rings are refined at the wing and raises the lift by 1.9%. A slipstream deflected by the wing lowers it by 3.5%, so the treatment near the wing sets the sign of the rotor effect. A second unit one loop diameter downstream loses 0.5% of its lift and 2.2% of its aerodynamic power, with or without rotors.

From Stochastic Transport Models to High-Dimensional Bayesian Inference

Dan Crisan, Oana Lang

The Stochastic Transport in Upper Ocean Dynamics project introduced a new paradigm for representing uncertainty in geophysical fluid dynamics through stochastic transport models calibrated from data. This paper provides a synthesis of the principal theoretical and data assimilation developments achieved as part of the project. The central theme of the paper is the transformation of stochastic transport models from a modelling paradigm into a mathematically rigorous framework equipped with analytical, variational, calibration and Bayesian data assimilation methodologies suitable for geophysical fluid dynamics. We describe the development of the theory of stochastic transport equations, including well-posedness, regularity and approximation results, together with their geometric foundations based on stochastic variational principles, rough paths and fractional transport models. We further review methodologies for calibrating stochastic transport structures from observational and high-resolution simulation data, ranging from reduced-order stochastic models to modern generative approaches. Finally, we discuss Bayesian inference and high-dimensional data assimilation techniques developed for stochastic transport models, including particle filter methodologies and machine learning uncertainty quantification. Taken together, these developments establish a coherent framework linking stochastic transport modelling, rigorous analysis, calibration and Bayesian inference, thereby providing a foundation for uncertainty quantification and forecasting in geophysical fluid dynamics.

Submerged Immiscible Oil Jet: Breakup Dynamics and Droplet Size Modelling

Lokendra Mohan Sharma, Aditya K. Aiyer, Harish N. Dixit, Lakshmana Dora Chandrala

Immiscible liquid-liquid jets occur in numerous industrial and environmental settings, including oil spill response and liquid-liquid extraction, yet the coupled evolution of turbulence, interfacial instability, and droplet size distribution across transitional to turbulent regimes remains poorly understood. We present a combined experimental and theoretical investigation of a silicone oil jet injected into refractive-index-matched sugar water, employing simultaneous Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV) and Planar Laser-Induced Fluorescence (PLIF). Beyond the breakup region, mean velocity and Reynolds stress profiles exhibit self-similarity. Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) of the volume fraction reveals a systematic shift in the dominant instability mechanism with increasing Reynolds number: axisymmetric Rayleigh-Plateau modes govern breakup at low Reynolds numbers, sinuous and varicose modes coexist in the transitional regime, and Kelvin-Helmholtz-driven shear-layer breakup dominates at higher Reynolds numbers. Droplet sizes are extracted from PLIF images using a convolutional neural network-based segmentation approach. In the transitional regime, a bimodal droplet size distribution is observed, reflecting the coexistence of shear-induced breakup and Rayleigh-Plateau instability. As the Reynolds number increases and turbulent dissipation intensifies, the distribution evolves toward a single mode with smaller characteristic droplet sizes. To predict the droplet size distribution, existing population balance models are extended to include droplet coalescence. Incorporating coalescence substantially improves agreement with experimental measurements, particularly for larger droplets. These findings demonstrate that turbulent dissipation, interfacial instabilities, and droplet coalescence collectively govern the evolution of droplet size distribution in immiscible liquid-liquid jets.

A Systematic Assessment of Artificial Flame Thickening for Thermodiffusively Unstable Lean Hydrogen Flames in Homogeneous Isotropic Turbulence

Max Schneider, Vinzenz Schuh, Felix Rong, Driss Kaddar, Andrew J. Aspden, Thomas L. Howarth

The Artificially Thickened Flame (ATF) approach is widely used to render premixed flames resolvable on coarse computational meshes, but thickening alters their interaction with turbulence. While considerable research has focused on the effects of thickening on turbulence-induced flame wrinkling, its influence on the mean local flame propagation speed in turbulent, thermodiffusively unstable hydrogen flames remains poorly understood. More than 200 flame-resolved simulations of premixed flames in homogeneous isotropic turbulence with detailed chemistry and transport are used to systematically isolate and quantify the effects of artificial thickening. Thickening the flame by a factor $F$ increases its effective flame-scale Karlovitz number according to $Ka_{\mathrm{f}}^F = Ka_{\mathrm{f}}\sqrt{F}$, while decreasing its effective Damköhler number according to $Da_{\mathrm{f}}^F = Da_{\mathrm{f}}/F$. In the regime considered here, the former enhances the mean local flame speed, whereas the latter reduces flame wrinkling, resulting in opposing effects on the flame consumption speed; hence, changes in both local flame propagation and flame area must be accounted for. Simulations matched with respect to $Ka_{\mathrm{f}}^F$ and $Da_{\mathrm{f}}^F$ collapse across different thickening factors $F$ until the thickened flame approaches the integral or geometric length scales of the configuration. Based on these scaling relations, a self-consistent efficiency correction is proposed to recover the unthickened flame consumption speed. Since the efficiency factor feeds back on the effective Karlovitz and Damköhler numbers, it must be determined implicitly.

Development and assessment of a target-$ω$ interfacial turbulence-damping model for stratified gas--liquid flow

Di Wu, Junda Liu, Qiaopeng Wang, Liu Liu, Dongling Wu, Ping Zhou

Interfacial turbulence damping is required in Reynolds-averaged simulations of stratified gas--liquid flows when strong interfacial velocity gradients are present. In this work, we develop a target-$ω$ interfacial damping formulation for the low-Reynolds-number Wilcox-type $k$--$ω$ model in a one-field RANS/VOF framework. A gas-side target value of the specific dissipation rate $ω$ is constructed from an effective interfacial wall coordinate determined from the local resolved flow. The constructed target-$ω$ provides a smooth transition between the viscous and logarithmic limits through power-law blending and requires no calibration of model constants against target data. \textcolor{black}{The formulation is first assessed against three gas-layer DNS cases at $Re_τ\simeq180$ with different interface velocities. The model reproduces the principal features of the mean velocity, turbulent kinetic energy and Reynolds shear-stress distributions, while showing substantially weaker sensitivity to the near-interface grid resolution than the Egorov-type formulation, although some grid dependence remains on the coarsest mesh. The assessment is then extended to the co-current Fabre stratified-flow experiment at a higher Reynolds number and to the counter-current Stäbler experiment}. In both cases, the present formulation provides generally good predictions of the gas-side mean flow and turbulence statistics without case-specific adjustment. For the Stäbler case, in which the liquid level develops as part of the coupled two-phase solution, the predicted mean liquid depth agrees closely with the measured value. These results indicate that the target-$ω$ formulation provides a consistent interfacial turbulence-damping treatment for resolved large gas--liquid interfaces over the range of conditions considered.

Solving and Learning Navier-Stokes Dynamics with the Neural Basis Method

Yuhe Wang, Zhu Wang, Min Wang

Physics-informed machine learning has become a major direction in predictive modeling of fluid dynamics, combining governing physics with learned representations. Yet prevailing formulations neither guarantee that small PDE-residual losses imply small solution errors nor scale effectively with increasing problem size. Here we present the neural basis method (NBM) as a new approach for Navier--Stokes dynamics. NBM is built on a ``representation-then-projection'' principle: fixed neural bases define the representation space, while projection of the governing equations determines the expansion coefficients. The same bases define common coordinates for the parametric solution manifold, recasting operator learning as a parameter-to-coefficient mapping and enabling scalability by removing explicit spatial dependence. For incompressible flow, NBM yields a velocity-only formulation whose residual provides an a posteriori velocity-error estimate, establishing a certified, data-free training objective for coefficient learning. Theoretical analysis and numerical experiments demonstrate the accuracy, efficiency, generalization, and scalability of NBM, with substantial improvements over baselines.

Quantum hydrodynamics from Nambu's unpublished fluid notes

Kazuki Ikeda

We develop a quantum hydrodynamic theory based on Nambu's unpublished note \emph{A New Look at Fluid Dynamics}. The kinetic action defines a metric on configuration space and a mechanical connection coupling an observable cyclic deformation to unobserved fluid modes. Tracing out these modes yields a quantum channel for the observed deformation. We establish a criterion for the exact preservation of coherence between opposite momenta and show how nonzero connection curvature prevents this coupling from being removed. The leading quadratic decay of quantum coherence is determined by the prepared quantum state. For two shear flows, we identify an exact noiseless sign subsystem. Introducing additional generating flows cancels the initial residual of the Euler equations while producing nonzero connection curvature, governed by the same kinetic integrals that determine the classical correction. At fixed mean energy, choosing amplitudes adapted to the dominant coupling flow substantially reduces the initial decoherence, even along the direction that cancels the Euler residual. This advantage gradually diminishes under conditional evolution, eventually leading to a crossing of the decoherence curves. Our example shows that a preparation with a smaller initial coherence-decay coefficient can have a larger maximum sampled loss over a finite readout interval.

Classical logarithmic and Prandtl mixing-length scalings provide the starting points, while the regression identifies near-wall transition functions under asymptotic constraints and Reynolds-number-dependent outer corrections. These results illustrate how physical scaling knowledge can guide symbolic regression toward compact, interpretable analytical descriptions of canonical wall turbulence.

Local Metrics for Exact Streaming on Curvilinear Lattices: A Curvilinear D3Q27 Cumulant Lattice-Boltzmann Method

Maher Sawaf, Jonas Latt, Nikolaus A. Adams, Mostafa S. Shadloo

A three-dimensional curvilinear lattice-Boltzmann method is introduced for simulations on smoothly deformed grids while retaining exact one-to-one streaming on a uniform computational lattice. The coordinate transformation is represented locally through a metric-Gaussian equilibrium and a Christoffel-connection source in moment space. A dynamically reconstructed homogeneous-state perturbation streaming procedure preserves homogeneous physical states on warped meshes without interpolation in the advection step. The method is implemented on a D3Q27 lattice with a cumulant collision operator, while the geometric construction remains compatible with other local collision models. The formulation is assessed using two sets of tests. First, static non-orthogonal warped-cube cases validate the bulk curvilinear solver through a convected helical shear wave, the Taylor--Green vortex, and Arnold--Beltrami--Childress flow. These tests verify viscous decay, clean freestream preservation, vortex-breakdown dynamics, and the nonlinear inertial--pressure balance induced by geometric acceleration. Second, wall-bounded and moving-boundary demonstrations assess the boundary-compatible extension through warped plane Poiseuille flow, arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian (ALE) peristaltic pumping, and a two-way fluid--structure interaction (FSI) case with added-mass frequency shift. The results show that the proposed curvilinear LBM avoids interpolation-based transport, reduces geometry-locked numerical modes, preserves global mass and momentum to round-off accuracy in the tested warped periodic cases, and extends to wall-bounded ALE/FSI configurations with conservative treatment of mass and physically consistent momentum exchange.
