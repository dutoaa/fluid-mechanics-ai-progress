A 3D PINN-Based Framework for Patient-Specific Pulmonary Hemodynamics Using Windkessel Models

Celia Taboada, Luis Nuñez, Angélica Casero, Pedro Navas

Cardiovascular fluid dynamics modeling relies heavily on mesh-based numerical methods such as Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD). While accurate, these methods entail significant computational costs and complex meshing workflows that limit their translation to clinical practice. Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) are a promising meshless alternative that embeds the governing equations directly into neural network training. In this work, we propose a PINN framework for pulsatile blood flow in patient-specific geometries with dynamic lumped-parameter Windkessel (RCR) outlet conditions representing distal vascular impedance. Coupling the Windkessel ODE to a 3D network raises two difficulties: the ODE alone does not fix the pressure level, and the local pressure variations that drive the flow are orders of magnitude smaller than the basal pressure imposed by the outlet. We address them by decomposing pressure into a time-dependent basal part and a spatially varying part, an initial condition and gauge for the Windkessel equation, a global mass-balance constraint, and a periodic Fourier embedding of time. Validated on 2D canonical channels (steady and pulsatile, with and without RCR outlet), the framework is then applied to a patient-specific right pulmonary artery, reproducing outlet flow, mean pressure, and the velocity and dynamic pressure fields with errors on the order of the data resolution. Finally, we solve the inverse problem of identifying the three Windkessel parameters of the outlet from synthetic data, recovering them with errors of about 0.3% from deliberately wrong initial values. Residual biases in the local pressure field and the need to test robustness to noisy clinical data remain open, but the results support PINNs as complementary tools for meshless hemodynamics and patient-specific digital twins.

Measuring and Improving Transfer of Neural Surrogates for Vehicle Aerodynamics

Favour Nerrise, Julian Busch, Gurpreet Singh Hora, Martin Sipka, Danielle C. Maddix, Georgia Olympia Brikis

Neural surrogates for external vehicle aerodynamics are fast alternatives to physics-based computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations. Their foundational limitation is a lack of zero-shot generalization across vehicle classes, which results in an expensive, custom training campaign for each new vehicle design. Similar to pretrained models in other fields, a pretrained surrogate should adapt to a new vehicle class using only a few simulations and stay accurate on its pretraining domain. No prior study has systematically tested this transfer behavior. We conduct a large-scale, controlled benchmarking study on five DrivAerML-pretrained surrogates across four independent target datasets. These datasets differ from the source in geometry, CFD solver, turbulence model, and flow conditions. We find that fine-tuning these surrogates outperforms training from scratch in fewer than half of the comparisons and lowers the accuracy of the surrogate on its source dataset. We introduce GeoReplay, a geometry- and gradient-aligned source replay that keeps source simulations resembling the target in training and applies their updates only when they agree with the target gradient. A risk bound separates geometric coverage from differences in the simulated physics, and we prove that every sufficiently small GeoReplay step decreases the target training objective. GeoReplay lowers the source error of fine-tuning in 77 of 80 settings and matches its target accuracy.

Nonlinearly Causal General-Relativistic Superfluid Hydrodynamics: Simulating Vortex Dynamics in Neutron-Star Interiors

Yuan Feng, Elias R. Most, Lorenzo Gavassino

Neutron stars provide a natural laboratory for superfluid matter under extreme conditions. Its nonlinear dynamics is central to pulsar glitches, their possible links to fast radio bursts, and tidal interactions in inspiralling binaries. We develop a general-relativistic superfluid hydrodynamics framework incorporating entrainment, vortex forces, and line energy, and derive, for the first time, necessary and sufficient conditions for nonlinear causality and strong hyperbolicity. We also show that commonly used dissipative, force-balanced formulations without line tension are generically ill-posed at short wavelengths because of the Donnelly-Glaberson instability. Exploiting the analogy between vortex arrays with tension and force-free electrodynamics, we recast vortex dynamics in terms of effective electric and magnetic fields and drift velocities. This motivates a new finite-volume scheme combining constrained transport of superfluid vorticity with implicit electric-field evolution, enabling stable numerical evolution of the full system. We validate the scheme using an exact solution to the relativistic superfluid Riemann problem and Kelvin-wave dispersion relations, among other tests. Finally, we simulate dynamical, global general-relativistic two-fluid neutron-star equilibria, validate their oscillation modes, and present the first simplified calculation of superfluid interior spin-down with prescribed vortex release and repinning. The resulting stationary state retains both a net twist in the vortex array and residual differential rotation of the superfluid. Our framework thus enables nonlinear numerical studies of general-relativistic superfluid dynamics in neutron-star interiors.

Singular layer neural networks for the Navier-Stokes equations at small viscosity

Teng-Yuan Chang, Gung-Min Gie, Youngjoon Hong, Chang-Yeol Jung

We develop neural network methods for the two-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equations at a small viscosity $\varepsilon > 0$, whose accuracy is uniform in $\varepsilon$ and whose residual is evaluated on a grid that does not resolve the vorticity boundary layer. In the vorticity-stream function formulation with the slip boundary conditions, a vorticity layer of width $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ forms along the walls. The trial function of the singular layer Physics-Informed Neural Network (sl-PINN) contains the corrector of the asymptotic analysis, together with the law that fixes its amplitude at the wall. The amplitude is tied to the wall trace of the network itself, so that the method requires no a priori knowledge of the limit solution. The sl-PINN keeps the error uniform in $\varepsilon$ over six decades, for the Stokes and the Navier-Stokes equations alike, although in the latter the wall trace travels with the flow and is not known in advance. An enrichment that uses the thin scale but leaves the profile and the amplitude of the layer free deteriorates as $\varepsilon\to0$. Since the corrector carries the layer analytically, the Laplacian in the residual can be discretized by a finite volume flux difference on a coarse grid. The resulting singular layer Finite Volume Neural Network (sl-FVNN) attains the same uniform accuracy as the sl-PINN, to within the scatter of the random seed, at about half the cost of a training step. The combination of the asymptotic corrector with the coarse-grid finite volume discretization is the main result of this article.

Droplet formation dynamics in a T-junction via pulsed electrohydrodynamics

He Yan, Kang luo, Bin Gong, Lei Wang

Numerical simulations based on the phase-field lattice Boltzmann method are employed to investigate droplet formation dynamics in a T-junction microchannel under pulsed electrohydrodynamics. The applied electric field modulates the interfacial morphology, thereby altering the hydrodynamic stress distribution acting on the liquid-liquid interface. Three configurations are examined: no electric field, top-biased electrode, and bottom-biased electrode. The results show that the top-biased electrode promotes rapid transverse migration of the dispersed phase towards the upper wall, thereby accelerating the transition to the channel-blocking stage. By contrast, under the bottom-biased electrode, the dispersed phase first adheres to the lower wall and subsequently deforms upward under the electric force. As a result, the droplet formation dynamics are strongly affected by the spatial distribution of the electric field. The droplet generation process is further divided into a filling stage and a squeezing stage based on theoretical analysis. During the filling stage, the electric force modifies the filling duration but has little influence on the filling flux. During the squeezing stage, the necking rate and breakup time are governed by the pinning of the dispersed-phase contact line on the channel wall. Compared with constant electric forcing, pulsed electric forcing introduces an additional temporal degree of freedom, enabling stage-dependent modulation of interfacial evolution and breakup dynamics. Finally, droplet sequence diagrams and regime maps are presented for different pulse parameters. The results demonstrate that pulsed electrohydrodynamics in a T-junction enables active and programmable control of consecutive droplet generation.

Onset of three dimensionality in transonic shock boundary layer interaction over a natural laminar flow airfoil

Abhijeet Guha, Aditi Sengupta

The development of three-dimensionality in transonic shock--boundary-layer interactions (SBLI) can significantly influence the aerodynamic response of natural laminar flow (NLF) airfoils, yet the associated vorticity dynamics remain insufficiently understood. The present study investigates the short-time evolution of transonic SBLI over the SHM1 NLF airfoil using high-fidelity three-dimensional (3D) simulations at three operating conditions reported by Fujino et al., Natural-laminar-flow airfoil development for a lightweight business jet, J. Aircraft 40, 609-615 (2003). The selected cases represent three distinct operating regimes: the initial development of a normal shock, drag divergence, and shock-induced separation. Comparisons with previously reported two-dimensional (2D) simulations establish the temporal interval over which the spanwise-averaged 3D flow remains close to the corresponding 2D evolution, while departures in surface pressure and skin-friction responses identify the subsequent development of 3D effects within the SBLI region. The temporal evolution of volume-integrated streamwise and wall-normal enstrophies further characterizes the development of the associated 3D vortical field. The compressible enstrophy transport equation (CETE) is subsequently employed to examine the mechanisms governing the evolution of the 3D vortical field. Viscous and compressibility-related terms dominate the normalized enstrophy budget during the early evolution, while vortex stretching remains a minor contribution in the normal-shock and drag-divergence regimes but becomes appreciable, albeit intermittently, with the development of shock-induced separation. Across the three operating regimes, the same principal enstrophy-transport mechanisms remain active, but their relative contributions to the normalized budget are redistributed with the SBLI topology.

HAVAPlot: A Web-Based Scientific Visualization and Post-Processing Platform

Ozgur Tumuklu

This paper presents HAVAPlot, a browser-based framework for scientific visualization and post-processing of numerical simulation data. Operating entirely within the user's browser, HAVAPlot requires no software installation or data uploads while supporting mesh-based and tabular datasets beyond computational fluid dynamics. The framework directly reads native OpenFOAM cases, including polyhedral meshes, ASCII and binary fields, boundary data, and Lagrangian particles, as well as legacy and XML VTK files and tabular datasets. It provides line, contour, and three-dimensional plots; exact polyhedral slicing; boundary extraction; variable calculations; customizable color scales; symmetry mirroring; zoomed insets; streamlines; isosurfaces; particle visualization; and vector PDF/EPS and data export. The underlying numerical algorithms are independently verified. Cell centers and volumes agree with an independent implementation of OpenFOAM's geometric algorithm, while 6.1 million field values across four two-dimensional, axisymmetric, and three-dimensional production cases are reproduced to machine precision. For analytical fields on distorted hexahedral meshes, cell-to-point interpolation exhibits second-order convergence in the interior and first-order convergence at boundaries. Meshes containing 250,000 cells load in at most 1.3 seconds. Applications to an impinging supersonic jet, hypersonic cylinder flow, a hybrid particle-continuum cold-spray nozzle, a three-dimensional gas-bearing film, and supersonic cone flow demonstrate the framework's capabilities.

Stochastic Representation-Inspired Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Incompressible Fluid Flows

Zihao Guo, Zhongmin Qian

The effectiveness of neural networks in solving partial differential equations depends not only on their approximation capability but also on the suitability of their representations for the underlying solutions. Motivated by stochastic integral representations of incompressible fluid flows and the associated Biot--Savart kernel, we propose a Biot--Savart-informed (i.e. physics-informed) neural network (BS-PINN) for the incompressible Navier--Stokes equations. By extracting a rational profile from a regularized Biot--Savart kernel, we construct a smooth activation function with fixed and learnable kernel scales. Both variants retain the conventional velocity--pressure formulation and physics-informed training framework, allowing the influence of the activation function to be examined independently. We evaluate the method on five benchmarks, including Kovasznay flow, cylinder wake, three-dimensional Beltrami flow, Taylor--Green vortex, and double shear layer. Across 21 benchmark--architecture comparisons, both variants consistently reduce the combined velocity error relative to the conventional $\tanh$-based PINN, achieving mean relative error reductions of 31.7\% and 33.2\%, respectively. Improvements are also observed in reported pressure and vorticity errors. Notably, the double-shear-layer experiments demonstrate improved resolution of localized shear structures and, for BS-PINN-F, qualitatively clearer vortex-roll-up patterns in the long-time simulation. These findings highlight the potential of incorporating analytical flow structures into neural-network representations for incompressible flow simulations.

Closed-loop policy optimisation for cylinder wake control via differentiable simulation

Siwen Zhang, Xizeng Zhao, Zhouteng Ye, Yulin Xie, Zesheng Yao

Suppressing vortex shedding behind a circular cylinder is a classical problem in active flow control.Model-free deep reinforcement learning (DRL) learns such policies by interacting with the flow without differentiating its dynamics, which typically requires a large interaction budget. This study optimises closed-loop policies by differentiable simulation: control-loss gradients propagate through the simulated flow to the policy parameters, which are updated by reverse-mode automatic differentiation. The policy maps local velocity measurements to a jet amplitude imposed through differentiable synthetic-jet boundary conditions. In a two-dimensional confined cylinder wake at Rey=100, eight policy updates (about 78 shedding periods of forward simulation) give approximately 8% drag reduction and 99.6% suppression of lift fluctuations, the drag level being comparable to reported DRL results. Suppression is sustained by feedback: a mean-matched constant jet does not maintain it, whereas the same policy responds similarly at three activation phases and recovers after upstream disturbances. A velocity-deviation reward alone retains strong suppression, and observations of two instantaneous velocity components at one near-wake or surface location reach about 99.8% suppression, whereas a single surface pressure leaves residual oscillations. Together with related DRL results, this suggests that reward and observation requirements here are set by policy optimisation and flow physics.

Effects of sinusoidal pitch amplitude and phase on power extraction and pitching-energy exchange in a single-blade vertical-axis turbine model

Zihan Zhang, Anchi Li, Qimin Feng, Yuanhang Zhu, Qiang Zhong

Sinusoidal blade pitching can improve vertical-axis turbine performance, but whether a blade could follow the same motion passively depends on the energy exchanged through pitching. This study experimentally examines 480 prescribed-pitch conditions using a single-blade model following a circular trajectory in a water channel at four tip-speed ratios (TSRs) from 1.25 to 3.14. The amplitude associated with the highest measured power decreases from 16° to 12° and then 8° as TSR increases, with the corresponding phase shifting from 0 to pi/4. At TSRs of 1.25 and 3.14, the cycle-averaged power coefficient increases from 0.0047 to 0.1037 and from 0.4698 to 0.5948, respectively. Pitching-energy maps show nearly neutral net hydrodynamic pitching work for the highest-performing motions at the two lower TSRs and net energy transfer from the flow to pitching at the two higher TSRs. Virtual damping narrows the positive-energy region, although the higher-TSR best-tested motions retain an energy surplus at nonzero damping. However, net energy transfer from the flow to pitching also occurs for motions with substantial power losses. At the lowest TSR, phase-resolved force measurements and particle image velocimetry link the gain to delayed dynamic-stall-vortex formation and shedding, prolonged favorable loading, and reduced subsequent negative power. At the highest TSR, the flow and loading change locally, with little alteration of the main power-producing event. The joint maps identify performance-enhancing motions with an energetic margin against assumed dissipation, providing candidates for further passive or assisted pitching studies.

Targeting maximal suppression of turbulence, we pose a composite optimization problem that minimizes the steady-state variance of velocity fluctuations and riblet-induced dynamical augmentations while maintaining a constant bulk-flux. For a fixed spanwise wavelength (riblet spacing), this framework yields optimal wall-normal profiles that achieve maximal turbulence attenuation.

How Meandering Flow Sculpts Ice

Yihong Du, Jin Hu, Feng Wang, Lars ten Hacken, Ziqi Wang, Rui Yang

Ice-water phase change subject to a mean flow widely exists in the Cryosphere, with implications on glacier evolution, human transportation, flooding events, etc. Understanding the relevant phase change dynamics requires identifying the roles of shear and rotation due to the mean flow. Combining experiments, numerical simulations and theoretical modelling, we reveal the combined effects of shear and rotation on the coupled dynamics of water flow, heat transfer and phase change in an annular system. Increasing shear and rotation drive a stronger secondary circulation which significantly enhances global heat transfer and redistributes the radial heat flux, and eventually lead to a thinner equilibrium ice layer and a larger ice thickness difference in the radial direction. Further, we develop an integral thermal balance model that explains the mean ice thickness through estimating the additional heat transfer, and a local thermal balance model that explains the inner-outer ice thickness difference by relating it to the non-uniform convective heat flux. The local model shows qualitative agreement with field measurements when applied to an ice-covered meandering river. These results contribute to understanding the common physical mechanism for ice-water phase change under shear and rotation, offering a theoretical basis for explaining and predicting ice evolution in meandering rivers among other relevant scenarios.

Augmentation of cardiovascular flow measurements with generative diffusion models

Fabio Guglietta, Tianyi Li, Francesco Viola, Luca Biferale

Cardiovascular imaging offers several ways to measure blood flow, but each provides an incomplete, resolution-limited, or noisy view of the underlying dynamics. Reconstructing the missing information is inherently ambiguous because several flow fields may be compatible with the same observation. Here, we investigate generative diffusion models as tools for cardiovascular-flow generation and reconstruction using a fully controlled state-of-the-art dataset from a digital twin of a healthy human left heart. First, we show how unconditional samples reproduce the changing anatomy and the highly fluctuating statistical properties of the flow throughout the cardiac cycle. Second, we test the conditional inference of our generative model by reconstructing an entire 2D planar section of geometry and flow configurations from a limited portion. Repeated reconstructions allow us to represent a range of plausible completions, going beyond a single deterministic estimate, while their mean retains the principal features of the reference flow. Comparison with a Gaussian process regression baseline on the same reduced domain highlights the main advantage of diffusion models: their reconstructed ensembles capture a broader range of velocity and gradient fluctuations, better encompassing ground-truth features that the baseline distributions can miss. Our results provide a quantitative proof of concept for probabilistic reconstruction of incomplete cardiovascular-flow measurements and demonstrate the potential for groundbreaking applications to the augmentation of in-vivo cardiovascular imaging data.

A sliding mode observer perspective on chaos synchronisation of turbulent flows

Defne E. Ozan, Andrew Wynn

State estimation of turbulent flows is challenging as infinitesimal prediction errors grow exponentially due to chaos. However, when signals from one chaotic system (the master) are injected into an identical system with different initial conditions (the slave), the slave can synchronise with the master --- a phenomenon observed in turbulent flows. From a control-theoretic perspective, this synchronisation problem can be viewed as observer design.
