Hard Constraint Projection in a Physics Informed Neural Network (Burgers)

Miranda J S Horne, Peter K Jimack, Amirul Khan, He Wang

Physics informed machine learning provides a framework to study and simulate fluid dynamics, potentially at a fraction of the computational cost of traditional numerical methods, with the goal of improving the explainability, accuracy and robustness of purely data driven machine learning methods. In this work, we extend the hard constraint projection (HCP) method developed by Chen et al. [Journal of Computational Physics, 445, 110624, 2021] from a linear partial differential equation (PDE) to a nonlinear PDE: the 1D Burgers' equation. To strictly impose a discrete approximation of the governing equation, the Burgers' equation is first locally discretised (using a finite difference stencil) then decomposed into a constraint matrix and a prediction matrix. Using tools from linear algebra, the constraint matrix is used to construct a projection matrix. A physics informed neural network (PINN) [Raissi et al. Journal of Computational Physics, 378, 686, 2019] is used to predict the velocity, and a final unlearnable layer projects the velocity prediction to the hyperplane of exact solutions to a discretised form of the Burgers' equation. Our initial results indicate that the HCP-PINN initially trains similarly to an equivalent PINN, and as the PINN loss plateaus, the HCP-PINN loss continues to decline. When tested on a previously unseen uniform grid of points covering the domain, the HCP-PINN exhibits higher accuracy, converging to a data error approximately an order of magnitude lower than the comparable PINN. We also observe that the inclusion of the HCP layer results in better observance of the governing PDE. Our findings suggest that the HCP-PINN can be more robust to the selection of physics collocation points, and less dependent on collocation point quantity.

Differentiable Schwarz-Coupled Solvers for Thin Boundary Layer Transport: Inverse Modeling via Agentic AI Optimization

Siva Viknesh, Amirhossein Arzani

High-Péclet-number transport problems give rise to thin boundary layers with steep near-wall gradients whose accurate solution requires high-resolution meshes and could challenge inverse modeling. To address this challenge, we develop an overlapping Schwarz domain-decomposition framework grounded in perturbation theory, in which an inner equation governs near-wall dynamics and an outer equation governs bulk transport, and both subproblems are discretized using the finite element method and assembled into a fully differentiable multidomain physics solver. The formulation permits coarser mesh resolutions, reducing computational cost relative to a single-domain formulation. Exploiting the differentiability of the coupled solver, we formulate inverse problems in which the governing equations and boundary conditions are imposed as hard constraints, with the Schwarz coupling enforcing interface continuity. To accelerate the resulting adjoint-based optimization, we integrate large language models (LLMs) into the inverse workflow in two distinct configurations: a standalone optimizer that proposes successive parameter updates from the adjoint-computed sensitivities and an agentic artificial intelligence (AI) approach with access to the complete solver workflow, enabling it to perform multistage optimization procedures. The agentic AI configuration is shown to be most advantageous when the spatially varying unknown is represented in a functional basis rather than a high-dimensional nodal field representation. In this case, the AI agent grows the library of candidate basis functions during optimization under a prescribed admission policy, admitting predefined families in stages and initializing their parameters. In short, our proposed framework integrates Schwarz-coupled differentiable physics with agentic AI optimization for the inverse modeling of multiscale transport problems.

Physics-Informed Latent Neural ODEs for Reduced-Order Modeling of Hydrogen, Ammonia, and Methane Combustion Kinetics

Jay Patel, Shubhangi Bansude

Detailed chemical kinetic mechanisms are computationally expensive in reacting-flow simulations because of their high dimensionality and numerical stiffness. We develop an end-to-end physics-informed autoencoder neural ordinary differential equation (PI-AE-NODE) framework in which an encoder compresses the thermochemical state into a low-dimensional latent space, a Neural ODE advances it continuously in time, and a decoder reconstructs the temperature and all species mass fractions. Elemental conservation, mass conservation, and enthalpy consistency are embedded in the training objective. The framework is applied to H$_2$/air, NH$_3$/air, and CH$_4$/air autoignition using detailed mechanisms of increasing size and complexity, which are represented accurately with only a few latent variables. Compared with an otherwise identical data-driven model, the physical constraints improve ignition delay prediction and conservation behaviour. The models reproduce major and intermediate species evolution at both training and unseen conditions, and their latent dynamics permit efficient explicit integration, with the computational advantage over the detailed mechanisms increasing with mechanism size.

Triadic energy cascades and linear amplification mechanisms in supersonic turbulent boundary layers over curved walls

Lucas Feitosa de Souza, Hugo F. da Silva Lui, William Roberto Wolf, Pedro Stefanin Volpiani

Direct numerical simulations are performed to investigate supersonic turbulent boundary layers subjected to continuous concave and convex surface curvatures. Scale-resolved spectral turbulent kinetic energy (TKE) budgets, triadic energy-transfer bispectra, spectral proper orthogonal decomposition (SPOD), and global resolvent analysis are employed to elucidate the effects of compressibility and pressure gradients, and to distinguish the linear and nonlinear mechanisms at play. Over concave walls, the destabilizing curvature enhances pressure-strain redistribution, shifting near-wall turbulence toward a two-component state, and induces a shear-driven outer-layer production peak at low spanwise wavenumbers. Bispectral and SPOD analyses reveal that this outer-layer energy feeds a structured, multi-step triadic transfer toward fundamental large-scale and spanwise-uniform modes, which physically manifests as streamwise vortex breakdown rather than a three-dimensional inverse cascade. Global resolvent analysis further demonstrates that while nonlinear triadic interactions select the dominant scale amplitudes, the spatial topology of these coherent structures is dictated by linear mean-flow receptivity and spatially developing convective instabilities. Conversely, over convex walls, stabilizing curvature and dilatation attenuate pressure fluctuations and suppress pressure-strain redistribution, breaking the near-wall turbulent cycle and trapping energy within elongated, one-component streamwise streaks, while a localized resolvent wavemaker energizes the downstream recovery region.

Agentic AI Reasoning and Learned Sampling for Task-Aware Projection Selection in Sparse-View CT: Predicting Hemodynamics

Mahmoud Elhadidy, Roshan M. D'Souza, Amirhossein Arzani

Sparse-view computed tomography (CT) reduces the number of acquired projections, but conventional sampling strategies are designed primarily for image reconstruction rather than for downstream physical inference. Here, we investigate optimal task-aware projection selection for directly predicting hemodynamics from sparse CT angiography (CTA) measurements. Using computational fluid dynamics simulations of intracranial aneurysms, we generate synthetic fan-beam CTA projections and train a Fourier neural operator (FNO) to predict two targets: the spatially resolved time-averaged wall shear stress (TAWSS) field and the spatially averaged TAWSS. The FNO was coupled with optimal input selection for simultaneous optimal sampling and training. Our goal was to investigate if agentic AI could discover optimal sampling strategies that outperform current methods. We compared fixed uniform and golden-angle sampling with three task-aware optimal selection strategies: Gumbel-Softmax, a Soft top-K method, and an agentic AI selector. The methods were compared at different view selection budgets. A detailed analysis of the AI agent was performed to understand its reasoning and performance. For spatially resolved TAWSS, Agentic and Soft top-K achieve the lowest mean errors (Agentic performs slightly better) and substantially outperform fixed sampling under extreme sparsity. For spatially averaged TAWSS, optimized selection becomes important at extremely sparse acquisitions. Analysis of the selected masks reveals multiple distinct subsets concentrated around informative regions. Moreover, masks optimized for hemodynamic prediction yield poor image reconstructions, showing that task-optimal acquisition differs fundamentally from reconstruction-optimal acquisition. This work suggests the possibility of designing sparse acquisitions directly around downstream physical quantities.

Inherent Failure Modes of Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Buoyancy-Driven Flow: Role of Non-Dimensionalization in Network Training

Krishan Chand, Ahmad Peyvan, Haoxiang Luo

We investigate the influence of non-dimensionalization on the trainability and accuracy of physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) for buoyancy-driven flows. Using a differentially heated square cavity at $\mathrm{Pr}=0.71$ and $\mathrm{Ra}=10^3$--$10^5$, we compare thermal-diffusive and free-fall velocity scalings under identical training conditions. The free-fall scaling, with a unit buoyancy coefficient, achieves errors below 1.1\% in the mean Nusselt number for $\mathrm{Ra}\le10^4$, whereas the diffusive scaling, with a buoyancy coefficient of $\mathrm{Ra}\,\mathrm{Pr}$, produces errors of 157--650\% and diverges at $\mathrm{Ra}=10^5$. We identify a coefficient-induced gradient imbalance, with the dominant buoyancy-related contribution to the loss gradient scaling as $(\mathrm{Ra}\,\mathrm{Pr})^2$, and confirm it through direct gradient measurements. Neither sparse-data supervision nor adaptive loss weighting resolves the training difficulties under the tested conditions. For unsteady convection, the free-fall PINN accurately predicts long-time heat transfer but fails to capture the transient Nusselt-number overshoot. These findings establish non-dimensionalization as an important design consideration for improving PINN trainability in buoyancy-driven flows.

Benchmarking One-Dimensional Quantum Lattice Boltzmann Methods on Quantum Hardware

Melvina Quartey, Lucy T. Zhang

Classical computational fluid dynamics (CFD) methods encounter significant computational bottlenecks in large systems and at high Reynolds number flows due to the rapid increase in degrees of freedom, even on flagship supercomputers. As quantum computers emerge, the Quantum Lattice Boltzmann Method (QLBM) has been proposed as a quantum-native alternative for solving fluid dynamics problems. However, until recently, nearly all studies have relied on classical quantum simulators rather than real quantum hardware. This study presents a benchmark of a one-dimensional QLBM implementation executed on three different IBM Quantum hardware. We design and test a quantum circuit for the advection-diffusion equation using amplitude encoding, a Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook (BGK)-type collision operator, and a streaming step with periodic boundary conditions. Our numerical experiments evaluate transpiled two-qubit gate count, total circuit depth, runtime, and accuracy metrics including maximum absolute error ($L_\infty$ norm) and root-mean-square error (RMSE). We find that the present collision and streaming operators dominate the overall cost and become the primary scalability bottleneck on current hardware. To mitigate the prohibitive cost of full quantum state tomography on hardware-based benchmarking exercises, we introduce a sampler-based measurement strategy that reconstructs macroscopic density fields directly from computational-basis measurements. This approach substantially reduces measurement overhead while preserving the expected advection-diffusion dynamics on hardware. These results provide a quantitative assessment of QLBM performance in the noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) regime and identify the principal algorithmic and experimental limitations that must be addressed for QLBM to become viable for fluid dynamics.

Regional accuracy of physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) for recirculation in a deep lid-driven cavity

Ehsan Roohi, Christopher J. McDevitt

Deep lid-driven cavities contain strong motion beneath the lid and much weaker recirculation at depth, so agreement in bulk velocity is an incomplete assessment of a computed flow. We examine data-free physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) for steady, two-dimensional incompressible flow in a cavity of depth-to-width ratio five at Reynolds numbers 100, 250, 500 and 1000. An established hard-constrained streamfunction formulation is combined with prescribed regional collocation, transferred initialization, and selected residual-scaling and envelope variants. Independent spectral/$hp$ calculations with the same Gaussian-regularized lid provide the reference fields. For a cavity of depth-to-width ratio $D/W=5$ at Reynolds numbers $\mathrm{Re}=100$, 250, 500, and 1000, evaluation on a common $121\times605$ grid gives interior relative velocity discrepancies of 5.130\%--7.850\%. Over the same cases, floor-region relative velocity discrepancies remain below 5\%, with absolute RMS discrepancies ranging from $1.255\times10^{-10}U$ to $2.332\times10^{-9}U$, where $U$ is the lid-velocity scale. In a separate $161\times801$ assessment at $\Rey=1000$, the refined workflow lowers interior velocity discrepancy from 5.624\% to 2.658\% and gauge-aligned pressure RMS discrepancy from $2.793\times10^{-3}$ to $1.167\times10^{-3}ρU^2$. The improvements compare accumulated training workflows and do not isolate regional sampling. Configuration rankings depend on both region and observable. These finite-budget comparisons establish where the retained workflows agree with the reference and where rotational-field discrepancies remain substantial.

An Optimal Seed Reveals the Dynamical Route to Spontaneous Splitting of Turbulent Puffs

Chihang Liu, Hao Ni, Jiashun Guan

The localized turbulence in pipe flow, known as puffs, is sustained by their proliferation. This process is crucial to the subcritical laminar-turbulent transition, a century-old fundamental problem related to a wide range of natural and engineered systems. Here, we leverage the nonlinear nonmodal analysis of a reduced-order model to capture the full Navier-Stokes dynamics of the splitting process: from the splitting seed through the edge state to successful splitting, even when the pre-edge evolution is obscured by the puff's intrinsic chaotic fluctuations. Moreover, the optimized evolution provides a splitting-progress coordinate to frame a phase-space representation of the proliferation, resolving the temporal desynchronization among chaotic realizations and thereby enabling dynamically aligned ensemble averaging to uncover the underlying splitting dynamics. The splitting seed is characterized by a strong velocity inflection, and its energy threshold exhibits a power-law scaling with the Reynolds number, giving the optimal solution direct physical consistency in spontaneous puff splitting.

Brener Ramos, William R. Wolf, Tarcísio C. D. Oliveira, Scott T. M. Dawson

Full-order surrogates for high-dimensional nonlinear flows are often unnecessary when applications require only selected degrees of freedom. Applied to parameterized 2D/3D confined cylinder flows and an unevenly heated cavity over Reynolds/Rayleigh-number ranges, the models generalize to unseen conditions and accurately predict sparse probe locations and integrated quantities. These results show that coarse-mesh representations, adaptive sampling, and noise-augmented unrolled training form an efficient framework for reduced-order modeling of parameterized unsteady flows without solver-resolution reconstruction.

Turbulence on Hybrid Lattices: regular grids meet self similar cascade models

Lorenzo Manfredini, Özgür D. Gürcan, Sai Swetha Venkata Kolluru, Berengere Dubrulle

Turbulent cascades in high Reynolds number regimes span many decades in scale, which can be challenging for numerical simulations. Cascade models that use logarithmic discretization can help, but these have limited capabilities to describe spatial structures or real space details. Hybrid Lattices (HLs) are proposed in order to provide a novel framework to investigate turbulent cascades in Fourier space, combining regular grids and cascade models based on logarithmic discretisations. The approach consists of a regular spectral region, capable of resolving large scale coherent structures, that is extended in a recurrent, asymptotically self similar fashion, spanning many decades in scale. The idea comes from shell models and their extensions, namely the logarithmic lattices, the nested polyhedra models or the spiral chain models. The logarithmic spacing factor of those models, such as the golden ratio, typically prevents interactions between a regular grid and a self similar model. HLs are constructed by expanding a regular region in Fourier space through recurrence relations (e.g. the Fibonacci recurrence relation), generating an asymptotically logarithmic lattice where Cartesian components remain integer multiples of the regular Fourier space grid spacing. This way, the resulting grid preserves triadic interactions linking regular and recurrent regions, allowing energy transfer across these different regions. However, the sharp reduction in triads connecting the two regions creates a bottleneck effect, and in order to address this issue, a region of ghost points at the boundary had to be introduced, efficiently allowing the flux to leak into the recurrent part. The HL framework is then tested for the one dimensional Burgers equation, and for two dimensional incompressible Navier Stokes turbulence.

Hydrodynamic regimes of the edge-tone in a simplified recorder mouthpiece

A. Tassawar, R. Velasco-Segura, P. Rendon, M. Fosas de Pando, R. Ostilla-Mónico

The acoustic mechanism of flue instruments originates in the interaction of an air jet with a sharp edge, the labium. Previous studies have examined isolated edge-tones or full instruments in which acoustic resonance obscures the underlying hydrodynamics, and a systematic understanding of how geometric parameters govern jet stability is lacking. We present a large-scale parametric study of the jet-labium interaction in a simplified recorder mouthpiece without a resonator, using more than 200 two-dimensional direct numerical simulations with an immersed boundary method. We vary the mouth distance, the labium angle, the vertical offset of the labium, and the Reynolds number (Re), and characterize the dynamics through spectra of the transverse force on the labium. The baseline configuration oscillates as a predominantly periodic Stage I edge-tone with Strouhal number St ~ 0.07, nearly constant for Re >= 330 and for all labium angles at the baseline mouth distance, so the frequency is set by the geometry rather than by viscosity. The mouth distance has the strongest influence: it is the only parameter that introduces a second oscillation stage, and the Stage I frequency is inversely proportional to it. The second stage is weakly present at five and six windway heights, established from six and a half, and dominant from eight, where it strongly modulates the amplitude. The vertical offset mainly affects the strength of the oscillation, and the labium angle does not change the instability mechanism. A baseline configuration is identified for future aeroacoustic coupling studies.

ART of PIV: Agentic Real-Time Particle Image Velocimetry for Active Flow Control

Antonio Terpin, Francesco Banelli, Alan Bonomi, Raffaello D'Andrea

Particle image velocimetry (PIV) measures fluid motion from images of tracer particles, but its accuracy depends on choices throughout the estimation pipeline, including image preprocessing, flow estimation, and postprocessing. These choices are typically left to expert tuning, and a fixed configuration can become less accurate as conditions change. This matters especially when PIV supplies feedback for real-time control, where measurements must be both accurate and fast, and flow conditions vary. We introduce ART of PIV -- short for Agentic Real-Time and also a nod to the art of tuning a PIV pipeline -- which automates pipeline choice for each image pair. A learned agent selects a configuration according to a chosen measurement objective from a library assembled for a specified speed budget. Across cross-correlation, classical optical-flow, and learning-based estimators, ART of PIV reduces average end-point error (AEPE) by 12.2-22.4 % relative to the best single estimator at matched speed, with gains extending to 1000-4000 image pair/s. In a simulated local velocity regulation task, it reduces mean normalized closed-loop cost by 13.24-13.59 percentage points relative to a tuned fixed estimator, with the same classical controller in both cases. In a tabletop water channel, we illustrate two ways to tune ART of PIV for a fluidic pinball of three rotating cylinders: selecting between complementary optical-flow tunings for accuracy, and selecting regions of interest with coherent rotation or shear using a common flow estimator. For the first variant, we train a DreamerV3 reinforcement learning controller directly in the physical world and demonstrate tracking of a prescribed flow field in the wake of a fluidic pinball.

A Large Eddy Simulation Study of Turbine Pitch Motion Effects on Power Generation in Fully Developed Floating Wind Turbine Arrays

Yishu Zhang, Dennice F. Gayme, Charles Meneveau

Interactions between floating wind turbines and the marine atmospheric boundary layer affect turbine wakes and power output. This study examines the impact of turbine representations and platform pitching motion on power predictions for aligned floating wind turbine arrays. Large-eddy simulations (LES) are performed for a fully developed wind turbine array boundary layer with periodic streamwise and spanwise boundary conditions and moving surface drag (MOSD) at the bottom surface. A proposed power-curve-aware extension of the conventional actuator disk model (PCADM) is compared with the traditional constant coefficient actuator disk model (CCADM), which is valid in principle only during Region 2 operation. Harmonic pitching motion matched to a representative ocean wave frequency is prescribed for IEA 15MW turbines mounted on the UMaine VolturnUS-S platform. Results demonstrate that the CCADM produces unphysical power overshoot when relative velocities induced by harmonic pitching periodically exceed rated speed, while the PCADM eliminates these unrealistic excursions from the actual turbine operating range. Various wind-wave conditions and relative pitching motions for an aligned farm with PCADM turbines are analyzed, including in-phase, opposite-phase, and incommensurate-phase row-to-row relative turbine motion. Results reveal that, depending on operational regions, turbine pitching motion can either increase or decrease mean turbine power output. Among the relative phases considered, in-phase motions produce larger fluctuations at the farm scale due to stronger turbine-turbine correlations. These findings highlight the importance of accounting for turbine motion and associated changes in operational regions when modeling turbine and wind farm power production in LES of offshore floating wind farms using actuator disk representations.

An analytical framework for wave-coherent airflow over fast surface waves

Aditya K. Aiyer

Classical theories attribute wind-wave momentum transfer to a critical layer, where the mean wind speed equals the wave phase speed. For waves that travel faster than the near-surface wind, this layer may lie outside the wave boundary layer or be absent altogether. We develop an analytical description of the wave-coherent airflow in this regime for a prescribed mean wind, using a Wentzel--Kramers--Brillouin--Jeffreys (WKBJ) approximation to the viscous Orr--Sommerfeld equation in wave-following coordinates. The solution combines an outer Rayleigh mode, which carries the dominant wave-coherent motion, with a thin viscous Stokes layer that supplies the phase shift producing the pressure form stress. At leading order, the form stress scales with the inverse square root of the wave Reynolds number, with a coefficient set by an integral of the outer response, and transfers momentum from the waves to the air. For an exponential mean profile, the WKBJ approximation gives closed-form expressions for the wave-coherent response. To accommodate the rapid near-surface variation of realistic wall-bounded profiles, we develop a long-wave expansion near the surface and match it to the outer WKBJ mode (LW--WKBJ). For wall-modelled simulations, whose first resolved level lies above the viscous sublayer, we replace the molecular viscosity in the Stokes layer with a constant-stress effective viscosity. Using mean profiles from direct numerical simulations and wave-phase-resolved large-eddy simulations, the LW--WKBJ framework closely reproduces the dominant wave-coherent velocity and pressure obtained from numerical solutions and available simulation data. The predicted form-stress coefficients also agree well with the available LES estimates.

Physics of Fluids 36] which can impact the accuracy and robustness of AD-LES. Specifically, we introduce the Alternative Local Dynamic Model with Kinetic energy equation (A-LDMK), an improved non-equilibrium one-equation mixed eddy-viscosity AD framework. Specifically, the proposed A-LDMK provides the most accurate prediction of the multiscale primary atomization cascade in terms of droplet size distribution, compared to the other assessed closures.

Frozen in Time: How a Physics Foundation Model Forgets Under Sim-to-Real Adaptation

Haaris Mian, Miles Cranmer, Alessandro Favero, Payel Mukhopadhyay

Scientific foundation models (SciFMs) have achieved remarkable performance on a wide range of PDEs, including the ability to efficiently adapt to unseen data distributions with additional finetuning. As with all neural architectures, finetuning such models comes with the risk of catastrophic forgetting, whereby adapting to new tasks degrades prior knowledge of the model. We study catastrophic forgetting in Walrus-1.3B, a SciFM for continuum dynamics, over the course of a two-stage sim-to-real adaptation: first finetuning on simulated flow around a cylinder and then adapting to experimental data of the same system. We find that finetuning on the simulations rapidly degrades rollout performance on the original pretraining datasets, with one-step errors increasing up to 35x after just 20 epochs. Subsequent adaptation to real data further erodes performance on the simulated task. We characterize the potential drivers of forgetting, such as dataset dependence and task representation similarity. We then show that replaying as little as 1% simulated data during real-data finetuning recovers most of the lost simulation performance while retaining effective adaptation to the experimental dynamics. Our results identify catastrophic forgetting as a practical obstacle to sim-to-real adaptation of SciFMs and show that lightweight replay strategies significantly improve retention.

Optimization of curved vortex generator geometry for enhanced thermal performance in water-to-air finned-tube heat exchanger

Josip Batista, Anica Trp, Kristian Lenić, Mateo Kirinčić

In the study, the geometry of curved rectangular vortex generators (RVG) has been optimized to enhance heat transfer and overall performance of the analyzed water-to-air finned-tube heat exchanger (FTHE). The developed and validated conjugate numerical model includes a small section of the full-scale heat exchanger representing the computational domain, with fully developed velocity and temperature profiles specified at the inlet boundaries of the water. Four influential geometry parameters of the vortex generator were selected for the optimization; attack angle, curvature angle, axial position in the streamwise direction and height. The optimization process has been performed according to two criteria: maximization of heat transfer solely and maximization of heat transfer while considering the influence of pressure drop. Accordingly, one objective function related to the Colburn factor (j) and two objective functions combining the Colburn factor and the friction factor (f) were defined. The regression analysis has been used and the Central composite design of experiment was selected in order to obtain non-linear regression polynomial functions which were integrated into the investigated objectives. The optimization procedure has been performed using the genetic algorithm. Optimized configurations performance improvement was evaluated through comparison with the performance of the reference FTHE with plain fins. For the RVG configuration optimized according to the objective of maximizing heat transfer, the Colburn factor was enhanced by 53.1%. Optimizing the RVG configuration to maximize heat transfer while accounting for the influence of pressure drop resulted in an enhancement of the FTHE area goodness factor by 14.4% for one set of geometry parameters, and an enhancement of volume goodness factor by 26.5% for another.

Causality-informed dynamic mode decomposition for unsteady flow analysis

Yihua Pan, Jiaqing Kou

Causal analysis characterizes directional dependencies in dynamical systems, while such information is not typically used to inform reduced-order modeling of unsteady flows. This study proposes a Granger Causality-informed Dynamic Mode Decomposition (GC-DMD) framework that incorporates such information into data-driven modeling and modal analysis of high-dimensional flow data. The framework uses conditional Granger causality estimates to regularize the identification of the DMD operator, preferentially suppressing weakly supported interactions while preserving dynamically relevant couplings. This regularization promotes sparse operators informed by directional predictive dependencies, leading to improved predictive accuracy and identification of dynamically relevant modes. GC-DMD is evaluated on a linear system with a known coupling structure, lid-driven cavity flows with varying dynamical complexity, and a broadband transonic turbulent jet. Compared with standard DMD, GC-DMD improves operator identification accuracy in the linear benchmark and improves the robustness of the identified spectrum while reducing long-horizon prediction errors in the cavity flows. For the turbulent jet, it yields a sparser operator with improved prediction accuracy. These results demonstrate the potential of incorporating causality information to guide the identification of sparse, predictive reduced-order models of complex flows. Nonlinear dynamics of short-wave instabilities in a helical vortex within and beyond the weakly nonlinear regime

Daisuke Nishiyama, Yuji Hattori

The nonlinear evolution of a helical vortex with axial flow disturbed by an elliptic or curvature instability mode is investigated by direct numerical simulations. The normalized vortex core radius and reduced pitch are systematically varied to examine a wide range of linear growth rates, revealing two types of nonlinear evolution. At relatively small growth rates, both instabilities exhibit weakly nonlinear saturation, with the vortex and unstable mode structures largely preserved. Together with previous findings for other vortex configurations, these results reveal that weakly nonlinear saturation is a generic feature of short-wave instabilities. At larger growth rates, the instabilities reach higher energy levels and evolve beyond the weakly nonlinear regime, with the vortex and unstable mode structures substantially deformed. Comparison with weakly nonlinear analysis suggests that larger linear growth rates require larger disturbance amplitudes for nonlinear effects to balance linear growth. At these larger amplitudes, higher-order nonlinear effects and modal components not retained in the weakly nonlinear analysis become increasingly important, as demonstrated by the numerical results. This provides a new physical interpretation of the departure from the weakly nonlinear regime. Since helical vortices serve as a model for tip vortices shed from rotating blades, these results offer useful insights for tip-vortex control in wind turbines, which can potentially accelerate wake recovery and thereby improve turbine performance.
