Assisted Turbulence Modeling via Data Assimilation and Machine Learning for Turbomachinery Flows

Tom Moussie, Pierric Joseph, Marcello Meldi

This work presents the development and assessment of a hybrid framework coupling numerical simulation, experimental samples, data assimilation (DA) and machine learning (ML) to investigate the flow within an axial compressor stage. The methodology first implements a Multi-Fidelity Ensemble Kalman Filter (MFEnKF) which integrates experimental PIV data with a two-fidelity numerical simulation setup, combining steady-state RANS on a fixed grid with unsteady hybrid RANS/LES simulations on a moving grid. This data assimilation step successfully reduces discrepancies between experiments and CFD realizations. Besides, it provides additional high fidelity samples when compared with the available PIV data. This enriched database is used to train machine learning (ML) tools, with the objective to obtain black-box forcing terms to be included in the discretized form of the Navier-Stokes equations. These models are expected to preserve the accuracy of the DA results while not longer requiring the input of experimental data. The neural network architecture used here maps local physical features such as the velocity, the velocity gradients and the turbulent viscosity, to obtain the volume forcing terms. The analysis of the performance of such ML models shows that global accuracy is preserved but localized increased errors in specific regions of the compressor, such as close to the blade tips, can exceed those observed from the baseline RANS simulations. To resolve this issue, a Mixture of Experts (MoE) method is employed, which classifies and combines the trained ML tools for different flow regions. The discrepancy with experimental data of this method is systematically lower than the one observed for the baseline RANS solution and very similar to the one obtained with the MFEnKF.

CuAD: Source-to-Source Automatic Differentiation of CUDA Kernels for Discrete Adjoints in Computational Fluid Dynamics

Seunghyun Joo

Discrete adjoint methods give the sensitivities that aerodynamic design and data assimilation require, but they remain hard to obtain for flow solvers that run on graphics processors. This paper presents CuAD, a source-to-source automatic differentiation tool that reads CUDA kernels and writes their tangent and adjoint versions as ordinary CUDA source. Its reverse mode is organised around the GPU thread and exploits the structure of flow-solver kernels: pure accumulations need no tape, the remaining intermediate values are recomputed rather than stored, and adjoint updates are atomic only where several threads reach the same memory. We evaluate CuAD on four kernels from simple flow models: a viscous Burgers residual, a two-dimensional advection-diffusion residual, a quasi-one-dimensional Euler residual and a Biot-Savart summation over point vortices. All generated derivatives agree with finite differences and pass adjoint dot-product tests to round-off, while the adjoint kernels cost two to six times the primal kernels. Compared with the compiler-based tool Clad under the same compiler, CuAD gives identical results at identical cost on the stencil kernels and is 16 to 470 times faster on the summation kernel because it stores no tape. A steady Burgers study shows that in single precision the adjoint gradient of a finite-difference Jacobian reaches errors of 45 to 75 per cent once the condition number grows to $2.5\times10^{5}$, whereas the CuAD Jacobian keeps the error below $5\times10^{-5}$. Four applications complete the evaluation: adjoint fields of the Burgers shock, the sensitivity of a scalar sensor in a cellular vortex, the inverse design of a transonic nozzle whose area is recovered to $2\times10^{-8}$, and an unsteady adjoint of the roll-up of a vortex sheet over 400 time steps.

Reynolds-number-dependent entrance bias in temperature polarization of lab-scale membrane distillation

Soyoung Kim, Tiezheng Tong, Yinuo Noah Yao

Lab-scale membrane distillation (MD) modules are commonly used for membrane characterization and developing empirical heat-transfer correlations. Entrance-induced flow structures can introduce geometry-specific effects into measured temperature polarization (TP) and heat transfer. However, the dependence of these effects on module geometry and flow condition remains unclear. We performed three-dimensional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations of direct contact membrane distillation (DCMD) over inlet Reynolds numbers of $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}=10$-$1000$, focusing on side-chamber length and main-channel height. We discovered that counter-rotating streamwise vortices formed near the main-channel entrance across all geometries, but their downstream persistence increased with $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$. At lower $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$, the vortices mostly decayed before mid-channel, while at higher $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$ they persisted farther downstream and produced pronounced spatial variations in TP. Increasing side-chamber length reduced the secondary-flow kinetic energy entering the main channel, and generally strengthened TP, whereas increasing main-channel height reduced the bulk velocity and streamwise kinetic energy, resulting in a thicker thermal boundary layer and stronger TP. For most geometries, the deviation from the straight-channel baseline changed from stronger TP at lower $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$ to weaker TP at higher $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$. Entrance effects therefore vary in both magnitude and direction with operating condition and cannot be represented by a constant correction across the Reynolds-number range.

Fourier-Feature-Embedded Complex-Valued Physics-Informed Convolutional Residual Networks for Sparse and Gappy Noisy Flow-Field Reconstruction

Biswanath Barman, Rajendra K. Ray

Reconstructing flow fields from sparse and noisy measurements becomes particularly challenging when data are completely absent from dynamically critical regions, such as the vortex-formation region of a wake. Conventional interpolation and numerical reconstruction methods generally require sufficient spatial or boundary information, while physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) can also face difficulties when sparse and noisy observations provide little or no supervision in the region of interest. To address this gap, we propose a Fourier-feature-embedded complex-valued physics-informed convolutional residual network (FCV-PICRN) for sparse, gappy, and noisy flow-field reconstruction. The framework integrates Fourier features for oscillatory and multiscale structures, complex-valued representations for coupled amplitude--phase learning, and convolutional residual blocks constrained by the Navier--Stokes equations. The method is first verified using the Taylor--Green vortex at $Re=100$ and then evaluated for the cylinder wake at $Re=100$. Three severe missing-data configurations are imposed in the dynamically sensitive wake: a large hole, a large truncation, and a plus-shaped gap extending in both streamwise and transverse directions. These gaps are further combined with additive Gaussian and multiplicative speckle noise. Despite having no supervised velocity data inside the excluded regions, FCV-PICRN reconstructs velocity, pressure, and vorticity with high structural similarity index (SSIM) and peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), and remains robust under Gaussian noise up to $20\%$. Finally, for forced two-dimensional Kolmogorov turbulence with $5\%$ Gaussian noise, FCV-PICRN yields lower reconstruction errors than a conventional PINN. These results demonstrate robust physics-informed reconstruction of severely incomplete and noisy flows across laminar and turbulent regimes.

How stagnation lines move, persist and break up in fluid flows

Prabakaran Rajamanickam

Stagnation curves of arbitrary shape (zeros of $\mathbf v(\mathbf x,t)$) are non-generic yet common; we derive their motion. Differentiating $\mathbf v=\mathbf0$ gives $\mathbf L\mathbf U=-\mathbf a$ ($\mathbf U$ curve velocity, $\mathbf a=\mathbf v_t$, $\mathbf L=\nabla\mathbf v$ kills tangent, acts on normal plane via $2\times2$ block $\mathbf K$); dynamics enters only via $\mathbf a$. Normal velocity: $-\mathbf K^{-1}$ times normal $\mathbf a$; tangential solvability: scalar condition $Σ=0$ per point ($Σ$: part of $\mathbf a$ outside range $\mathbf L$). Potential flow: $\mathbf K$ is strain rate, normal velocity pressure gradient over principal strain rate $ε$, persistence needs uniform pressure on it. Stokes flow: tangential vorticity enters $\mathbf K$ (centre lines), $Σ$ involves normal vorticity. Navier-Stokes: $Σ$ set by instantaneous flow via nonlocal pressure, viscosity. If $Σ\ne0$, line becomes, to first order in $t$, isolated points at zeros of $Σ$ (critical points of $\varphi_t$ if potential; maybe none in Stokes flow: loops vanish). Symmetry (line an orbit of rigid-motion group, no velocity along it) suffices for persistence, not necessary: flows steady in rigidly moving frame have $Σ\equiv0$. Incompressibility enters via $\mathrm{tr}\mathbf K=0$; for inviscid irrotational homentropic compressible flow, line dilatation is $\varphi_{tt}/c^2$, so lines can be nodes, persistence needs uniform enthalpy, singular $\mathbf K$ arises at ordinary points, acoustic standing waves give exact examples (node line breaking into alternating sinks, saddles); also degenerate $\mathbf K$ (pairs born at distance $O(\sqrt t)$), curvature, torsion evolution, closure for moving-singularity-driven flows, helical and elliptically conical stagnation-point flow, exact Navier-Stokes example: saddle, centre lines split into four points per period.

Influence of Droplet Morphology on Fluid Dynamics and Impact Erosion of Wind Turbine Blades

Hao Hao, Antonis Sergis, Maria N. Charalambides, Alex M. K. P. Taylor, Yannis Hardalupas

The impact of droplets, such as rain and sea sprays, on wind turbine blade surfaces can cause severe damage through leading-edge erosion (LEE). Current industrial practice has largely relied on empirical fatigue models to predict damage progression and guide maintenance. However, an important limitation of existing models is the assumption on ideal impact conditions, including perfectly spherical droplet and normal incidence, which rarely occur in reality. This study develops a fluid-structure interaction (FSI) model that couples incompressible multiphase flow simulations with finite element (FE) analysis of blade materials. Using this framework, we investigate the impact dynamics of droplets with arbitrary shapes and orientations. The results reveal a positive correlation between the peak impact pressure, the influential area at the pressure peak, and the peak impact force. In contrast, the time of peak force scales inversely due to momentum conservation. From these findings, we propose a $\textit{universal scaling law}$ that relates all key impact loadings to the simple parameter of the droplet $\textit{projection area}$, which can be determined solely from the initial droplet geometry and orientation. This law provides a predictive framework for impact loading dynamics of non-ideal shapes, offering new insights into the mechanisms of LEE and improving the accuracy of erosion predictions for wind turbine blades.

Fractal forcing of turbulence by settling particles

Simone Tandurella, Alessandro Chiarini, Marco Edoardo Rosti

The settling of particles in a fluid medium can trigger complex collective dynamics, dramatically affecting the distribution of energy across flow scales. In this work we present the first fully-resolved observations of the turbulence induced by large particle structures, also known as cluster-induced-turbulence (CIT), obtained through particle-resolved direct numerical simulations at high volume fractions and density ratios in large domains. We find that particles arrange in macroscopic fractal structures, exciting turbulent fluctuations at scales much larger than the individual constituents. By virtue of their fractal nature, these clusters force the continuous-phase in a multiscale, self-similar way. We further show that these cluster-driven, vertically decorrelated fluctuations at large scales coexist with, but are fundamentally distinct from, the established particle-driven high-wavenumber scaling. Beyond unveiling a different form of clustering in particle-settling, these results highlight a naturally arising, self-assembling path to fractal-generated turbulence.

Sangwon Park, Vinod Ramakrishnan, Arturo Machado Burgos, Andres Goza, Kathryn H. Matlack

Phononic materials (PMs) have emerged as a promising candidate to passively and beneficially interact with unsteady flows. However, their fluid-structure interaction (FSI) with separated flows exhibiting sustained periodic vortex shedding remains completely unexplored. In this study, we use a high-fidelity immersed boundary method to investigate the strongly coupled, nonlinear FSI dynamics of vortex shedding from a fully separated flow over a flat plate, with an embedded PM subsurface (PSub) modeled as a grounded diatomic chain driving a compliant section on the flat plate. We probe the dependence of the coupled system dynamics on two parameters: (1) the PSub chordwise position, which sets its spatial relationship to the flow structure, and (2) the rate of growth of the resonant displacement amplitude of a PSub, a behavioral parameter identified as a key driving parameter in PSub interaction with latent vortex shedding in prior work. Simulation results show that these two parameters drive clear and systematic changes to the limit-cycle vortex shedding. The PSub chordwise position determines the limit cycle PM-FSI dynamics, which can be classified into four distinct regimes as a function of position, ranging from substantial amplification to strong attenuation of lift and drag oscillation amplitudes in the limit cycle. Results reveal a configuration that reduces lift oscillation amplitude by over 30% and drag oscillation amplitude by over 50% with less than 0.5% change in mean lift and drag. Displacement amplitude envelope primarily governs transient responsiveness toward the limit cycle, where its influence also depends on the chordwise position. We analyze characteristic frequencies, the phase relationship between PSub displacement and lift, and unsteady and mean flow fields of the vortex-shedding process to explain mechanisms by which the PM modifies the flow.

Yielding-dependent Taylor dispersion in electrokinetic-magnetohydrodynamic Casson microflows

Nanda Poddar, Debabrata Das, Suman Chakraborty

Longitudinal dispersion in a yielding fluid depends on the velocity distribution and on how the flow is maintained. We study a neutral tracer in pressure-assisted electrokinetic and magnetohydrodynamic flow of a Casson fluid through a slit microchannel. The nonlinear stress and velocity equations determine the yielded layers, the unyielded core and the axial flow. Mei's multiple-scale homogenization of the advection-diffusion equation then separates transverse relaxation, mean drift and longitudinal spreading, yielding the Taylor-Aris auxiliary problem and effective diffusivity. An exact pressure-driven solution gives cubic decay of the mean velocity and eighth-power decay of the dispersion enhancement near flow arrest. Under fixed pressure and electrical forcing, increasing the yield parameter from $Y=0$ to $0.7$ moves the optimum Hartmann number from $Ha_p=0.696$ to $2.634$, while the corresponding normalized diffusivity decreases from $D_T^{\mathrm C}=1.454$ to $1.015$. At fixed mean throughput and $Ha_p=1$, the trend reverses: $D_T^{\mathrm C}$ increases from $1.000433$ to $1.002311$. The homogenized energy identity traces this difference to the velocity departure from the sectional mean, including that within the unyielded core. Analytical limiting solutions, direct concentration calculations, numerical convergence studies and a coupled Casson shear test support the reduced-flow predictions. An electrolyte-based dimensional assessment identifies the magnetic optima as theoretical forcing states for representative aqueous microchannels. These results are relevant to predicting longitudinal tracer spreading in microchannels carrying fluids with a yield stress, whether pressure or throughput is controlled.

These advances establish a unified moment framework bridging LBM and moment methods. The framework enables efficient simulations across all Knudsen regimes, overcoming the prohibitive complexity of existing moment methods at high Knudsen numbers. It further provides a common basis for integrating and extending key features of diverse kinetic methods.

Effects of Spanwise Curvature on Hypersonic Boundary Layer Transition in Görtler-driven Flows

Andrew Welter, Ngoc Cuong Nguyen, Nicholas Webber, Mark Gragston, Jaime Peraire, Wesley Harris

The influence of spanwise convex curvature on hypersonic boundary-layer transition over a streamwise-concave surface is investigated using direct numerical simulation and Mach 7 experiments. Smooth doubly curved geometries with fixed streamwise concavity and increasing spanwise convex curvature isolate the effects of three-dimensional wall curvature on a centrifugally unstable boundary layer. Increasing spanwise curvature delays transition while preserving a Gortler-dominated route to breakdown. As spanwise curvature increases, the boundary layer thins, reducing the effective Görtler number and weakening centrifugal amplification of streamwise vortices. Consequently, growth of the primary Görtler instability is reduced, vortical structures remain closer to the wall, and secondary instability and turbulent breakdown shift downstream. Spectral proper orthogonal decomposition confirms that low-frequency vortical dynamics remain dominant in controlling transition, with sinuous secondary instability of Görtler vortices providing the principal route to breakdown. Meanwhile, boundary-layer thinning modifies acoustic instability dynamics: the second Mack mode shifts to higher frequencies and higher-frequency supersonic disturbances become increasingly prominent near breakdown as spanwise curvature increases. These acoustic disturbances remain subdominant and do not replace the Görtler-sinuous mechanism as the primary transition pathway. Numerical and experimental results exhibit consistent trends in transition location, thermal-streak organisation and disturbance evolution, supporting a common physical interpretation. These findings demonstrate that spanwise convex curvature stabilises hypersonic Görtler-dominated boundary layers, primarily by suppressing centrifugal amplification and delaying secondary instability, while leaving the transition pathway qualitatively unchanged.

Scaling laws for contamination traversal times during vacuum breaks in superconducting accelerators

Yinghe Qi, Qutadah Rababah, Wei Guo

A sudden loss of vacuum in a superconducting radio-frequency (SRF) accelerator drives warm gas into the evacuated beamline, where it propagates through interconnected SRF cavities cooled by liquid helium, causing surface contamination, intense heat deposition, and dangerous pressure buildup. Yet predicting how such a failure propagates through realistic multicell cavities has remained difficult because compressible gas dynamics, condensation and cryogenic heat transfer are strongly coupled. Here we develop and experimentally validate a multidimensional computational framework that resolves these processes, and apply it to a full-scale nine-cell SRF cavity. We find that the repeated cell geometry produces staircase-like gas-front propagation, as impingement, recirculation and localized cryopumping strongly retard the bulk flow within each cell. Across a broad range of breach sizes, the cavity traversal time follows a robust power-law scaling with leakage area, providing quantitative guidance for the response time of beamline isolation and protection systems. The simulations also reveal a geometry-confined Mach disk whose position and size track the advancing gas front, suggesting that its acoustic emission could potentially enable rapid, non-invasive detection and localization of vacuum failures. These findings pave the way for a new generation of predictive diagnostics and protection strategies to safeguard large-scale superconducting accelerators.

A GENERIC-Informed Neural Network for Generative Rheology

Imanol García de Beristain, David Nieto Simavilla, Marco Ellero

We present GINNGER (GENERIC-Informed Neural Network for GEnerative Rheology), a framework for discovering thermodynamically admissible constitutive equations from microstructural flow data. Unlike existing approaches that learn constitutive operators, GINNGER reformulates constitutive discovery as the learning of the underlying thermodynamic generators from which constitutive equations emerge. Embedding the GENERIC formalism into the learning process restricts the search to constitutive models that satisfy the first and second laws of thermodynamics by construction, eliminating the need to prescribe phenomenological constitutive equations a priori. A second conceptual advance is a spectral formulation of constitutive discovery in the intrinsic eigenspace of the polymer conformation tensor. This provides a kinematics-independent thermodynamic state space in which the thermodynamic generators depend only on the instantaneous microstructural state, while flow kinematics acts solely as an external driver transporting the system through this space. Consequently, data from shear, extensional, and mixed flows contribute to the discovery of a single constitutive model that generalizes across deformation protocols and flow geometries. By learning thermodynamic generators rather than high-dimensional constitutive operators, GINNGER substantially reduces the complexity of the discovery problem while preserving thermodynamic consistency, material objectivity, and physical interpretability by construction. The framework is validated using high-fidelity simulations, accurately reconstructing constitutive behavior and predicting complex flow fields across previously unseen geometries. GINNGER establishes a new paradigm for constitutive discovery by combining generative, spectral, and thermodynamically grounded learning within a unified framework for data-driven modeling of complex fluids.

Transient growth and Counter-propagating Rossby Waves in a sloped Eady problem

J. Yin, N. Harnik, E. Heifetz, J. Mak

A mechanistic interpretation for baroclinic normal mode instability with exponential growth is the interaction arising from Counter-propagating Rossby Waves (CRWs), where pairs of CRWs phase-lock and interfere constructively. On the other hand, an arbitrary initial condition can lead to finite transient growth as long as there is constructive interference. The present work investigates the characteristics of finite time transient instabilities and its description in terms of CRWs in a baroclinic system with asymmetric interaction, in contrast to usual settings where there is an inherent symmetry that leads to equal and opposite CRWs. A sloped Eady problem on a $f$-plane is considered here, which has the benefit that there are only two CRWs in the system, and the presence of a bottom topographic slope can be formulated in a such a way that only one of the CRW is affected and controlled by a single parameter representing the asymmetry, via modifying the bottom background potential vorticity gradient. We derive conditions where the instantaneous growths have key CRW properties that are independent of the asymmetry parameter for a reasonably general family of norms, and that the instability characteristic in the total energy norm is entirely independent of the asymmetry parameter. For finite time transient growth, the optimal initial condition is in some sense counter to that of the exponential growing normal mode: where the bottom slope is such that it is strengthening the bottom CRW, the normal mode has a larger bottom CRW, but the finite time optimal initial condition wants the bottom CRW to be weak instead, and vice-versa. While the results presented here are for the sloped Eady problem, the same results were also found for an analogous Phillips problem, and we speculate the instability characteristics may have origins in the underlying symmetries that are common to those two problems.

The physics-informed neural network (PINN) faces significant challenges when solving compressible multi-medium flows, including the inability to locate interface positions, poor approximation of discontinuities, and difficulty in handling high-pressure-ratio problems between media with large stiffness differences. To address these issues, we propose PINN with Learnable and Physically Consistent Domain Decomposition (PINN-LPCDD), a framework that incorporates the sharp interface method (SIM) into PINNs for solving compressible multi-medium flows. Firstly, we design separate sub-networks for each medium with learnable parameters to automatically learn interface positions, so that the domain decomposition is physically consistent, and the contact discontinuity at the interface is naturally sharp. Secondly, the Euler equations with artificial viscosity are solved to enhance robustness when handling problems involving shock waves. Lastly, the scaling invariance of the Euler equations is exploited to facilitate the solution of high pressure ratio problems. Numerical experiments on air-air, air-helium, air-water, and air-R22 problems demonstrate the acceptable accuracy of the proposed method in simulating multi-medium flows.
