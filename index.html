Automated Discovery of Physically Interpretable Subgrid Length Scales for Detached Eddy Simulation via Agentic AI

Jiawei Chen, Xiang I. A. Yang, Ping He

Traditional turbulence-model development relies heavily on expert knowledge and is time-consuming. This study develops an agentic AI framework, DEScover (automated Discovery of DES closures), to explore subgrid length scales for detached eddy simulation (DES). The user provides only a modeling idea: the length scale is the grid width normal to the local vortex axis. Four large language model (LLM) agents then propose, review, implement, and evaluate candidate formulations and generate scripts for CFD simulations. The Discovery Agent infers the vortex axis from various vortex-identification methods. The candidates are tested on four cases spanning canonical and engineering flows: a flat plate, a backward-facing step, a free shear layer, and an iced airfoil. For all test cases, the best candidate consistently outperforms the standard length scale based on the maximum cell size. Its accuracy is similar to, or slightly better than, that of the expert-designed shear-layer-adapted length scale. Each agentic discovery run takes about 25 minutes on average, excluding the CFD simulations. These results show that LLM-based reasoning can significantly accelerate the discovery of physically interpretable turbulence models and reduce reliance on manual expert formulation.

Assisted Turbulence Modeling via Data Assimilation and Machine Learning for Turbomachinery Flows

Tom Moussie, Pierric Joseph, Marcello Meldi

This work presents the development and assessment of a hybrid framework coupling numerical simulation, experimental samples, data assimilation (DA) and machine learning (ML) to investigate the flow within an axial compressor stage. The methodology first implements a Multi-Fidelity Ensemble Kalman Filter (MFEnKF) which integrates experimental PIV data with a two-fidelity numerical simulation setup, combining steady-state RANS on a fixed grid with unsteady hybrid RANS/LES simulations on a moving grid. This data assimilation step successfully reduces discrepancies between experiments and CFD realizations. Besides, it provides additional high fidelity samples when compared with the available PIV data. This enriched database is used to train machine learning (ML) tools, with the objective to obtain black-box forcing terms to be included in the discretized form of the Navier-Stokes equations. These models are expected to preserve the accuracy of the DA results while not longer requiring the input of experimental data. The neural network architecture used here maps local physical features such as the velocity, the velocity gradients and the turbulent viscosity, to obtain the volume forcing terms. The analysis of the performance of such ML models shows that global accuracy is preserved but localized increased errors in specific regions of the compressor, such as close to the blade tips, can exceed those observed from the baseline RANS simulations. To resolve this issue, a Mixture of Experts (MoE) method is employed, which classifies and combines the trained ML tools for different flow regions. The discrepancy with experimental data of this method is systematically lower than the one observed for the baseline RANS solution and very similar to the one obtained with the MFEnKF.

Synthetic Dataset Generation, Physical Consistency and Active Learning for Shape-Informed Surrogate Modeling of Hemodynamics

Francesco Romor, Richard Schussnig, Charles A. Taylor, Gerhard A. Holzapfel, Alfonso Caiazzo

Model order reduction has the potential to enable real-time patient-specific assessment of cardiovascular flows. However, it still faces significant challenges stemming from the geometric variability of vascular anatomies. Building upon the SynthAorta dataset (Bosnjak et al., IEEE Trans. Med. Imag. 45(1), 2026) of physiological aortic geometries, we generate an extensive dataset of physiologically consistent computational fluid dynamics solutions through an automatic boundary-condition adjustment procedure. A common reference geometry allows all patient-specific anatomies to be represented by displacement fields, providing point-to-point correspondence without the need for geometric registration. Based on this dataset, we develop Graph U-Net and Transformer U-Net surrogate models to predict velocity and pressure fields at peak systole. We further investigate two strategies to enhance data efficiency and prediction accuracy: active learning and differentiable physical consistency constraints based on the finite element residual. The results show that active learning is strongly regime-dependent: while it offers little benefit over random sampling during early training, it improves performance during fine-tuning through gradient- and subspace-based acquisition strategies. Similarly, physical consistency constraints prove to be substantially more effective as a fine-tuning objective than as a regularizer applied throughout training. Finally, out-of-distribution studies demonstrate that the primary limitation of surrogate models lies in the geometric support of the underlying parametrization rather than in the neural network architectures themselves. The proposed framework combines synthetic dataset generation, geometry-aware surrogate modeling, physics-informed optimization, and active learning into a scalable workflow for inter-patient hemodynamic prediction.

DiffVortex: A quantum-inspired fast solver for incompressible flows

Yu Jun Loo, Silas Alben, Shravan Veerapaneni

Matrix-product-state (MPS) and quantized tensor-train (QTT) representations offer a promising framework for computational fluid dynamics, with logarithmic run time and memory scaling in mesh size when bond dimensions remain bounded. Existing MPS-based solvers are Eulerian: they compress velocity fields and enforce immersed-body boundary conditions through static masks or body-fitted coordinates. Moving or flexible bodies complicate this approach: deformation requires updating MPS masks every time step, while filamentary vorticity in high-Reynolds-number flows may resist low-rank compression. We propose a complementary approach: compress the Biot--Savart kernel rather than the flow state, evolve vorticity using Lagrangian vortex particles, and represent immersed bodies by bound vortex sheets on Lagrangian surface points. Our main theoretical result is a lifting theorem: QTT rank $r$ of the kernel implies matrix-product-operator (MPO) rank at most $2^d r$ for the corresponding convolution operator in $d$ dimensions, independent of grid resolution. This bound is sharp for our interleaved ordering. With quantics tensor cross interpolation, the moment-to-local stage of a single-level fast multipole method becomes a precomputed tensor-network contraction with quasi-linear complexity, retaining Lagrangian flexibility for immersed bodies. Our PyTorch implementation (\texttt{DiffVortex}) is end-to-end differentiable, runs on GPUs, and handles $6.7\times10^{7}$ particles on a single device. We validate against reference vortex-method results for an impulsively started cylinder and exact and reference solutions for inviscid elliptical vortices. We also demonstrate two-dimensional flows past rigidly moving, multiple, and deforming bodies, including gradients propagated through the full time integration.

Swarmlet: a GPU-resident reformulated vortex particle method for coupled multirotor aerodynamics and flight dynamics

Wyatt James Horne

We present Swarmlet, a GPU solver for the unsteady aerodynamics and flight dynamics of one or many multirotor vehicles. The wake is a meshless, evolving-core vortex particle field in the reformulated (rVPM) large-eddy formulation, coupled to rotor models of three fidelities, six-degree-of-freedom vehicles and flight control. Particles carry a smooth core of compact support whose field outside four core radii is exactly the singular Biot-Savart field, so the multipole far field of the treecode carries no regularization error. The subfilter closure is a dynamic eddy viscosity on a fixed filter width, applied by core spreading with a Lagrangian exchange of its local excess under an explicit stability bound, and the strengths are relaxed by projection; global corrections make both the exchange and the relaxation conserve total vorticity and linear impulse. All rotor models shed connected bound circulation, the disks along the arcs swept in each step however long, so the wake receives the lift impulse. The solver agrees with direct summation to $5\times 10^{-7}$ and converges at third order in time. Without calibration, an actuator-line model that includes its own near sheet predicts the hover thrust of a DJI 9443 propeller within 2%. With calibrated section polars it reproduces a Crazyflie propeller's thrust and torque within 3.2% at three speeds and the near wake of a hovering quadrotor with relative $L^2$ differences of 0.08-0.21 against particle image velocimetry, while the wake keeps the delivered impulse. A closed-loop formation of 100 quadrotors, run at a coarser setting on one GPU, illustrates the coupled method at swarm scale.

Neural Operator Splitting for Fluid Dynamics in Complex Geometry

Jianxiang Li, Xi Deng, Hong Chul Nam, Anima Anandkumar

Solving dynamical systems in complex and diverse geometries remains challenging for both numerical solvers and learning-based surrogate models. We propose Neural Operator Splitting, a framework for learning fluid dynamics while preserving the physical structure of fractional-step numerical schemes. Instead of learning the full-step evolution monolithically, we decompose each time step into physically interpretable steps, and learn each operator separately using intermediate states from the underlying numerical solver. Guided by the linearity properties of the fractional steps, we introduce a conditional-linear Fourier neural operator (CL-FNO) whose linear state mappings are modulated by nonlinear conditioning on inputs such as boundary geometry. We further introduce two complementary strategies, symmetry sampling and corrective cycling, to mitigate error accumulation during autoregressive rollout. Experiments on 2D and 3D fluid flows with varying geometries and initial conditions show that our full model reduces mean relative $L^2$ rollout error by 68\% in 2D and 40\% in 3D compared with a single full-step neural operator. At comparable error levels, neural-operator inference is approximately $10^3$ times faster per step than the Monte Carlo solver.

Monocular 3D bubble reconstruction: DNS-informed ray tracing and deep learning

Chaitanya S. Nayak, Faizaan Mohammed, Vivek Kumar, Shivam Prajapati, Suhas S. Jain, Cyrus K. Aidun

Bubble size, depth, and spatial distribution govern transport and uniformity in multiphase flows. A single shadowgraphic view cannot separate bubble diameter from focal-plane displacement, as image signatures depend on both, while multicamera systems add optical access, calibration, and cost. We develop a single-camera physics-guided framework combining direct numerical simulation (DNS), validated Monte Carlo ray tracing, preprocessing, and focus-conditioned multitask deep learning to recover three-dimensional bubble position and physical-equivalent diameter from two-dimensional shadowgraphs. DNS-resolved bubble label images rendered at multiple focal-plane settings. The ray tracer reproduces shadowgraphic signatures with errors below $3\%$ (intensity), $1\%$ (apparent size), $15\%$ (blur), $6\%$ (numerical aperture), and $4\%$ (edge-spread-function shape), running $\sim30\times$ faster than the CPU implementation. The dataset has $900$ frames and $87{,}728$ labeled instances, partitioned by DNS timestamp to prevent leakage and expanded to over $10^{5}$ crops by broad-depth controlled-density rendering. A multitask network predicts signed depth and diameter from crops and focal-plane coordinates, achieving $\sim97.6\%$ normalized depth accuracy on held-out data; a closed-loop DNS-to-image-to-reconstruction test of $180$ bubbles gives diameter error below $9.71\%$ ($\sim90.29\%$ accuracy). On experimental images at four radial planes ($142$--$971$ bubbles each), PDF overlap is $68\%$--$92\%$ and log-PDF correlation is $0.72$--$0.87$. At the focal reference plane ($11{,}679$ bubbles), overlap is $\sim86\%$, correlation is $0.93$, and full-distribution, $D_{99.8}$, and composite errors are below $7.7\%$, increasing with focal-plane distance and defining a recoverability limit. Without stereoscopic hardware, the framework offers a scalable diagnostic for dense bubbly flows.

Predicting trigger in streak breakdown via Picard-Chebyshev approach in plane-Poiseuille flow

Nobutaka Taniguchi, Aiko Yakeno

The evolution of finite-amplitude disturbances is critical in the prediction of laminar-to-turbulent transition. To analyze the transient growth of disturbances, Pralits et al. (J. Fluid Mechanics, 2015) proposed a weakly nonlinear optimal perturbation (WNLOP) analysis that accounts for the effect of mean-flow distortion on the fundamental wave. The present study formulates a WNLOP analysis via Picard--Chebyshev iteration for compressible parallel flows to predict streak breakdown. The formulation incorporates the non-modal amplification of disturbances and their nonlinear interactions for polychromatic waves, considering all the possible nonlinear interactions starting from a finite set of initial wavenumber components. The nonlinear interaction is tracked as a monotonically increasing set of wavenumber components; specifically, the first-order approximation recovers the linear disturbance equation, the third-order approximation corresponds to the amplitude equation of weakly nonlinear theory, and further iterations systematically approach DNS results in the weakly nonlinear regime. By applying the proposed formulation to a subcritical plane Poiseuille flow at Mach number $\mathrm{M}=0.3$ and Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}=3000$, the proposed formulation demonstrates high predictive accuracy in comparison with DNS calculations and succeeds in identifying the optimal perturbation restricted to a planar wave that causes streak breakdown in full nonlinear simulation. The proposed method predicts the nonlinear interaction between the streak and the planar wave, which generates an oblique wave and subsequent harmonic waves characterized by an alternating streamwise vortex structure, whose amplitude determines whether further nonlinear interactions are induced to trigger the turbulent transition.

CuAD: Source-to-Source Automatic Differentiation of CUDA Kernels for Discrete Adjoints in Computational Fluid Dynamics

Seunghyun Joo

Discrete adjoint methods give the sensitivities that aerodynamic design and data assimilation require, but they remain hard to obtain for flow solvers that run on graphics processors. This paper presents CuAD, a source-to-source automatic differentiation tool that reads CUDA kernels and writes their tangent and adjoint versions as ordinary CUDA source. Its reverse mode is organised around the GPU thread. It adopts the convention of CUDA kernels that inputs and outputs do not alias, recomputes intermediate values instead of storing them, differentiates pure accumulations without a tape and keeps any remaining tape in a fixed thread-local array. We evaluate CuAD on four kernels from simple flow models and on a two-dimensional Euler solver. All generated derivatives pass dot-product tests to round-off, and the adjoint kernels cost two to six times the primal kernels. On a Biot-Savart summation compiled by one compiler, the CuAD adjoint costs about four times the primal at every size, as does Enzyme when the source declares the absence of aliasing with __restrict__. On the unchanged source Enzyme caches the loop in the device heap and costs 9 to 240 times the primal, while the heap tape of Clad costs 60 to 4400 times the primal and fails beyond 8192 vortices. Exact Jacobians assembled from the generated tangents also matter for accuracy. In single precision, the adjoint gradient of a finite-difference Jacobian reaches errors of 45 to 75 per cent on a stiff Burgers problem and of $5\times10^{-4}$ to $4\times10^{-2}$ on a transonic airfoil, whereas the generated Jacobian keeps them below $5\times10^{-5}$ and $3\times10^{-6}$. The applications include the shape sensitivities of lift and drag of a transonic NACA 0012, for which the residual, the boundary conditions and the wall force are differentiated by CuAD and the adjoint agrees with the tangent to $10^{-13}$.

Fourier-Feature-Embedded Complex-Valued Physics-Informed Convolutional Residual Networks for Sparse and Gappy Noisy Flow-Field Reconstruction

Biswanath Barman, Rajendra K. Ray

Reconstructing flow fields from sparse and noisy measurements becomes particularly challenging when data are completely absent from dynamically critical regions, such as the vortex-formation region of a wake. Conventional interpolation and numerical reconstruction methods generally require sufficient spatial or boundary information, while physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) can also face difficulties when sparse and noisy observations provide little or no supervision in the region of interest. To address this gap, we propose a Fourier-feature-embedded complex-valued physics-informed convolutional residual network (FCV-PICRN) for sparse, gappy, and noisy flow-field reconstruction. The framework integrates Fourier features for oscillatory and multiscale structures, complex-valued representations for coupled amplitude--phase learning, and convolutional residual blocks constrained by the Navier--Stokes equations. The method is first verified using the Taylor--Green vortex at $Re=100$ and then evaluated for the cylinder wake at $Re=100$. Three severe missing-data configurations are imposed in the dynamically sensitive wake: a large hole, a large truncation, and a plus-shaped gap extending in both streamwise and transverse directions. These gaps are further combined with additive Gaussian and multiplicative speckle noise.
