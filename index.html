Spatial symmetry invariance of three-dimensional Navier-Stokes turbulence

Shijun Liao

Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS) of the deterministic Navier-Stokes (NS) equations is widely used to provide benchmark solutions of turbulent flow, but its accuracy and reliability have not been rigorously inspected from a mathematical viewpoint. Here we prove a spatial symmetry invariance theorem (SSIT) of three-dimensional NS equations whereby an exact solution of deterministic NS equations under periodic boundary conditions maintains the same spatial symmetry as its symmetric initial flow, forever. SSIT provides an absolute benchmark test for computational fluid dynamics (CFD). It is shown herein that traditional DNS of NS turbulence quickly violates SSIT, revealing that the spatiotemporal trajectory of NS turbulence predicted by DNS soon becomes badly polluted by numerical noise. Importantly, it is found that even the statistical results of DNS might be unstable, i.e., exhibit sensitivity dependence on the initial flow conditions. In other words, the loss of spatial symmetry in traditional DNS of NS turbulence is not a physical instability but instead a numerical artifact caused by the chaotic nature of the flow. Using the SSIT, it is straightforward to deduce several key properties of NS turbulence, including `ultra-chaos', the so-called `noise-expansion cascade', `permanent memory of symmetric initial flow conditions', and so on. Note that permanent memory of symmetric initial flow conditions suggests that NS turbulence governed by deterministic NS equations subject to symmetric initial flow should have no `mixing', corresponding to non-ergodicity, although ergodicity is one of the most fundamental assumptions of turbulence theory.

Generative AI in Turbulence: Foundations, Applications, Validations and Benchmarks

Luca Biferale, Michele Buzzicotti

Generative AI offers new approaches to turbulence problems in which incomplete observations or unresolved d.o.f. admit multiple physically plausible solutions. Potential applications include stochastic closures, super-resolution, data assimilation, uncertainty quantification, and the generation of Eulerian and Lagrangian flow ensembles. However, turbulence is not a conventional image-generation problem. Its multiscale dynamics, intermittency, extreme events, rough velocity fields and intrinsically stochastic transport make visual realism, pointwise error, and low-order spectral agreement insufficient measures of physical fidelity. We examine where generative AI provide novel capabilities with emphasis on diffusion models, score-based methods, and related probability-transport formulations. We discuss their use in unconditional generation, probabilistic reconstruction, reduced-order modeling, temporal evolution, and mesh-free representations. We argue that validation must address turbulence properties: energy transfer, structure functions, anomalous scaling, pdf tails, coherent structures, conservation laws, temporal consistency, ensemble diversity and generalization. Finally, we discuss potential limitations in validation, baseline, data availability and reproducibility, and the need for a benchmark ecosystem combining open data, standardized challenges, hidden tests, physics-aware metrics, and documented failures. This paper serves as a bridge between two communities. For AI practitioners, we provide a fair and accessible introduction to turbulence, essential for appreciating the challenges posed by the field and for assessing the physical relevance of generative models. For turbulent practitioners, we provide an introduction to the key technological aspects of Generative AI, clarifying what these tools actually do, and where their potential and current limitations lie.

The flow control strategy is capable of simultaneously achieving multiple control objectives, including reducing drag, suppressing lift, eliminating vortex shedding, and decreasing energy consumption. These findings highlight the potential of optimizing deep reinforcement learning frameworks to achieve more adaptive flow control strategies for various flow scenarios.

From Stochastic Transport Models to High-Dimensional Bayesian Inference

Dan Crisan, Oana Lang

The Stochastic Transport in Upper Ocean Dynamics project introduced a new paradigm for representing uncertainty in geophysical fluid dynamics through stochastic transport models calibrated from data. This paper provides a synthesis of the principal theoretical and data assimilation developments achieved as part of the project. The central theme of the paper is the transformation of stochastic transport models from a modelling paradigm into a mathematically rigorous framework equipped with analytical, variational, calibration and Bayesian data assimilation methodologies suitable for geophysical fluid dynamics. We describe the development of the theory of stochastic transport equations, including well-posedness, regularity and approximation results, together with their geometric foundations based on stochastic variational principles, rough paths and fractional transport models. We further review methodologies for calibrating stochastic transport structures from observational and high-resolution simulation data, ranging from reduced-order stochastic models to modern generative approaches. Finally, we discuss Bayesian inference and high-dimensional data assimilation techniques developed for stochastic transport models, including particle filter methodologies and machine learning uncertainty quantification. Taken together, these developments establish a coherent framework linking stochastic transport modelling, rigorous analysis, calibration and Bayesian inference, thereby providing a foundation for uncertainty quantification and forecasting in geophysical fluid dynamics.

Unlike conventional methods, our approach successfully achieves complete vortex shedding suppression, a maximum drag reduction of 32.2%, and eliminates periodic oscillations, stabilizing the flow. These results demonstrate the efficacy of state-enhanced RL in controlling highly slender geometries.

Solving and Learning Navier-Stokes Dynamics with the Neural Basis Method

Yuhe Wang, Zhu Wang, Min Wang

Physics-informed machine learning has become a major direction in predictive modeling of fluid dynamics, combining governing physics with learned representations. Yet prevailing formulations neither guarantee that small PDE-residual losses imply small solution errors nor scale effectively with increasing problem size. Here we present the neural basis method (NBM) as a new approach for Navier--Stokes dynamics. NBM is built on a ``representation-then-projection'' principle: fixed neural bases define the representation space, while projection of the governing equations determines the expansion coefficients. The same bases define common coordinates for the parametric solution manifold, recasting operator learning as a parameter-to-coefficient mapping and enabling scalability by removing explicit spatial dependence. For incompressible flow, NBM yields a velocity-only formulation whose residual provides an a posteriori velocity-error estimate, establishing a certified, data-free training objective for coefficient learning. Theoretical analysis and numerical experiments demonstrate the accuracy, efficiency, generalization, and scalability of NBM, with substantial improvements over baselines.

Physics-Constrained Symbolic Regression for Mean-Velocity and Mixing-Length Scaling in Zero-Pressure-Gradient Turbulent Boundary Layers

Xianglin Shan, Weiwei Zhang

Developing interpretable full-profile relations for mean velocity and effective mixing length remains challenging because the near-wall, overlap, and outer regions exhibit distinct scaling behaviors. In this paper, physics-constrained symbolic regression is used to construct analytical relations for incompressible zero-pressure-gradient turbulent boundary layers. Classical logarithmic and Prandtl mixing-length scalings provide the starting points, while the regression identifies near-wall transition functions under asymptotic constraints and Reynolds-number-dependent outer corrections. The resulting expressions retain the prescribed leading-order near-wall exponents, reproduce the principal overlap-region behavior, and describe finite-Reynolds-number variations. The mean-velocity relation agrees well with 35 profiles from direct numerical simulations, large-eddy simulations, and experiments spanning friction Reynolds numbers from 252 to 72,526, while the mixing-length relation captures the principal trends in the simulation-derived data. These results illustrate how physical scaling knowledge can guide symbolic regression toward compact, interpretable analytical descriptions of canonical wall turbulence.

Traditional turbulence-model development relies heavily on expert knowledge and is time-consuming. This study develops an agentic AI framework, DEScover (automated Discovery of DES closures), to explore subgrid length scales for detached eddy simulation (DES). The user provides only a modeling idea: the length scale is the grid width normal to the local vortex axis. Four large language model (LLM) agents then propose, review, implement, and evaluate candidate formulations and generate scripts for CFD simulations. Its accuracy is similar to, or slightly better than, that of the expert-designed shear-layer-adapted length scale. Each agentic discovery run takes about 25 minutes on average, excluding the CFD simulations. These results show that LLM-based reasoning can significantly accelerate the discovery of physically interpretable turbulence models and reduce reliance on manual expert formulation.

Thermal-fluid field distributions underpin the analysis and operation of energy infrastructure, from nuclear-reactor cooling channels to industrial heat exchangers, but resolving these distributions through computational fluid dynamics requires prohibitively large simulation ensembles. Their complex geometries also demand unstructured meshes that are poorly accommodated by generative models developed for regular grids. Here we introduce FlowMesh, a generative framework that combines permutation-invariant mesh encoding with deterministic latent flow matching and an l1 penalty on the learned transport velocity. FlowMesh generates full-field realizations directly on fixed unstructured meshes without auxiliary grid interpolation or a stochastic denoising chain. Across lid-driven-cavity, pressurized-water-reactor subchannel and heat-exchanger datasets, evaluated over six matched training seeds, FlowMesh achieves macro-averaged probability-density-function coverage of 91.7%-97.0%, exceeding an architecture-controlled latent diffusion model by $11.6$--$45.4$ percentage points. Latent-velocity regularization increases coverage by $3.8$ percentage points for reactor flows and 1.1 points for heat-exchanger flows, while reducing temperature-field Kullback--Leibler divergence by 1.7-2.4-fold. Each full-field realization requires 1.23 \pm 0.15 s, providing a 585--1902-fold wall-clock acceleration over computational fluid dynamics. FlowMesh therefore makes large-scale generative characterization of thermal-fluid states computationally feasible, providing a foundation for data-driven analysis and digital-twin development in fixed-geometry energy systems.

Neural Eigenfluid Bases: Spectral Objectives, Gauge, and Topology

Baiming Zhang, Shiying Xiong, Zhecheng Wang

Reduced-order models simplify fluid simulation by representing complex flows with a small number of spatial modes. Building on Laplacian Eigenfluids, we introduce Neural Eigenfluid Bases (NEB), a neural representation whose basis functions can be evaluated anywhere in space. We enforce incompressibility and impermeable boundaries directly through differential lifts and potential boundary conditions. Geometry-local orthogonalization and Rayleigh minimization then learn an ordered basis. We connect learning in potential and velocity spaces, and derive a spectral-variance relation explaining why these objectives weight approximate modes differently, even though their exact positive spectra agree. Gauge fixing reduces the number of spatial derivatives needed for potential learning, while a Dirichlet energy loss learns independent circulation fields around obstacles. With 1024 modes, both objectives keep velocity errors below 1% in periodic vortex merger. For random flows around obstacles, potential-based learning captures more initial energy and gives lower final errors than numerical Laplacian bases of the same size. The reduced state is about a thousand times smaller than the full reference model. Three-dimensional tests separate spatial reconstruction errors from errors in coefficient evolution. Precomputed Galerkin operators allow millisecond-scale updates when the same basis is reused across simulations. This provides a physically constrained basis for continuous flow reconstruction and efficient reduced-order simulation.

When does boundary-condition uncertainty outweigh thermal and vegetation effects in street canyon flow? A global sensitivity analysis of input uncertainty

Albert Moreno Català, Joan Calafell Sandiumenge, Nikolaj T. Mücke, Jan Mateu Armengol

Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) is increasingly used to resolve wind, heat, and pollution patterns in cities with high fidelity but high computational cost. Thermal effects and vegetation are often neglected despite being important drivers, yet their influence may be masked by variability from other uncertain inputs. This work determines how precisely model parameters and boundary conditions must be known for these effects to govern output uncertainty in the velocity, temperature, and concentration fields of a two-dimensional street canyon. A global Sobol analysis of 24 parameters is made tractable by a surrogate trained on Reynolds-Averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) simulations, combining principal component analysis (PCA) with multilayer perceptrons (MLPs). Two ventilation regimes assess the variance due to thermal and vegetation parameters as uncertainty in all other inputs is progressively reduced. At typical uncertainty levels, turbulence closure constants and inflow turbulence profiles govern variability in all three fields in both regimes. Under weak ventilation, solar forcing becomes the main source of temperature variability once the remaining uncertainties are halved, and of concentration variance once they are reduced to a quarter (about $\pm11\%$ for the dominant closure constant and $\pm12\%$ for the emission rate). In contrast, vegetation contributes significantly at typical uncertainty levels. Under strong ventilation, however, the canopy never overtakes the closure constants, even when the dominant one is known to $\pm5\%$. Spatially, both effects peak at road level and along canyon walls, which influence outdoor exposure and indoor infiltration, suggesting that high-resolution exposure studies should account for them.

These results demonstrate that PINNs can model substantially more realistic self-gravitating turbulent flows than previously explored and provide a promising complementary framework for studying these systems.

Sensitivity-Guided Parameter Estimation of Bubbly Liquid via a Nonlinear Wave Equation and Physics-InformedNeuralNetworks

Takeru Hasegawa, Naoto Mitsume, Shota Deguchi, Mitsuteru Asai, Tetsuya Kanagawa

This study proposes a parameter estimation framework that combines a Korteweg-de Vries-Burgers (KdVB) equation, describing weakly nonlinear wave propagation in a bubbly liquid, with a physics-informed neural network (PINN). The contributions are threefold. First, the PINN reproduces the reference solution of the governing equation in the forward problem, with a near-zero prediction error at the initial time that confirms the initial condition is enforced exactly as intended. Second, prior to inverse estimation, a sensitivity analysis is introduced that quantifies, at both the coefficient and solution levels, how each of eight physical parameters influences the KdVB equation, allowing the expected difficulty of estimating each parameter to be assessed in advance. These results establish a general framework for identifying and estimating the physical parameters governing nonlinear wave propagation in bubbly liquids from observed waveform data.
