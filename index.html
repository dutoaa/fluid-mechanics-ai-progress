Data-free continuous-time physics-informed neural networks for vortex shedding: a consistent pressure across time windows

Mickaël Delcey, Cyril Regan, Masaki Fuchiwaki, Yoann Cheny

Without data, physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) in continuous time have so far failed to develop vortex shedding past a circular cylinder in a domain symmetric about the wake centerline: the training converges to the steady symmetric solution. We obtain shedding at Re=100 with a continuous-time PINN trained over successive time windows, without any reference data and without temporal discretization, the symmetry being broken by an analytical, divergence-free initial condition. The Strouhal number and the mean drag coefficient agree with an OpenFOAM solution computed on the same domain to within 0.5%. The pressure, however, is discontinuous at the junctions between windows, and the lift coefficient is a staircase. We relate this discontinuity to the differential-algebraic structure of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, of index 2 after spatial discretization: a consistent initial condition of each window must satisfy the constraint $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0$ for the velocity, and the hidden constraint $\nabla\cdot\partial_t\mathbf{v}=0$, with $\partial_t\mathbf{v}$ given by the momentum equation, then determines the pressure. Imposing the pressure alone makes the solution continuous but not consistent; our remedy imposes the pressure at the junctions and adds this hidden constraint to the loss. Over five shedding periods, the jumps of the lift coefficient become 20 times smaller.

SPARK: Spectral Preconditioned Analytic Residual Kolmogorov Arnold Networks for incompressible Navier Stokes equations

Dathi Rajasekhar, Thien-Tam Nguyen, Trung Bao Le

We propose Spectral Jet KAN (SJ-KAN) to reduce the cost of differentiation and the network complexity of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) for the Navier-Stokes (NS) equations. The input coordinates are embedded through Chebyshev bases whose spatial derivatives are known in closed form, and all differentiation matrices are computed once for the collocation points before training. A single linear layer compresses the Chebyshev bases into a few latent features, and a single nonlinear Chebyshev Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (CKAN) layer maps these features to the physical variables. Because the basis derivatives are known in advance and the compression is linear, the spatial derivatives in the NS equations are carried through the network analytically by a fixed sequence of matrix products and elementwise operations. Automatic differentiation (AD) is used only in backpropagation, to compute the gradients of the loss with respect to the network weights. We examine three variants of SJ-KAN (JetKAN, JetKAN-F, JetKAN-H), which differ in how the output layer maps the latent features to the physical variables. All variants are tested on the Taylor-Green vortex and on the lid driven cavity at Reynolds numbers Re = 100, 1000, 5000 and 10,000. The results are compared with a KAN that uses AD (PIKAN) and with a standard PINN. The SJ-KAN variants reach comparable or better accuracy than both, with up to 47 times fewer parameters and up to 8.2 times faster training per epoch. SJ-KAN also solves the lid driven cavity flow at Re = 10,000 without any reference data and with fewer than 9,000 trainable parameters. These results show that analytic derivative propagation, a small spectral input layer and a single KAN layer give a faster and simpler solver than methods based on AD, also for flows at high Reynolds numbers.

AI Buckingham: Agentic Discovery of Dimensionless Scaling Laws Across Physical Regimes

Ao Cheng, Lei Zhang, Guowei He

Classical dimensional analysis defines admissible dimensionless representations but does not determine which combinations capture the dominant physical dependence, whether it changes across physical regimes, or when dependent-variable rescaling is required. We present AI Buckingham, an agentic framework for autonomous dimensionless discovery from natural-language descriptions of physical problems and observational data. It couples reasoning and planning by a large language model (LLM) with predefined symbolic and numerical tools. Literature-based knowledge retrieval supports dimensional checking, gradient-based clustering identifies physical regimes, and mutual information provides an objective, reparameterization-invariant criterion for identifying dominant dimensionless groups. Explicit numerical criteria determine the group count and assess simplification. Across ten physical systems spanning fluid dynamics, magnetohydrodynamics, thermal convection and additive manufacturing, AI Buckingham recovers established similarity representations and resolves regime-dependent scalings. For the longstanding challenge of predicting turbulent drag on rough surfaces, it identifies informative reparameterizations of surface statistics that improve equivalent sand-grain roughness estimates for engineering-oriented drag modelling.

Three-Dimensional Wake Reconstruction and Cross-Scale Transfer for Floating Offshore Wind Turbines via Physics-Informed Fourier Neural Operators

Guodan Dong, Qi Qian, Feifei Xue, Jianhua Qin, Chang Xu, Wenzhong Shen

Coupled surge-pitch motions drive unsteady vortex interactions and dynamic wake meandering behind floating offshore wind turbines, complicating three-dimensional wake reconstruction from limited measurements and transfer across turbine scales. Large-eddy simulations coupled with an actuator line model generate fixed and surge-pitch wakes at motion-based Strouhal numbers $St=fD/U_\infty$ of 0.2-0.6 (where $f$, $D$, and $U_\infty$ are the motion frequency, rotor diameter, and inflow velocity) for the National Renewable Energy Laboratory 5 MW ($D=126$ m) and International Energy Agency 22 MW ($D=284$ m) turbines. A physics-informed Fourier neural operator framework combines four-dimensional spectral convolutions with incompressible continuity and rotor-forced momentum-residual regularization to reconstruct three-dimensional unsteady wakes from two observation planes. Trained on the 5 MW fixed and $St=0.2$-$0.5$ cases, the framework recovers near-wake double-peak deficits, motion-driven wake expansion, and coherent vortices through $St=0.6$ (mean wake-center and half-width errors within 1.3% and 1.0% of $D$), while 10% planar sampling preserves near-dense accuracy (relative $L_2$ error 0.0689 versus 0.0684). Applying transfer learning from the 5 MW to the 22 MW turbine lowers the three-dimensional streamwise-velocity error at $St=0.6$ by 36% relative to zero-shot inference (0.1066 to 0.0682), halves scratch-training error with one fixed case (0.0835 versus 0.1693), and restores wake-center tracking and motion-locked harmonics ($St$-$3St$). Large-scale motion-induced dynamics transfer cleanly across turbine scales, whereas fine-scale vortex breakdown remains damped. The framework shows potential for three-dimensional wake reconstruction and downstream-inflow assessment in floating wind farms.

Physics-informed neural networks for strong normal shocks in kinetic relaxation models and the hard-sphere Boltzmann equation

Ehsan Roohi

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) for kinetic equations have mostly been applied near equilibrium and judged by density, velocity and temperature. Here a network trained on the kinetic equation alone, with no reference data in the loss, solves steady normal shock waves and is assessed in eight fields: density, velocity, temperature, heat flux, stress, a third-order moment and two fourth-order moments. The network corrects a mixture of the two end-state Maxwellians by an unbounded, signed factor with exact inflow; the loss, the residual in three norms with conservation terms, is minimised by Adam and a self-scaled quasi-Newton method with continuation in the Mach number. For the hard-sphere Boltzmann equation at Mach 8, in a comparison made once after reference-free tests, the largest relative $L^2$ difference from direct simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) over the eight fields is 0.19 per cent; at Mach 10 it is 0.34 per cent. The distribution function itself agrees with the reference over six decades in velocity space. For the Bhatnagar--Gross--Krook (BGK) model, a blind prediction at Mach 50 gives density, velocity and temperature to about 0.1 per cent and exposes a loss of accuracy confined to the two fourth-order moments. Its origin is identified as a passive reduced distribution that carries little weight in the loss; with a larger weight on that distribution and a longer optimisation, both moments are within 3.2 per cent of the discrete-velocity solution up to Mach 70 and 7.1 per cent at Mach 100. The Shakhov model is solved to Mach 20 and a Rykov-type model of nitrogen to Mach 15.

Dopamine: A Cartesian grid large-eddy-simulation framework for coastal ocean modelling of free-surface waves

Akshay Patil

We present a Cartesian staggered-grid large-eddy simulation framework for modelling coastal ocean waves using the Dopamine solver. The framework combines a geometric volume-of-fluid method for simulating free-surface wave evolution with one-way-coupled Lagrangian point-particle tracking for heavy and buoyant particles. A ghost-cell immersed-boundary method represents stationary solid boundaries, including the seabed and coastal obstacles, and their interaction with free-surface waves. The framework includes the Vreman and anisotropic minimum-dissipation (AMD) sub-grid-scale models for simulating near-wall turbulence. Comparisons with analytical solutions and established experimental and numerical benchmarks illustrate the framework's ability to reproduce wave dynamics and particle motion. The results provide promising insights into the framework's capabilities for coastal ocean modelling applications focused on investigating small-scale turbulent physics using a low-numerical-dissipation approach. The solver runs on multi-CPU architectures, with two-fluid strong- and weak-scaling efficiencies at 512 ranks relative to 64 ranks of up to 77% and 73%, respectively, and a single-GPGPU build has been verified against the CPU solver.

Energy cascade spectrum in wave turbulence of a two-dimensional quantum fluid

Ying Zhu, Jason Laurie, Giorgio Krstulovic, Sergey Nazarenko

In the two-dimensional (2D) Gross-Pitaevskii model for a Bose gas, the naive dimensional non-equilibrium wave-turbulence power-law prediction for the direct energy cascade coincides with thermal equilibrium and therefore carries no energy flux. We resolve this degeneracy by deriving a logarithmically corrected spectrum that supports a positive, constant energy flux toward small scales. A systematic asymptotic expansion of the wave-kinetic collision integral determines the logarithmic exponent to be 1/3, while the small-scale dissipation scale sets the upper cutoff in the logarithmic correction. Regularization of a marginal infrared divergence at the forcing scale yields an estimate of the spectral prefactor for asymptotically well-separated forcing and dissipation scales. Simulations of the Gross-Pitaevskii and wave-kinetic equations support the predicted spectral shape. Over finite spectral ranges, the correction produces apparent power laws steeper than the dimensional prediction, offering a possible explanation for the steep spectra observed in 2D Bose gas experiments. Our approach provides a systematic framework for constructing wave turbulent cascade spectra when the power-law prediction is degenerate with thermodynamic equilibrium.

BridgeSRDA: Efficient Probabilistic Super-Resolution Data Assimilation with Forecast-Informed Stochastic Interpolants

Aditya Sai Pranith Ayapilla, Yuki Yasuda, Ryo Onishi

High-resolution ensemble data assimilation is computationally demanding because it requires repeated forecasts and updates of many flow states. We introduce BridgeSRDA, a probabilistic super-resolution data assimilation framework that combines low-resolution numerical forecasts with sparse, noisy observations to generate high-resolution analysis windows. A neural network learns forecast-informed stochastic transport, which is sampled to produce an ensemble of reconstructions. Each current analysis initializes the next low-resolution forecast, while the same window provides retrospective reconstructions and short-range nowcasts. We evaluate BridgeSRDA over ten independently initialized trajectories of a three-dimensional quasi-geostrophic flow in a narrow-jet regime different from training. At the training observation spacing, BridgeSRDA approaches the mean and structural reconstruction accuracy of a high-resolution local ensemble transform Kalman filter and reduces the mean absolute error ratio by approximately 32.5% relative to deterministic four-dimensional SRDA. With 30 ensemble members, BridgeSRDA requires approximately 17 s per analysis, compared with 31 s for the filter under the recorded conditions. Ten members reduce the cost to approximately 6.6 s with little change in mean accuracy, though fine-scale accuracy and interval coverage are affected. BridgeSRDA achieves a competitive continuous ranked probability score, but its ensembles are underdispersed: nominal 90% intervals contain approximately 62% of reference values. Boundary spread artifacts and reduced skill under sparser observations also warrant attention. These results demonstrate the potential of forecast-informed stochastic transport for probabilistic data assimilation, while highlighting the need to improve uncertainty calibration and robustness to changing observation networks.

Floquet Decomposition: Floquet Multipliers and Time-Periodic Modes from Period-Sampled Snapshot Data

John Mathew Quinlan

Dynamic mode decomposition (DMD) fits one constant linear operator to a sequence of snapshots. When the dynamics, or the view of them, vary periodically, as in a parametrically excited flow or a rotating structure seen in a fixed plane, no such operator exists, and stability is decided by the Floquet multipliers, the eigenvalues of the map over one period. In the paper that introduced DMD, Schmid observed that snapshots sampled at the period of an oscillatory flow would identify the Floquet matrix. We develop that observation into a method and analyze it. The Floquet decomposition (FD) applies DMD to fixed-rate data subsampled once per period; the ensemble Floquet decomposition (EFD) stacks the snapshots of each period into one column, giving one set of multipliers and phase-resolved modes. For linear time-periodic systems both are exact: FD when the measurement at one instant determines the state, EFD when the measurements over one period do---so EFD succeeds with partial measurements. EFD and Hankel (delay-embedded) DMD use the same data matrix with different shifts between columns. With $p$ samples per period, Hankel-DMD returns each Floquet mode as a carrier with sidebands, a comb of $p$-th roots of its multiplier, which the period shift collapses onto the multiplier. The methods are verified against known multipliers; outside their hypotheses, on nonlinear or noisy data, the estimated moduli are biased and are not decay rates. Measurements of velocity, scalars, and acoustic pressure in a swirl-stabilized combustor illustrate the choice of period, rank, and data scaling.

Stable eigenmodes of the Large-scale circulation in Turbulent Convection

Yin Wang, Pik-Yin Lai, Penger Tong

Turbulent Rayleigh-Bénard convection (RBC) is a canonical system for studying buoyancy-driven turbulence. Its defining feature is a system-size large-scale circulation (LSC), whose dynamics reflect the coupling between coherent motion and turbulent fluctuations. Starting from the Boussinesq Navier-Stokes and temperature equations, we derive three coupled stochastic equations for the LSC angular velocity and its associated dipolar temperature field. The deterministic dynamics contain an unstable no-LSC set and two symmetry-related stable finite-LSC states. Linearization about either finite-LSC state yields one purely relaxational eigenmode and a pair of damped oscillatory eigenmodes whose frequency is set by the LSC turnover rate. Turbulent fluctuations continually excite the latter, providing a dynamical origin for the observed quasi-periodic oscillations. Experiments in a thin-disk cell directly resolve both eigenmodes and show their predicted Rayleigh-number scalings. The same stochastic dynamics also provide a route to rare cessations and reversals. This theory-experiment framework provides a tractable example of how coherent circulation states, intrinsic modes, and fluctuation-driven transitions emerge in turbulent convection, with broader relevance to geophysical and astrophysical flows.

A centered finite-volume method with explicit pressure relaxation for weakly compressible single- and two-phase flows

Shintaro Matsushita, Suhas S. Jain, Andrei G. Fedorov, Tetsuya Suekane

We present a non-dissipative centered finite-volume method for weakly compressible single- and two-phase flows. The method couples kinetic-energy-preserving advection to conservative diffuse-interface regularization and explicit pressure relaxation on structured collocated grids. To control excessive phase-mass outflow when neighboring cells have different intrinsic densities, we construct phase-mass fluxes using harmonic face densities. This connects phase-volume and phase-mass transport without altering the advective kinetic-energy identity, yielding a sufficient positivity condition and an inverse-density transport identity at finite velocity divergence. To retain the benefit of low-dissipation transport in the coupled calculation, a pressure-increment formulation incorporates the existing pressure gradient in the predictor. Compatible cell and face operators give a conditional energy estimate for additional pressure iterations, while fully discrete budgets separate advection, predictor pressure work, and the final correction. In inviscid homogeneous isotropic turbulence on $128^3$ cells, the relative kinetic-energy change remains below $1.2\times10^{-7}$ in magnitude at $t^*=8$ in both single-phase and density-ratio-$1000$ two-phase calculations. Matched upwind calculations retain only $3.60\%$ and $30.72\%$ of their initial energies, respectively. Shear layers, cavities, rising bubbles, and dam breaks demonstrate applicability to incompressible-regime flows. In a three-dimensional dam break, harmonic averaging retains positive phase masses where arithmetic averaging produces negative values. With centered advection, a prescribed subgrid-scale stress supplies the principal dissipation. The local pressure algorithm and collocated finite-volume form support future large-scale multiphase large-eddy simulation on GPUs with adaptive mesh refinement.

Basin-Scale Nonlinear Dynamics of Thermohaline Ocean Circulation. Part I: Theory

Conner W. Lester, Dylan E. McNamara, Till J. W. Wagner

Earth's thermohaline ocean circulation may support multiple equilibria and abrupt transitions. Similar bifurcation structures for different ocean models suggest common underlying dynamics across the model hierarchy. Here we propose a unifying framework from tracer conservation, in which surface fluxes are balanced by dissipative and advective tracer transport. We express this flux balance through a large-scale tracer gradient and a transport efficiency that measures how strongly circulation displaces the tracer field from its no-flow "conductive" state. The flux balance quantifies the transport contributions that sustain each circulation state and give rise to abrupt transitions.
