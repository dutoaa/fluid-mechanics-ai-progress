Reynolds-number-dependent entrance bias in temperature polarization of lab-scale membrane distillation

Soyoung Kim, Tiezheng Tong, Yinuo Noah Yao

Lab-scale membrane distillation (MD) modules are commonly used for membrane characterization and developing empirical heat-transfer correlations. Entrance-induced flow structures can introduce geometry-specific effects into measured temperature polarization (TP) and heat transfer. However, the dependence of these effects on module geometry and flow condition remains unclear. We performed three-dimensional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations of direct contact membrane distillation (DCMD) over inlet Reynolds numbers of $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}=10$-$1000$, focusing on side-chamber length and main-channel height. We discovered that counter-rotating streamwise vortices formed near the main-channel entrance across all geometries, but their downstream persistence increased with $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$. At lower $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$, the vortices mostly decayed before mid-channel, while at higher $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$ they persisted farther downstream and produced pronounced spatial variations in TP. Increasing side-chamber length reduced the secondary-flow kinetic energy entering the main channel, and generally strengthened TP, whereas increasing main-channel height reduced the bulk velocity and streamwise kinetic energy, resulting in a thicker thermal boundary layer and stronger TP. For most geometries, the deviation from the straight-channel baseline changed from stronger TP at lower $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$ to weaker TP at higher $\Re_{\mathrm{in}}$. Entrance effects therefore vary in both magnitude and direction with operating condition and cannot be represented by a constant correction across the Reynolds-number range.

High-fidelity Three-Dimensional Flow Field Reconstruction Driven by Turbulent Fluid Dynamics

Hanlin Kong, Jianan Wei, Yuntian Chen, Yujia Wang, Wenguan Wang

High-fidelity three-dimensional turbulent flow fields are essential for scientific and engineering analyses, yet obtaining them through direct numerical simulation or high-resolution measurements remains expensive. Super-resolution reconstruction from coarse-grained data therefore offers a promising alternative, but remains fundamentally challenging because fine-scale structures are intrinsically three-dimensional and dynamically coupled across scales. Existing data-driven approaches still struggle to recover multiscale volumetric structures and their coherent temporal evolution. To address these limitations, we propose a turbulence-aware framework for time-resolved three-dimensional flow reconstruction. The framework couples a multiscale neural operator, designed to recover volumetric structures, with a state space model to promote temporal consistency in the reconstructed dynamics. In addition, Navier-Stokes residuals are incorporated as soft constraints to regularize the reconstruction toward physically plausible dynamics. A mixture-of-experts mechanism is also introduced to support unsupervised adaptation to unknown coarse-graining. Across evaluations on canonical turbulent systems and atmospheric reanalysis data, the proposed framework reduces reconstruction errors relative to learning-based and classical baselines, while better preserving multiscale spatial structure and coherent temporal evolution. These results validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in reconstructing high-fidelity three-dimensional flow fields from coarse-grained data.

Component-wise outer similarity of turbulent boundary layers with strong adverse-pressure gradient

Yu-Xuan Shi, Mingze Ma, Xi Chen

The adverse-pressure-gradient (APG) effect challenges the classical scaling of turbulent boundary layers (TBLs) over a flat plate, and it remains unclear whether outer-layer similarity holds across a wide range of APG strengths.We assemble a benchmark comprising the present direct numerical simulation of a suction--blowing-induced separation bubble and published profiles, spanning Clauser parameters $β_e=0.04$--$180$ and Reynolds numbers $Re_θ=530$--$19000$. Across this broad $(β_e,Re_θ)$ parameter range, existing APG normalizations do not simultaneously collapse the two mean-velocity defects and four Reynolds-stress components, leaving open whether outer similarity persists. Instead of defining new scalings, we normalize each quantity by its value at a common location $y_s$. Results are shown using $y_s=0.5δ$, beyond the outer stress peaks. For each of the six quantities, the normalized profiles exhibit a common outer shape across the conditions, with collapse emerging for $y/δ\gtrsim0.2$ for the velocity defects and $y/δ\gtrsim0.4$ for the Reynolds stresses. The collapse-error analysis shows that outer self-similarity is retained over a broad range of $y_s$ within the outer region and is not restricted to $y_s=0.5δ$. Furthermore, explicit expressions are provided for the normalized outer profiles. These results support component-wise outer similarity under strong APGs and provide compact references for APG turbulence-model assessment and conditional profile reconstruction.

AI Meets Turbulence Modeling: Complex Systems Perspective

Sharath S. Girimaji

Despite widespread enthusiasm, applying artificial intelligence and machine learning (AI/ML) to turbulence modeling has yielded only modest progress, primarily due to a lack of situation-aware deployment. Data-driven Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (ML-RANS) closures frequently fail to generalize across diverse flow regimes, while scale-resolving simulations (SRS) remain computationally prohibitive. As a result, ML-RANS is often misapplied outside its domain of validity, and ML-SRS is unnecessarily deployed on flows fully amenable to RANS. To guide optimal AI/ML deployment, we propose a turbulence taxonomy map parameterized by degrees of freedom ($Re$) and the degree of non-equilibrium ($SK/ε$). By applying Weaver's well-recognized complexity framework, we classify turbulence into distinct physical regimes. This categorization identifies where local ML-RANS surrogates can generalize, clarifies where explicit scale resolution is physically mandatory, and highlights where physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) are most effective. Ultimately, this perspective establishes a physics-grounded framework for the flow-aware, successful deployment of AI/ML in turbulence.

Extreme-Scale Super-Resolution of Homogeneous Turbulence from a Single Realization

Arth Sojitra, Muhammad Abid, Omer San

Homogeneous turbulent flows exhibit recurring multiscale structures across spatially separated regions, suggesting that a single flow realization may contain sufficient information to learn representations of unresolved scales. We investigate this hypothesis through an extreme super-resolution framework in which a wide-activation deep super-resolution (WDSR) network is trained exclusively on overlapping patches extracted from a single high-resolution turbulent realization. Previously unseen flow fields are reconstructed through patchwise prediction followed by Hann-window-weighted overlap-add assembly. The framework is evaluated for two-dimensional homogeneous isotropic turbulence at super-resolution factors of x32 and x64, representing severe spatial under-resolution. Despite the limited training data, the proposed approach reconstructs coherent vortical structures and recovers fine-scale features more accurately than conventional interpolation. Spectral and statistical analyses further demonstrate improved recovery of high-wavenumber energy content and multiscale flow characteristics. To assess the effectiveness of exploiting internal spatial information, we compare the proposed framework against WDSR models trained on 1, 25, 50, and 100 complete high-resolution snapshots without patch extraction. This comparison distinguishes the benefits of localized learning from those associated with increasing the number of training realizations. The results demonstrate that exploiting spatial redundancy within a single turbulent realization enables accurate reconstruction of previously unseen flow fields, even under extreme spatial under-resolution. These findings establish single-realization patch-based learning as a promising data-efficient approach for turbulent super-resolution when high-resolution training data are scarce.

Two-Way Coupled Dynamics of Quantized Vortex Bundle Formation in Spatially Periodic Normal-Fluid Turbulence

Hiromichi Kobayashi, Makoto Tsubota, Rio Yokota

Using fully coupled two-way simulations combining the vortex filament model with the Navier--Stokes equations, we discover that quantized vortices in superfluid $^4$He are systematically rolled up into coherent bundles within normal-fluid vortex tubes. Although co-rotating quantized vortices do not aggregate spontaneously, mutual friction with the normal fluid actively drives their bundling, directly inheriting the spatial hierarchy of classical turbulence. Mutual friction excites high-wavenumber normal-fluid vortex tubes adjacent to quantized vortices, while the superfluid forms a nested structure of thick low-wavenumber outer tubes and fine high-wavenumber inner tubes around the vortex lines. A distinct time lag between superfluid kinetic energy and dissipation fuels the energy cascade, giving rise to Kolmogorov's $k^{-5/3}$ spectrum. Crucially, bundle lifetimes are governed by the ratio of the local coupling time scale $t_\mathrm{cl}$ to the Kolmogorov dissipation time scale $τ_η$ ($t_\mathrm{cl}/τ_η< 20$), leading to enhanced thermal stability at higher temperatures. These findings establish a unified mechanism for two-fluid multiscale vortex organization.

Numerical Modeling of a Metallic Alloy Droplet Impact, Solidification, and Macrosegregation

Ehsan Amani, Ali Moinirad

For the first time, we study the effects of mass transfer on alloy droplet impact, solidification, and solute non-homogeneity or MacroSegregation (MS). For this purpose, a multi-physics model, including fully-coupled hydrodynamics of multiphase flow, wetting, conjugate heat transfer, phase change, and mass transfer, is established and validated carefully across several benchmarks. In addition, a concentration-flux-correction algorithm is proposed to conserve the solute mass within the droplet at the numerical discretization level. The validated model is subsequently used to explore the impact and solidification of an alloy droplet. The results show that accounting for mass transfer and solute transport improves the prediction of the dynamics of a solidifying splat, in a consistent manner with experimental observations, while this effect has been neglected in all previous studies. The analysis demonstrates that this improvement results from the flow sensitivity to the mushy region thickness, which is regulated by local solute concentration through the solidus and liquidus temperatures. Furthermore, analyses of the MS map identify several critical segregation regions most susceptible to defect initiation, such as cracking, including a long negatively segregated vein within the lamella, formed by the severe advective flow through the mush in the middle stages of the impact, a positively segregated region near the rim of the splat, appearing as a result of hook formation, and a major positively segregated zone at the top of the rim, which develops following the accumulation of the solute-rich melt sustained by washout from the lamella.

eMOOD: Entropy-Based Multidimensional Optimal Order Detection for High-Order Shock-Capturing Finite-Volume Schemes

Michał Umiński, Panagiotis Tsoutsanis, Ahmed A Sheikh Al-Shabab, Alan S. Dawes

We present eMOOD, an entropy-guided evolution of the Multidimensional Optimal Order Detection (MOOD) framework for high-order finite-volume methods on unstructured meshes. A novel troubled-cell indicator based on numerical entropy production replaces the classical relaxed discrete maximum principle. Using entropy generation as a physically motivated measure of non-smoothness, the detector targets shocks, contact discontinuities and under-resolved flow features with a compact stencil. Compared with conventional MOOD detection, eMOOD reduces flagged cells by up to 75%, improving efficiency and reducing memory-access overhead. The detector is used within both the classical MOOD cascade of progressively reduced-order schemes and a novel MOOD-CWENO hybrid, which retains inexpensive linear reconstruction in smooth regions and activates nonlinear high-order reconstruction only where needed. Implemented in the open-source parallel solver UCNS3D, the methodology is assessed on the Shu-Osher shock-entropy-wave interaction, double Mach reflection, supersonic forward-facing step, shock-helium-bubble interaction and triple material point problems. These benchmarks encompass strong shock interactions, shock-induced instabilities, multi-material interfaces and vortex-dominated flows, testing robustness, shock capturing and accuracy on unstructured meshes. Results show that eMOOD preserves the accuracy and robustness of reference CWENO and MOOD formulations while achieving speed-ups of up to 20% without loss of solution quality. Its compact detection stencil, reduced memory footprint and improved localisation offer a more scalable and efficient framework for massively parallel high-order compressible-flow simulations. Active Sculpting: morphodynamics of internally stirred active vesicles

Devadevan M M, Brato Chakrabarti

Active flows are often confined by soft, deformable boundaries that they can push against, reshape, and in turn get reorganized by. Motivated by this interplay, we combine theory and numerical simulations to analyze the morphodynamics of an elastic vesicle enclosing a dilute suspension of extensile active particles. We model the two-dimensional vesicle as a weakly extensible elastic ring with bending rigidity, immersed in a Stokesian fluid, and describe the confined active suspension using a kinetic theory. The vesicle and the suspension are coupled hydrodynamically, through the kinematic and dynamic boundary conditions at the interface, and energetically, through a single alignment interaction that simultaneously aligns active particles with the membrane and the membrane with the particles. We show that this interaction reduces to a weak-anchoring boundary condition familiar from passive liquid crystals and derive a closed-form quiescent equilibrium state in which the nematic order in the suspension organizes into a vortex with a +1 defect core. A stability analysis of this state predicts an ordered hierarchy of activity thresholds at which successive shape modes of the vesicle are destabilized, as well as a purely passive, anchoring-driven instability of floppy vesicles. Nonlinear simulations, performed with a volume-conserving immersed boundary method, reveal that each threshold crossing gives rise to a distinct emergent state: a static spindle with boundary-pinned defects, a rotor that tank treads against its own rotation, a fluctuating spinner, and a turbulent state in which the vesicle acts as a self-stirring compartment. Beyond demonstrating how boundary deformability sculpts, and is sculpted by, confined active matter, our results open the door to study hydrodynamic interactions between such active vesicles and to use membrane fluctuations as a probe of activity of suspensions.

Response of Confined Mesoscale Flames to Acoustic Forcing

Akhil Aravind, Balasundaram Mohan, Saptarshi Basu

Mesoscale combustion plays a vital role in applications ranging from portable power generation to micro-propulsion systems. This study investigates the response of mesoscale flames to upstream acoustic forcing across three characteristic flame regimes: Flames with Repetitive Extinction and Ignition (FREI), Stable Flames (SF), and Propagating Flames (PF). Acoustic forcing is applied over a broad range of pressure intensities at the first harmonic of the combustor tube. At low forcing intensities, all the flame regimes exhibit oscillations at the forcing frequency without qualitative changes in their respective flame behaviours. However, as the acoustic pressure increases, all flame regimes transition to a common unsteady regime characterised by periodic ignition-extinction cycles (resembling FREI) alongside strong oscillations at the forcing frequency. This response is identified as the oscillating FREI (O-FREI) regime, in which increasing forcing intensity leads to a reduction in the mean heat release rate, an amplification of oscillations at the forcing frequency, and a drop in the Strouhal number associated with the ignition-extinction cycle. The response characteristics across all flame regimes are examined in detail and are further complemented using reduced-order mathematical models.

CFD Forge: A Physics-Constrained Agentic Framework for Autonomous CFD

Aakash Shailesh Oza, Ryan F. Johnson, Amir Barati Farimani

Setting up, running, and assessing a computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulation requires substantial manual effort, from meshing and solver setup to diagnosis and interpretation, and repeating it across design variants slows engineering iteration. We present CFD Forge, an agentic framework built around OpenFOAM that carries an engineering request through case construction, meshing, solver execution, diagnostics, and reporting. A large language model interprets the request, reads the simulation evidence, identifies likely causes of problems, and proposes actions from a closed set. Automation is only useful if its results can be trusted, so acceptance is not left to the model: deterministic checks, registered for each type of flow, measure explicit criteria and decide. We demonstrate the framework on a converging-diverging nozzle and a two-dimensional forward-facing step, including geometry and operating changes requested in text. A turbulent cube, run outside the agent loop, shows why the checks matter: the solver completed and the mean drag settled, but the lateral-force oscillations were still growing, so a retrospective assessment withheld acceptance. To test this separation, we replayed 36 recorded simulation states, some with deliberately altered diagnostics, through the same model in two setups. Judging from the evidence without the check results, the model accepted 12 of the 13 detectable faults in all three repeats; with CFD Forge's checks deciding, none was accepted. The two setups also differ in prompting, and a fixed rule reached the same verdicts, so the model's value lies in the open-ended work rather than the verdict. Case files, recorded model calls, and decisions are retained for audit. A model that does the work, paired with checks that decide what is accepted, is a step toward automated CFD that can be trusted in high-throughput design and active-learning loops.
