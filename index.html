AeroZoo: A Benchmark of Well-Posed Flow Problems for Foundation Models of Aerodynamics

Peter Sharpe, Carmelo Gonzales, Corey Adams, Mehdi Ataei, Sanjay Choudhry

Neural surrogates for computational fluid dynamics (CFD) are trained and ranked on datasets that each vary one design family in one flow domain. Such datasets cannot test the least that an aerodynamic foundation model must do: predict CFD-quality incompressible flow in seconds for new shapes in new enclosures. On DrivAerML, larger models and longer training barely lower Transolver's error; doubling the training cases cuts it by about 12% up to 400 cases, exhausting the dataset. We introduce AeroZoo, a benchmark and training corpus of 3,815 time-mean solutions to the unsteady Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) equations with a transition model, across seven geometry families, eight enclosure configurations and Reynolds numbers from $10^3$ to $10^8$. Each sample poses a well-posed PDE problem in the form a solver receives it: the boundary meshes grouped by condition type, the prescribed values and the fluid properties. The 2,816 samples with a stable time mean form the canonical benchmark: two tasks, engineering metrics and 22 frozen splits, disjoint in geometry, that hold out geometry families, enclosures, boundary-condition layouts and Reynolds ranges. On the random split, boundary-condition tokens cut GeoTransolver's median velocity error from 76.9% to 8.4% of the inlet speed in single-seed runs (uniform inlet flow: 11.0%). ISLA, a transformer for boundary-driven PDEs, reaches 5.3% over four seeds, about 4 times the change from a finer mesh. On the same test samples, a held-out geometry family barely changes ISLA's error relative to uniform flow, and a held-out enclosure or boundary-condition layout raises it 2.4 to 9.2 times. The data and the scoring code will be released.

Gradient-Aware AI Agents for Autonomous Turbulence Model Discovery via Differentiable CFD

Yi Liu, Xiantao Fan, Mingqi Xu, Jian-Xun Wang

Turbulence modeling relies on human choices of closure form, physical inputs, and tunable parameters. Training closures a posteriori is efficient only with gradients through long chaotic rollouts, which few solvers provide. Most a posteriori approaches therefore resort to gradient-free optimization, and nearly all fix the closure form in advance, learning only its parameters. In this work, we introduce a gradient-aware agentic framework that poses closure discovery as a bilevel problem: a multi-agent large language model (LLM) system searches over closure forms, while a GPU-native differentiable computational fluid dynamics (CFD) solver supplies gradients, making differentiable a posteriori learning an action of the autonomous AI agent. For each proposal, the agents construct a differentiable training program by embedding the analytical, neural, or hybrid closure in the solver, implementing a differentiable objective, and validating the gradient path. They then decide whether to evaluate the closure, compute its parameter sensitivities, or train it a posteriori, select the optimization settings, and revise subsequent proposals using the simulation outcomes and returned gradients. Truncated-horizon gradients make this training usable under chaotic dynamics for closures with tens of thousands of parameters. Demonstrations include dissipation-law and subgrid-scale closure discovery in two-dimensional turbulence and wall-model discovery for large-eddy simulation of three-dimensional channel flow. With gradient-based training, the agent recovers reference coefficients that forward evaluation alone does not reach within the same budget, and autonomously discovers and trains analytical and neural closures that improve agreement with the targeted reference statistics. These results demonstrate the feasibility of end-to-end autonomous turbulence model development in time-resolved flows.

SUMNO: Spatially Unrolled Multigrid Neural Operator for Fluid Flow Surrogates

Yingqi Hu, Nan Deng, Changxiao Shao, Xiaozhou He

Data-driven surrogate modeling has emerged as a powerful paradigm for accelerating computational fluid dynamics, and hierarchical U-shaped networks, which resemble the structure of classical Geometric Multigrid (GMG) methods, have become prevalent designs. Modern surrogates built on these designs achieve competitive accuracy at a fraction of the cost of numerical solvers; however, accurate prediction of complex flows remains challenging, and no existing architecture leads across them. In such networks, the multigrid analogy mainly concerns the multi-resolution topology, while their components largely follow common vision practice. To address this, this study introduces SUMNO, a multigrid neural operator for fluid flow fields on regular grids that spatially unrolls a V-cycle into a feed-forward U-shaped network. Parameter-free down- and upsampling stand in for restriction and prolongation, and additive skip connections for coarse-grid correction, with normalization-free transfer and skip pathways. Within this macro-architecture, convolutions are used at the fine and intermediate resolutions and shifted-window attention at the coarsest, whose window partitioning is generalized to handle periodicity independently per axis. Evaluated on unseen test configurations across four unsteady and steady flow benchmarks against five representative baselines, SUMNO is best in 5 of 7 settings and ranks second in the remaining two. It reduces the 10-step rollout MSE by about 30% relative to the strongest baseline, and outperforms three baselines with over 130 million parameters using 9.4 million parameters.

ISLA: A Coordinate-Free, Measure-Weighted Transformer for Boundary-Driven PDEs

Peter Sharpe

A foundational principle of physics is that all coordinate systems are equally valid: the behavior of a PDE-governed system does not depend on the origin or orientation of the frame, nor on the units or mesh used to write it down, and neither should a surrogate model. Yet the neural surrogates with the lowest errors on today's aerodynamics benchmarks lack these invariances and fail under simple transformations of the same problem: for models trained on PDEs of cars in one pose (i.e., translation and rotation) and from one mesher, rotating the full PDE raises the surface-pressure error from 4% to more than 100%, and re-sampling its boundary raises it to about 30%. We introduce ISLA (Invariant Slice Attention), a slice-attention transformer for steady, boundary-driven PDEs in which no learned operation receives a coordinate and every sum over the boundary is a quadrature. Positions enter only as distances and relative angles to learned slice anchors, lengths are measured in a unit computed from the boundary, and interior points query the boundary through a tree-accelerated learned kernel without influencing one another. With every boundary cell a token and an exact boundary read, ISLA is equivariant to rigid motions and reflections, invariant to the units of its declared data, and consistent under mesh refinement, while at every setting each query is independent of all other queries. On AeroZoo, 2,816 flows that vary in shape, size, speed, and boundary conditions, its mean reference-scaled velocity and pressure errors are at least 3.1 and 3.3 times lower than any baseline's. On AirfRANS its errors are second only to those of a boundary-integral model (GLOBE) and at least 1.6 times lower than those of Transolver and GeoTransolver.

An Invariant Neural-Network Eddy-Viscosity Closure with Iterative Solver Coupling for Molten Salt Fast Reactor Thermal-Hydraulics

Zaid Abulawi, William Oliveira, Akzhol Almukhametov, Jun Fang, Ling Zou, Rui Hu

System thermal-hydraulics codes such as the System Analysis Module (SAM) close turbulence on coarse meshes with algebraic zero-equation mixing-length models. These models cannot represent the non-equilibrium turbulence, flow separation, and large-scale recirculation of open-cavity advanced reactors. We develop an invariant neural-network eddy-viscosity closure for SAM and apply it to the Molten Salt Fast Reactor (MSFR). The closure predicts the turbulent kinetic energy and timescale of each element from the flow and geometry of its neighbors, without solving turbulence transport equations. The resulting non-negative eddy viscosity enters the implicit diffusive operator, which keeps the momentum equations well conditioned. We prove that the closure is exactly invariant to translation, Galilean boost, rotation, reflection, and neighbor ordering. A network trained on high-order Nek5000 fields sees a different feature distribution on the coarse SAM mesh. We remove this covariate shift by iteratively retraining the network on SAM flow states against fixed Nek5000 targets. For a held-out steady state, the coupled closure reproduces the single recirculation cell and eddy-viscosity plume of the Nek5000 reference. In a 120 s pump-coastdown transient, it halves the velocity error of the mixing-length model, from 37.6% to 17.7%, and matches the Nek5000 eddy viscosity with a time-averaged relative error of 12%. The remaining velocity error comes from the coarse mesh. The coupled transient takes about 0.2 core-hours, compared with about 330 core-hours for Nek5000. The results show that the closure can recover two-equation RANS turbulence behavior in a coarse-mesh system code at a small fraction of the CFD cost.

Spatiotemporal learning improves quantification of pore-space evolution and fluid displacement

Zhuangzhuang Ma, Rukuan Chai, Qianqian Ma, Yanghua Wang, Branko Bijeljic, Martin J. Blunt

Pore-space evolution and fluid displacement influence how rocks transmit and store fluids, affecting geological carbon storage, underground hydrogen storage and groundwater contaminant transport. A single time-resolved micro-CT experiment may produce tens to hundreds of three-dimensional images, and segmentation is required to quantify physical properties and run image-based simulations. Traditional methods such as thresholding perform poorly on noisy, low-contrast images and need manual adjustment per image; 3D deep learning networks segment each image without temporal information, so their errors vary between images and could be mistaken for physical change. Here we introduce a transformer-based 4D segmentation framework combining spatial and temporal information. It uses sequential images to resolve ambiguous phase assignments and separate physical change from segmentation error. The method is tested on images of mineral dissolution: it measures pore-volume growth and rock boundaries more accurately than an equivalent 3D model that ignores temporal information. It also captures the increase in permeability verified against reference segmentations and flow simulations. The advantage transfers to another dissolution sample without retraining. On two other datasets of fluid displacement, it reduces spurious changes in predicted rock and porosity fluctuations and accurately identifies filling events. Spatiotemporal segmentation turns sequences of hundreds of three-dimensional images into more reliable quantitative measurements of pore-space evolution and fluid displacement without per-image parameter adjustment to support the analysis of time-resolved CT imaging.

Complex nematic dynamics driven by steady Kolmogorov flows

Rahil N Valani, Sumesh P Thampi, Julia M Yeomans

Complex nematohydrodynamics, including irregular defect dynamics, is commonly associated with time-dependent flows, most notably in active turbulence. Here we consider a simple model of a planar nematic subjected to a temporally steady but spatially varying flow and investigate the onset of time-dependent dynamics. Subjecting a passive nematic to steady Kolmogorov flow, while eliminating velocity fluctuations and hydrodynamic feedback, we find that increasing the flow strength drives a transition from stationary to periodic and, at stronger forcing, irregular spatiotemporal dynamics. The time-dependent states involve repeated local suppression of nematic order and director reorganisation, arising from the competition between spatially varying shear and nematic relaxation. Bifurcation and phase-space analyses characterise this progression, while reduced-order models identify the dynamical origins. A two-mode model captures the onset of periodic motion through a saddle-node infinite-period bifurcation. A five-mode Galerkin truncation further recovers the progression from stationary to periodic and irregular dynamics. These results allow us to explain how a fixed, spatially heterogeneous velocity field can generate temporal complexity through the nonlinear dynamics of nematic order alone.

Data-driven Correction Reduced-Order Stochastic Collocation Method for the Time-Dependent Navier-Stokes Equations

Md. Abdul Aziz, Muhammad Mohebujjaman, Xuping Xie

We develop two hybrid data-driven Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) Reduced-Order Models (ROMs) for the non-stationary incompressible Navier--Stokes Equations (NSEs) with random viscosity. The proposed framework combines Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) ROMs, spatial filtering, data-driven closure modeling, and the Stochastic Collocation Method (SCM). A key feature is the approximation of the realization-dependent closure term by the closure associated with the expected viscosity. Consequently, a single data-driven closure model constructed from the full-order solution corresponding to the expected viscosity can be reused across all realizations, thereby avoiding the expensive construction of realization-specific closure models. This leads to the Data-Driven Correction UQ ROM (DDC-UQ-ROM) and its physically constrained variant, the Constrained DDC-UQ-ROM (CDDC-UQ-ROM), in which physical constraints are imposed on the data-driven correction model. The proposed methods are evaluated for a two-dimensional (2D) flow past a circular cylinder problem with a 5D random viscosity field, considering mean Reynolds numbers $Re=100$ and $Re=500$, different correlation lengths, and both in-sample and out-of-sample parameter settings. Numerical results show that the DDC-UQ-ROM generally improves upon the standard Galerkin ROM (G-ROM), while the CDDC-UQ-ROM exhibits the most accurate and robust approximation of the benchmark energy evolution across the cases considered. The robustness and accuracy of the CDDC-UQ-ROM are further assessed through statistical error analyses of the first- and second-order energy moments, reduced-training-data experiments, and an indicative comparison of wall-clock computational costs with the full-order UQ calculations.

Irreversible dynamics for ideal fluids on Riemannian manifolds: metriplectic energy-conserving alternatives to Navier-Stokes

Erwin Luesink, Archishman Saha, Tanya Schmah, Cristina Stoica

For modelling dissipative phenomena such as friction, diffusion and viscosity, the Hamiltonian framework has a key limitation: once the Casimir invariants are fixed, Hamiltonian flows preserve phase-space volume, which rules out relaxation to equilibrium. Thus modelling dissipation requires modifying the Hamiltonian structure. Following the metriplectic (equivalently, GENERIC) approach, we add to the Hamiltonian vector field a dissipative term generated by a symmetric bracket, here constructed from a Riemannian metric. The resulting flow conserves the energy, and optionally further invariants, while driving a chosen generalised entropy monotonically, which makes the dynamics irreversible; when the entropy is bounded on energy level sets in the direction in which it is driven, it acts as a Lyapunov functional. We apply this construction to ideal fluids on compact Riemannian manifolds, choosing Casimirs of the Lie-Poisson bracket as entropies. In two dimensions, dissipating enstrophy yields the already-known energy-constrained Navier-Stokes equations, which include a nonlocal term restoring energy balance. In three dimensions, where enstrophy is no longer a Casimir, we derive new irreversible models. For incompressible and compressible fluids carrying an advected scalar, dissipating potential enstrophy or higher potential-vorticity moments gives a nonlinear, anisotropic diffusion that acts to align the gradients of the scalar and of the potential vorticity. For a purely incompressible fluid, taking helicity as the entropy gives a first-order, non-smoothing dissipative term whose stationary states are Beltrami fields.

In contrast, the frequency content at the trailing edge and in the draft tube is considerably better resolved using the RSM than the other models because of its capability to consistently capture turbulence anisotropy.

Harmonic structure and parametric prediction of instantaneous power in oscillating foils

Junjie Shen, Jennifer A. Franck

A closed-form model is developed to predict the instantaneous lift, pitching moment, and power of an oscillating-foil energy harvester directly from its kinematic input. Such foils extract renewable power from moving water through sinusoidal heaving and pitching. However, due to their large-amplitude motion and vortex-dominated dynamics, classical unsteady aerodynamics cannot accurately predict the time-dependent force and power generation. To generate the model, 405 two-dimensional simulations are computed which vary heave amplitude, pitch amplitude, and frequency, representing the energy harvesting kinematic parameter space. The lift and pitching moment are decomposed into odd Fourier harmonics, and each Fourier coefficient is regressed on sixteen physics-based polynomial terms derived from the kinematic, power, and energy scalings. The regression is performed in two Ridge steps, one for attribution and one for the deployable predictor. The attribution shows that the first harmonic, which contains the mean power, depends on the motion amplitudes. The higher harmonics respond nonlinearly to the frequency, which determines the number and timing of the vortex shedding. The model recovers the cycle-averaged power with a leave-one-out $R^2$ of $0.98$, and reproduces the instantaneous lift with a median peak error of $9\%$ and a median phase error below $1\%$ of the period. The harmonic order of the response counts the discrete vortex events in each stroke, as confirmed by the wake visualization. The model's analytical expression for time-dependent force and power can be utilized as a fast and accurate alternative to high-fidelity simulations, specifically targeting device design and array optimization of oscillating foils.

A Conservative Darcy-Brinkman-Stokes Formulation for Digital Rock Flow Simulation with Embedded Second-Brinkman Effects

Accurate pore-scale direct numerical simulation (DNS) typically requires boundary-conforming meshes on fully resolved geometries, which is memory-intensive and often becomes impractical when modelling complex rock geometries. In contrast, the Darcy-Brinkman-Stokes (DBS) framework enables simulations to be performed directly on voxelized micro-CT images.
