CFD-driven symbolic regression of RANS transition models

Louenas Zemmour, Xavier Gloerfelt, Paola Cinnella

Laminar-turbulent transition governs skin friction, heat transfer and losses in turbomachinery, yet Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) transition models rely on empirical correlations and hand-calibrated activation functions whose structural deficiencies cannot be resolved by recalibration. We propose a CFD-driven sparse symbolic regression framework to learn interpretable, closed-form corrections to the production and destruction activation functions in the intermittency equation of the local, Galilean-invariant $k$-$ω$-$γ$ model of Ge et al. Each correction combines a polynomial of a local pressure-gradient parameter, controlling amplitude and sign, with a bounded exponential of local flow features, controlling spatial support. This construction bounds the corrections without ad hoc limiters. Coefficients are identified by gradient-free optimization with the RANS solver in the loop, accelerated by POD-Kriging field surrogates, using mean-velocity data from wall-resolved large-eddy simulations (LES) of the ERCOFTAC T3 flat-plate cases. Elastic-net hyperparameters are selected automatically through bi-level optimization with leave-one-case-out cross-validation. The resulting model retains 11 of 21 candidate terms, compared with 20 under conventional grid-search tuning, while achieving comparable or slightly better accuracy on training and unseen cases. Both approaches recover the same leading-order correction. Applied without retraining to the transonic LS89 turbine cascade, far beyond the training envelope, the augmented model remains numerically robust and improves heat-transfer predictions at all four operating points, reducing errors by up to 70% where the baseline predicts premature transition under high freestream turbulence. Residual errors in transition onset and length highlight the limits of training on flat-plate data alone.

Learned Surrogates of Three-Dimensional Magnetohydrodynamic Turbulence

Semih Kacmaz, E. A. Huerta

Three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence, the flow of astrophysical and fusion plasmas, is costly to simulate. We present, to our knowledge, the first learned surrogates of three-dimensional resistive MHD turbulence, and compare three standard backbones in that role: a convolutional UNet, a shifted-window transformer and a Fourier neural operator. They are trained as autoregressive emulators of decaying MHD turbulence at a resolution of $128^3$ and Reynolds numbers of 1000, 3000 and 6000, on 500 simulations per Reynolds number, each with one two-stage procedure, single steps and then short rollouts, and stopped by one rule. Over a window of about one large-eddy turnover time, the UNet and the transformer reach relative velocity errors of 0.018 to 0.053 and keep the current sheets, the enstrophy, the current density and the energy spectra of the simulation; which of the two is the more accurate depends on the Reynolds number, the UNet leading at 3000 and 6000 and the transformer at 1000. Applied with the same backbones and training procedure to two public benchmarks, the models reach the lowest error reported to date on the isotropic turbulence set of FactFormer, 39% below the best published model. On the compressible MHD set of The Well, their rollout errors are a third to a half below those of the best of The Well's baselines. They also match a 1.3 B-parameter pretrained foundation model in its rollout windows, with 20-62 times fewer parameters. The code, the configuration of every model, the evaluation scripts and the data generator with the seed of every simulation will be released upon publication.

Hard Constraint Projection in a Physics Informed Neural Network (Burgers)

Miranda J S Horne, Peter K Jimack, Amirul Khan, He Wang

Physics informed machine learning provides a framework to study and simulate fluid dynamics, potentially at a fraction of the computational cost of traditional numerical methods, with the goal of improving the explainability, accuracy and robustness of purely data driven machine learning methods. In this work, we extend the hard constraint projection (HCP) method developed by Chen et al. [Journal of Computational Physics, 445, 110624, 2021] from a linear partial differential equation (PDE) to a nonlinear PDE: the 1D Burgers' equation. To strictly impose a discrete approximation of the governing equation, the Burgers' equation is first locally discretised (using a finite difference stencil) then decomposed into a constraint matrix and a prediction matrix. Using tools from linear algebra, the constraint matrix is used to construct a projection matrix. A physics informed neural network (PINN) [Raissi et al. Journal of Computational Physics, 378, 686, 2019] is used to predict the velocity, and a final unlearnable layer projects the velocity prediction to the hyperplane of exact solutions to a discretised form of the Burgers' equation. Our initial results indicate that the HCP-PINN initially trains similarly to an equivalent PINN, and as the PINN loss plateaus, the HCP-PINN loss continues to decline. When tested on a previously unseen uniform grid of points covering the domain, the HCP-PINN exhibits higher accuracy, converging to a data error approximately an order of magnitude lower than the comparable PINN. We also observe that the inclusion of the HCP layer results in better observance of the governing PDE. Our findings suggest that the HCP-PINN can be more robust to the selection of physics collocation points, and less dependent on collocation point quantity.

Learned Warm Starts for Steady Transonic RANS Simulations of Wing and Aircraft Configurations

Muhammad Irfan Zafar, Saleh Abuhanieh, Şahin Yiğit

Neural surrogates can accelerate many-query aerodynamic analyses, but their standalone predictions may be insufficiently accurate or robust to support reliable design evaluations. We develop a learned warm-start formulation that predicts mean-flow and turbulence fields directly on native CFD meshes for subsequent correction by the RANS solver. A common architecture and objective structure are used across subcritical and transonic flows, including challenging conditions with shock-induced separation. A condition-modulated Transolver combines Fourier and wall features with target-gated regularization, a Sobolev-type loss, adaptive loss weighting and sampled training. Prediction requires no preliminary flow solution, and training requires no solver-residual evaluation. Extensive 3D CFD evaluations on SuperWing, the NASA Common Research Model (CRM) and DLR-F6 assess geometric and operating-condition generalization, including interpolation, extrapolation and cross-mesh deployment, using Spalart-Allmaras and $k$-$ω$ SST closures on million-cell meshes. Unseen nominal cases achieve geometric-mean iteration speed-ups of approximately $2$-$4\times$ under a common convergence criterion based on cold-run aerodynamic coefficients. On CRM, learned initialization provides greater aggregate acceleration than nearest-condition restarts using the same solution library. Resolution invariance enables direct deployment of coarse-mesh models on finer meshes without retraining, solution interpolation or target-condition coarse solves. Including data generation and training, estimated campaign speed-ups reach $2.1$-$2.3\times$. No warm run diverges; fewer than $2\%$ fail to satisfy the cold-reference convergence criterion. These results support learned initialization for repeated aerodynamic analyses, while highlighting initialization sensitivity near separation as a remaining limitation.

Physics-Informed Latent Neural ODEs for Reduced-Order Modeling of Hydrogen, Ammonia, and Methane Combustion Kinetics

Jay Patel, Shubhangi Bansude

Detailed chemical kinetic mechanisms are computationally expensive in reacting-flow simulations because of their high dimensionality and numerical stiffness. We develop an end-to-end physics-informed autoencoder neural ordinary differential equation (PI-AE-NODE) framework in which an encoder compresses the thermochemical state into a low-dimensional latent space, a Neural ODE advances it continuously in time, and a decoder reconstructs the temperature and all species mass fractions. Elemental conservation, mass conservation, and enthalpy consistency are embedded in the training objective. The framework is applied to H$_2$/air, NH$_3$/air, and CH$_4$/air autoignition using detailed mechanisms of increasing size and complexity, which are represented accurately with only a few latent variables. Compared with an otherwise identical data-driven model, the physical constraints improve ignition delay prediction and conservation behaviour. The models reproduce major and intermediate species evolution at both training and unseen conditions, and their latent dynamics permit efficient explicit integration, with the computational advantage over the detailed mechanisms increasing with mechanism size.

Optimizing synthetic jet placement for control stability in increasing Reynolds number flows based on deep reinforcement learning

Wang Jia, Hang Xu

This study utilizes reinforcement learning algorithms to develop advanced control strategies for manipulating synthetic jets positioned on a cylinder, aiming to achieve effective flow control. It addresses critical challenges in achieving robust performance across moderate to high Reynolds numbers (Re) and varying jet placement configurations. The results demonstrate that placing synthetic jets near flow separation regions yields optimal control performance. At Re = 500, the multi-objective flow control strategy achieves up to 37.4% drag reduction and 99% suppression of lift oscillations, while completely suppressing vortex shedding. At Re = 1,000, drag reduction improves to 49.1%, with equally effective lift suppression and complete vortex shedding suppression. At Re = 2,000, the strategy achieves a maximum drag reduction of 58.8% and suppresses 90% of lift oscillations, although some residual wake instabilities persist. Additionally, synthetic jets effectively disrupted detached vortices and stabilized the wake, resulting in streamlined flow and suppressed vortex shedding. These findings underscore the pivotal influence of jet placement in optimizing flow control efficacy, while affirming the advanced capability of control strategies to tackle intricate challenges.

Bistability in the stalling behavior of thick, cambered airfoils under elevated freestream turbulence

Jaylon E. McGhee, John A. N. Farnsworth

The aerodynamic performance of a thick, cambered FFA-W3-301 airfoil section near and beyond stall was examined under elevated freestream turbulence using time-resolved surface pressure measurements for chord-based Reynolds numbers from 125,000 to 275,000. Three passive turbulence grids were used to isolate the effects of turbulence intensity and integral length scale, and the results were compared with a clean inflow. The elevated turbulence largely had a positive effect on the mean performance of the airfoil section, eliminating premature flow separation and recovering lift and drag polars more representative of a higher effective Reynolds number; however, resolving the lift in time showed that the time-averaged polars concealed bistable switching. The time-averaged lift was an occupancy-weighted average of two states, and conventional mean and standard-deviation statistics misrepresented the true post-stall unsteady loads. Beyond the maximum-lift angle of attack, the lift alternated repeatedly between two distinct levels with no characteristic frequency. At the highest tested Reynolds number, the switching persisted over an angle-of-attack range of up to nine degrees, more than an order of magnitude wider than that reported for a thin, symmetric section in a low turbulence inflow. The lift levels of the two states were also found to be nearly independent of the inflow turbulence level, whereas the fraction of time spent in each differed between turbulence conditions. conditionally averaged surface pressure distributions and proper orthogonal decomposition of the surface pressure consistently showed that neither state is fully attached; the states correspond to two positions of a single separation front on the suction side, a switching accurately captured by a single pressure mode from the decomposition.

Three-dimensional computational rheometry of viscoplastic fluids in vane-in-cup fixtures

James Malcolm, Clare McColgan, Emma Mullarkey, Emad Chaparian

We develop a computational framework to investigate the flow kinematics of viscoplastic materials, described by the Herschel-Bulkley model, in vane-in-cup rheometers. Numerical simulations are performed using augmented Lagrangian finite element and regularised finite volume methods for two- and three-dimensional configurations, respectively, to characterise the hydrodynamics and yielding behaviour over a broad range of effective parameters. Three types of vanes are investigated: conventional 4- and 6-arm bespoke vanes and a 24-arm fractal vane (Owens et al., J. Rheol., vol. 64(3), 2020, 643-662). Our previous two-dimensional study (Chaparian et al., J. Non-Newtonian Fluid Mech., vol. 307, 2022, 104857) demonstrated the superiority of the fractal vane over conventional N-arm designs. The bespoke vanes produce highly non-uniform yielding, leading to significant deviations from the circular Couette analogy, whereas the 24-arm fractal vane generates nearly axisymmetric yield surfaces that effectively "cloak" the vane geometry. Consequently, the resulting flow closely resembles the azimuthal shear in a conventional "bob & cup" rheometer, providing substantially more accurate rheological measurements. Building on these findings, the present study revisits the torque-to-stress and rotation-to-strain rate conversions used in vane-in-cup rheometry. In particular, we re-examine the circular Couette analogy that underpins both the physical interpretation of the flow and the extraction of flow curves from raw rheometric data. Based on our extensive two- and three-dimensional simulations, we identify limitations of the existing conversion relations and propose improved closure expressions that enable more accurate determination of material's rheological properties. Furthermore, the three-dimensional flow structures and yielding patterns within the vane-in-cup fixtures are characterised.

Differentiable Schwarz-Coupled Solvers for Thin Boundary Layer Transport: Inverse Modeling via Agentic AI Optimization

Siva Viknesh, Amirhossein Arzani

High-Péclet-number transport problems give rise to thin boundary layers with steep near-wall gradients whose accurate solution requires high-resolution meshes and could challenge inverse modeling. To address this challenge, we develop an overlapping Schwarz domain-decomposition framework grounded in perturbation theory, in which an inner equation governs near-wall dynamics and an outer equation governs bulk transport, and both subproblems are discretized using the finite element method and assembled into a fully differentiable multidomain physics solver. The formulation permits coarser mesh resolutions, reducing computational cost relative to a single-domain formulation. Exploiting the differentiability of the coupled solver, we formulate inverse problems in which the governing equations and boundary conditions are imposed as hard constraints, with the Schwarz coupling enforcing interface continuity. To accelerate the resulting adjoint-based optimization, we integrate large language models (LLMs) into the inverse workflow in two distinct configurations: a standalone optimizer that proposes successive parameter updates from the adjoint-computed sensitivities and an agentic artificial intelligence (AI) approach with access to the complete solver workflow, enabling it to perform multistage optimization procedures. The agentic AI configuration is shown to be most advantageous when the spatially varying unknown is represented in a functional basis rather than a high-dimensional nodal field representation. In this case, the AI agent grows the library of candidate basis functions during optimization under a prescribed admission policy, admitting predefined families in stages and initializing their parameters. In short, our proposed framework integrates Schwarz-coupled differentiable physics with agentic AI optimization for the inverse modeling of multiscale transport problems.

Dual-Band Mid-Infrared Imaging for Measurement of Fuel Concentration and Temperature in a Supersonic Cavity

Loren C. Hahn, Erik L. Braun, Brendan J. McGann, Katherine C. Opacich, Timothy M. Ombrello

A dual-band mid-infrared absorption imaging method is demonstrated for spatially resolved measurement of ethylene concentration and temperature in a Mach-2 cavity flow. The method used narrowband filters selected to exploit the differing in-band temperature sensitivities of ethylene absorption, together with a broadband infrared source and high-frame-rate mid-wave infrared camera. Temperature-dependent, filter-weighted absorption cross-sections were used to measure two-dimensional ethylene number density and temperature fields. Measurements were obtained for cavity, curtain, and discrete fuel-injection configurations, including cases with the leading edge of a quasi-steady shock near the cavity. Cavity equivalence ratio measurements agreed well with independent extractive measurements and reproduced trends previously observed using hyperspectral imaging. A complementary single-band implementation using a temperature-independent filter provided instantaneous ethylene concentration fields at 100 Hz and revealed intermittent reductions in cavity equivalence ratio associated with plume deflection by the shock train leading edge. The dual-band approach provides quantitative full-field measurements with substantially simpler spectral instrumentation than hyperspectral imaging while retaining flexibility for time-resolved flow measurements.

Triadic energy cascades and linear amplification mechanisms in supersonic turbulent boundary layers over curved walls

Lucas Feitosa de Souza, Hugo F. da Silva Lui, William Roberto Wolf, Pedro Stefanin Volpiani

Direct numerical simulations are performed to investigate supersonic turbulent boundary layers subjected to continuous concave and convex surface curvatures. Scale-resolved spectral turbulent kinetic energy (TKE) budgets, triadic energy-transfer bispectra, spectral proper orthogonal decomposition (SPOD), and global resolvent analysis are employed to elucidate the effects of compressibility and pressure gradients, and to distinguish the linear and nonlinear mechanisms at play. Over concave walls, the destabilizing curvature enhances pressure-strain redistribution, shifting near-wall turbulence toward a two-component state, and induces a shear-driven outer-layer production peak at low spanwise wavenumbers. Bispectral and SPOD analyses reveal that this outer-layer energy feeds a structured, multi-step triadic transfer toward fundamental large-scale and spanwise-uniform modes, which physically manifests as streamwise vortex breakdown rather than a three-dimensional inverse cascade. Global resolvent analysis further demonstrates that while nonlinear triadic interactions select the dominant scale amplitudes, the spatial topology of these coherent structures is dictated by linear mean-flow receptivity and spatially developing convective instabilities. Conversely, over convex walls, stabilizing curvature and dilatation attenuate pressure fluctuations and suppress pressure-strain redistribution, breaking the near-wall turbulent cycle and trapping energy within elongated, one-component streamwise streaks, while a localized resolvent wavemaker energizes the downstream recovery region.

Inherent Failure Modes of Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Buoyancy-Driven Flow: Role of Non-Dimensionalization in Network Training

Krishan Chand, Ahmad Peyvan, Haoxiang Luo

We investigate the influence of non-dimensionalization on the trainability and accuracy of physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) for buoyancy-driven flows. Using a differentially heated square cavity at $\mathrm{Pr}=0.71$ and $\mathrm{Ra}=10^3$--$10^5$, we compare thermal-diffusive and free-fall velocity scalings under identical training conditions. The free-fall scaling, with a unit buoyancy coefficient, achieves errors below 1.1\% in the mean Nusselt number for $\mathrm{Ra}\le10^4$, whereas the diffusive scaling, with a buoyancy coefficient of $\mathrm{Ra}\,\mathrm{Pr}$, produces errors of 157--650\% and diverges at $\mathrm{Ra}=10^5$. We identify a coefficient-induced gradient imbalance, with the dominant buoyancy-related contribution to the loss gradient scaling as $(\mathrm{Ra}\,\mathrm{Pr})^2$, and confirm it through direct gradient measurements. Neither sparse-data supervision nor adaptive loss weighting resolves the training difficulties under the tested conditions. For unsteady convection, the free-fall PINN accurately predicts long-time heat transfer but fails to capture the transient Nusselt-number overshoot. These findings establish non-dimensionalization as an important design consideration for improving PINN trainability in buoyancy-driven flows.
