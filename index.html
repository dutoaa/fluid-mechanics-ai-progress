High-fidelity Three-Dimensional Flow Field Reconstruction Driven by Turbulent Fluid Dynamics

Hanlin Kong, Jianan Wei, Yuntian Chen, Yujia Wang, Wenguan Wang

High-fidelity three-dimensional turbulent flow fields are essential for scientific and engineering analyses, yet obtaining them through direct numerical simulation or high-resolution measurements remains expensive. Super-resolution reconstruction from coarse-grained data therefore offers a promising alternative, but remains fundamentally challenging because fine-scale structures are intrinsically three-dimensional and dynamically coupled across scales. Existing data-driven approaches still struggle to recover multiscale volumetric structures and their coherent temporal evolution. To address these limitations, we propose a turbulence-aware framework for time-resolved three-dimensional flow reconstruction. The framework couples a multiscale neural operator, designed to recover volumetric structures, with a state space model to promote temporal consistency in the reconstructed dynamics. In addition, Navier-Stokes residuals are incorporated as soft constraints to regularize the reconstruction toward physically plausible dynamics. A mixture-of-experts mechanism is also introduced to support unsupervised adaptation to unknown coarse-graining. Across evaluations on canonical turbulent systems and atmospheric reanalysis data, the proposed framework reduces reconstruction errors relative to learning-based and classical baselines, while better preserving multiscale spatial structure and coherent temporal evolution. These results validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in reconstructing high-fidelity three-dimensional flow fields from coarse-grained data.

AI Meets Turbulence Modeling: Complex Systems Perspective

Sharath S. Girimaji

Despite widespread enthusiasm, applying artificial intelligence and machine learning (AI/ML) to turbulence modeling has yielded only modest progress, primarily due to a lack of situation-aware deployment. Data-driven Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (ML-RANS) closures frequently fail to generalize across diverse flow regimes, while scale-resolving simulations (SRS) remain computationally prohibitive. As a result, ML-RANS is often misapplied outside its domain of validity, and ML-SRS is unnecessarily deployed on flows fully amenable to RANS. To guide optimal AI/ML deployment, we propose a turbulence taxonomy map parameterized by degrees of freedom ($Re$) and the degree of non-equilibrium ($SK/ε$). By applying Weaver's well-recognized complexity framework, we classify turbulence into distinct physical regimes. This categorization identifies where local ML-RANS surrogates can generalize, clarifies where explicit scale resolution is physically mandatory, and highlights where physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) are most effective. Ultimately, this perspective establishes a physics-grounded framework for the flow-aware, successful deployment of AI/ML in turbulence.

Numerical Modeling of a Metallic Alloy Droplet Impact, Solidification, and Macrosegregation

Ehsan Amani, Ali Moinirad

For the first time, we study the effects of mass transfer on alloy droplet impact, solidification, and solute non-homogeneity or MacroSegregation (MS). For this purpose, a multi-physics model, including fully-coupled hydrodynamics of multiphase flow, wetting, conjugate heat transfer, phase change, and mass transfer, is established and validated carefully across several benchmarks. In addition, a concentration-flux-correction algorithm is proposed to conserve the solute mass within the droplet at the numerical discretization level. The validated model is subsequently used to explore the impact and solidification of an alloy droplet. The results show that accounting for mass transfer and solute transport improves the prediction of the dynamics of a solidifying splat, in a consistent manner with experimental observations, while this effect has been neglected in all previous studies. The analysis demonstrates that this improvement results from the flow sensitivity to the mushy region thickness, which is regulated by local solute concentration through the solidus and liquidus temperatures. Furthermore, analyses of the MS map identify several critical segregation regions most susceptible to defect initiation, such as cracking, including a long negatively segregated vein within the lamella, formed by the severe advective flow through the mush in the middle stages of the impact, a positively segregated region near the rim of the splat, appearing as a result of hook formation, and a major positively segregated zone at the top of the rim, which develops following the accumulation of the solute-rich melt sustained by washout from the lamella.

CFD Forge: A Physics-Constrained Agentic Framework for Autonomous CFD

Aakash Shailesh Oza, Ryan F. Johnson, Amir Barati Farimani

Setting up, running, and assessing a computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulation requires substantial manual effort, from meshing and solver setup to diagnosis and interpretation, and repeating it across design variants slows engineering iteration. We present CFD Forge, an agentic framework built around OpenFOAM that carries an engineering request through case construction, meshing, solver execution, diagnostics, and reporting. A large language model interprets the request, reads the simulation evidence, identifies likely causes of problems, and proposes actions from a closed set. Automation is only useful if its results can be trusted, so acceptance is not left to the model: deterministic checks, registered for each type of flow, measure explicit criteria and decide. We demonstrate the framework on a converging-diverging nozzle and a two-dimensional forward-facing step, including geometry and operating changes requested in text. A turbulent cube, run outside the agent loop, shows why the checks matter: the solver completed and the mean drag settled, but the lateral-force oscillations were still growing, so a retrospective assessment withheld acceptance. To test this separation, we replayed 36 recorded simulation states, some with deliberately altered diagnostics, through the same model in two setups. Judging from the evidence without the check results, the model accepted 12 of the 13 detectable faults in all three repeats; with CFD Forge's checks deciding, none was accepted. The two setups also differ in prompting, and a fixed rule reached the same verdicts, so the model's value lies in the open-ended work rather than the verdict. Case files, recorded model calls, and decisions are retained for audit. A model that does the work, paired with checks that decide what is accepted, is a step toward automated CFD that can be trusted in high-throughput design and active-learning loops.

Wave-driven geostrophic vortices in rotating turbulence

Vladimir Parfenyev, Sergey Vergeles

We perform direct numerical simulations of rotating turbulence to investigate the mechanisms responsible for the maintenance of large-scale geostrophic vortices. We confirm that the Reynolds stresses generated by inertial waves are consistent with the Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes balance of the large-scale flow and demonstrate the predicted cyclone-anticyclone asymmetry, with cyclones exhibiting stronger azimuthal velocities and more compact cores. Wave-field analysis provides evidence that geostrophic vortices selectively reflect and absorb inertial waves according to the sign of their angular momentum. Wave absorption occurs either through the formation of a critical layer ahead of the reflection point or through enhanced viscous dissipation associated with increasing local wavenumber. As rotation increases, fewer inertial waves undergo resonant absorption, reducing angular-momentum transfer to the vortices and weakening the geostrophic flow. Consequently, the fraction of energy contained in geostrophic modes depends non-monotonically on the rotation rate, demonstrating that sufficiently rapid rotation can ultimately suppress, rather than enhance, turbulence two-dimensionalization.

Preferential concentration in a periodically forced particle-laden turbulent planar jet

Mohammad Ahmad, Pierre Bragança, Christophe Cuvier, Martin Obligado

Periodic forcing is common in particle-laden jets, yet its influence on turbulence and inertial-particle clustering remains poorly understood. We investigate a turbulent planar jet at two Reynolds numbers, approximately 4950 and 9900 based on nozzle height, subjected to sinusoidal forcing at a frequency three orders of magnitude below the preferred jet instability. Hot-wire anemometry and high-speed particle imaging characterise the carrier turbulence, while Voronoi tessellations quantify preferential concentration of dilute inertial droplets. Despite its low frequency and moderate amplitude, forcing substantially modifies turbulence while leaving the mean-flow development nearly unchanged. The integral scale decreases by up to $40\%$, while forced and unforced jets retain the same time-averaged relationship between the dimensionless dissipation coefficient and Taylor-scale Reynolds number. Within the forcing cycle, velocity fluctuations and the Taylor microscale vary approximately in opposite phase, keeping the Taylor-scale Reynolds number within $\pm10\%$ of its cycle mean despite substantial variations in dissipation and Kolmogorov time scale. Particle clustering is even more sensitive, with forcing increasing mean preferential concentration and producing phase-dependent variations of $21$--$49\%$ of the cycle mean. Clustering peaks when the Kolmogorov time scale is largest, corresponding to the smallest particle Stokes number. Thus, markedly different clustering states occur at similar instantaneous Taylor-scale Reynolds numbers. These findings demonstrate that weak temporal modulation can control particle organisation without substantially altering the mean transporting flow.

Gen-PINNs: Generative Adversarial Physics Informed Neural Networks for solving partial differential equations

Muhammad M. Akmal, Kamy Sepehrnoori, Michael J. Pyrcz

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) are a widely used data-free method for solving Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) using machine learning. With recent advances in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), adversarial learning has shown strong capabilities for modeling complex data-driven problems; however, the use of GANs in deterministic physics-informed PDE solutions remains limited. In this work, we first identify limitations of standard PINNs for solving PDEs, including spectral bias, loss imbalance, and optimizer stagnation. We then propose Generative Adversarial Physics-Informed Neural Networks (Gen-PINNs), a unified deterministic residual-adversarial framework designed to improve data-free solutions of PDEs with sharp or shock-front behavior. The generator learns the underlying PDE solution using dynamically weighted physics-informed loss components, while separate discriminators evaluate complementary PDE residual features against ideal zero-residual states. The framework further develops and adapts several methodological components, including a Fourier representation for resolving high-frequency spatial content, an orthonormal spectral diagnostic for quantifying frequency-dependent solution errors, and a modified gradient-based dynamic weighting system for physics, initial-condition, boundary-condition, and adversarial loss objectives. Gen-PINNs is tested against standard PINNs on nonlinear and higher-order PDEs, including the Burgers, Allen-Cahn, and Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equations. The results demonstrate substantial improvements in accuracy and convergence across sharp-front, stiff, and higher-order PDE solutions, highlighting the potential of deterministic residual-adversarial learning as an effective approach for solving challenging nonlinear PDEs.

On the relationships between pressure geometrization and vortex identification

Han Tu, Hang Zhao, Qi Gao

The profound mathematical and conceptual analogy between pressure in fluid mechanics and gravity in general relativity motivates a novel geometric reinterpretation of flows. This article proposes a theoretical framework for geometrizing pressure by establishing a Newton-Cartan geometry and applying a conformal transformation to the spatial metric. The pressure field is reinterpreted as a geometric potential that induces an effective spatial curvature, encoded in the Ricci tensor Rij and the associated Einstein-like tensor Gij. Within this curved effective space, fluid particles under the exclusive influence of pressure follow geodesics, and the classical Euler equation is recovered in the weak-field limit. The pressure Poisson equation emerges naturally as a consequence of the geodesic equation and the Bianchi identity, revealing pressure as an instantaneous geometric constraint rather than an external force. This framework provides a unified geometric interpretation of widely used vortex identification criteria, demonstrating that the prevailing Eulerian and Lagrangian schemes correspond to different geometric invariants or projections of the effective curvature. In the degenerate two-dimensional (2D) limit, the effective spatial tensor vanishes identically, and the global integral of the total curvature vanishes as well. This topological constraint offers a novel interpretation of the dual-cascade scenario in 2D turbulence: the inverse energy cascade arises from geometric condensation of positive-curvature structures toward larger scales, while the forward enstrophy cascade results from geometric dispersion of negative-curvature structures toward smaller scales.

SAGE-PINN: A Singularity-free Axisymmetric Geometry-Encoded Physics-Informed Neural Network for Axisymmetric Multiphysics Flow in Cylindrical Coordinates

Raj Maurya, Joyprakash Akhuli, Doyel Pandey

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) provide a mesh-free framework for multiphysics modeling, yet fluid flow in axisymmetric cylindrical coordinates remains largely unexplored because the governing operators are singular at the axis and momentum and energy are strongly coupled through advection and buoyancy. We introduce SAGE-PINN, a Singularity-free Axisymmetric Geometry-Encoded Physics-Informed Neural Network for this class of problems. SAGE-PINN removes the coordinate singularity by construction: the parity-preserving variable $s=r^2$ and a modified stream-function representation eliminate explicit $1/r$ terms from the trained residuals and satisfy mass conservation identically. Boundary-fitted analytical ansätze enforce the boundary conditions exactly, while staged momentum-thermal-joint training, residual normalization, Péclet- and Richardson-number continuation, and a physics-scaled drift guard stabilize the coupled optimization. The framework is trained without labelled data, using only physics and boundary constraints, and its predictions are validated against finite-element reference solutions for steady nanofluid flow and heat transfer through a stenosed artery over a wide range of flow and thermal regimes, without case-specific tuning. Mass conservation remains at single-precision round-off, and over the central operating range the axial velocity and temperature are reproduced within $1.6\%$ and $9.1\%$ relative $L_2$ error, respectively. The main deterioration occurs only in the limiting regimes of strongly buoyancy-influenced slow flow and highly advection-dominated heat transfer. Embedding cylindrical geometry, conservation structure, and boundary physics directly into the network provides a robust route to PINN simulation of axisymmetric multiphysics flows.
