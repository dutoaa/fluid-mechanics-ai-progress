Boundary Layers, Displacement Flow, and the High-Frequency Response of Porous Media: Beyond the Viscous and Thermal Characteristic Lengths, Order by Order

D. Lafarge, A. Bouchendouka, Z. E. A. Fellah

The Johnson-Koplik-Dashen viscous characteristic length $Λ$ and its thermal analogue $Λ'$ introduced by Champoux and Allard govern the leading $O(ω^{-1/2})$ boundary-layer behaviour of fluid-saturated porous media, but omit higher microgeometric effects associated with interface curvature and field gradients. We develop the asymptotic high-frequency expansions of dynamic tortuosity and dynamic compressibility beyond the leading boundary-layer terms. By resolving the oscillatory Stokes and heat conduction problems in the thin boundary-layer limit, we establish a recursive hierarchy, which can be continued to any order, defining successive microgeometric parameters, given by wall integrals. Each order requires transport equations of WKB type across the boundary layer, closed by a non-secularity condition, together with one Neumann problem for the potential displacement flow that the boundary layer induces. Applied to the values fitted on highly absorbing foams, these expressions show that the negative surface parameter is consistent with strong in-parallel disorder of the pore sizes, whereas the large positive third-order term points to strong constrictions or enlargements along part of the flow paths. The thermal parameters reduce to geometrical quantities: the integral mean curvature of the pore walls, a Willmore-type measure of their departure from umbilicity and, at the next order, this measure weighted by the mean curvature. Using the Avellaneda-Torquato relaxation-mode representation, we show that all these parameters are spectral data of the distribution of relaxation times. For isolated sharp edges across which the fluid flows, the singular terms of the formal expansion cancel, and the description persists with apparent, logarithmically varying values of the parameters.

CFD-driven symbolic regression of RANS transition models

Louenas Zemmour, Xavier Gloerfelt, Paola Cinnella

Laminar-turbulent transition governs skin friction, heat transfer and losses in turbomachinery, yet Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) transition models rely on empirical correlations and hand-calibrated activation functions whose structural deficiencies cannot be resolved by recalibration. We propose a CFD-driven sparse symbolic regression framework to learn interpretable, closed-form corrections to the production and destruction activation functions in the intermittency equation of the local, Galilean-invariant $k$-$ω$-$γ$ model of Ge et al. Each correction combines a polynomial of a local pressure-gradient parameter, controlling amplitude and sign, with a bounded exponential of local flow features, controlling spatial support. This construction bounds the corrections without ad hoc limiters. Coefficients are identified by gradient-free optimization with the RANS solver in the loop, accelerated by POD-Kriging field surrogates, using mean-velocity data from wall-resolved large-eddy simulations (LES) of the ERCOFTAC T3 flat-plate cases. Elastic-net hyperparameters are selected automatically through bi-level optimization with leave-one-case-out cross-validation. The resulting model retains 11 of 21 candidate terms, compared with 20 under conventional grid-search tuning, while achieving comparable or slightly better accuracy on training and unseen cases. Both approaches recover the same leading-order correction. Applied without retraining to the transonic LS89 turbine cascade, far beyond the training envelope, the augmented model remains numerically robust and improves heat-transfer predictions at all four operating points, reducing errors by up to 70% where the baseline predicts premature transition under high freestream turbulence. Residual errors in transition onset and length highlight the limits of training on flat-plate data alone.

Microdroplet Generator Surrogate Enhancement and Inverse Design Through an Uncertainty-Guided Framework

David Gabriel Harispe, Matías Gerard, Pablo A. Kler

Microfluidic droplet generation is critical for many applications, yet efficient device design in high-dimensional parameter spaces remains computationally prohibitive due to complex multiphase flow dynamics. This work presents a unified methodology that couples adaptive surrogate enhancement with simulation-driven inverse design. High-fidelity Volume-of-Fluid simulations provide ground-truth data, while neural network ensembles predict flow regimes and droplet diameters. Ensemble-based uncertainty quantification identifies epistemic uncertainty using consensus levels for regime classification and the coefficient of variation for diameter prediction, revealing that uncertainty concentrates near regime transition boundaries where physics is highly sensitive to parameter perturbations. A physics-informed enrichment strategy targets these critical zones and addresses class imbalances by deploying additional targeted simulations and retraining the surrogates forming the ensembles. A framework that integrates Particle Swarm Optimization for constrained inverse design, balancing accuracy with predictive confidence and physical consistency is therefore proposed. Validation on independent test datasets demonstrates that the enriched ensembles achieve higher accuracy, with the fraction of predictions within the 0-3% error range on droplet diameter nearly doubling, and high-error cases (above 10%) dropping substantially. The inverse design procedure reliably identifies physically realizable configurations meeting target droplet sizes. This approach significantly reduces computational costs while ensuring reliable predictions, offering a framework with potential applicability to the optimization of a broad range of microfluidic systems.

FlowReconBench: Accuracy, calibration and cost of learned and classical flow reconstruction from sparse sensors

Nicolas Tricard, Linzheng Wang, Jason Chen, Sili Deng

Reconstructing full flow fields from sparse sensors requires prior assumptions about unmeasured regions and physical variables. Yet it remains unclear when learned models outperform classical estimators and which aspects of their predictions can be trusted. FlowReconBench evaluates ten learned and five classical methods on three regimes: a laminar cylinder wake across Reynolds numbers, two-dimensional Kolmogorov turbulence and a large-scale three-dimensional (3D) isotropic turbulence. Methods share leakage-controlled splits and sensor draws and are scored on per-channel error, ensemble spread, calibration, spectra and cost. The leading method changes for each regime. Classical methods perform strongly in low-order and dense-sensing regimes. On the cylinder wake, linear stochastic estimation and gappy proper orthogonal decomposition lead, including for unobserved pressure. In 3D, inverse-distance weighting matches the best learned methods on observed channels. Data-driven methods become superior at decreasing sensor density or poor sensor observability. On Kolmogorov flow, continuous ranked probability score and error yield different method rankings: generative models offer broader but underdispersed ensembles, while deterministic models prioritize pointwise error. Generative models balance this tradeoff through their sampler budget, with a larger budget improving ensemble quality at higher computational cost. In 3D, scaling tests demonstrate that queryable methods are flat in memory, while full-grid construction methods run out of memory. Observing only 2 of 4 fields leaves the problem kinematically underconstrained, yet a latent model can reproduce structural similarities to the ground truth.

A coupled lattice Boltzmann model for pore-scale mushy-layer dynamics with application to sea ice

Nicola Trippella, Dario Maggiolo

A pore-scale lattice Boltzmann model is developed for reactive transport in phase-changing porous media, coupling solute transport in the liquid with conjugate heat transfer across the solid and liquid phases. Two methodological developments are introduced: a novel link-based Volume-of-Pixel formulation for direction-dependent interface motion, and a generalized Robin boundary treatment for solute redistribution at moving interfaces. The phase-change formulation is validated against thermal benchmarks, while the Robin treatment is assessed through literature test cases, exhibiting convergence rates between first and second order. The coupled model is then applied to a local pore-scale segment of a sea ice brine channel in a purely diffusive regime. The simulations recover the expected ideal mushy-layer behaviour under appropriate scale separation. At sea-ice-relevant concentration ratios, thermal perturbations produce relatively large variations in brine salinity, causing freezing rather than melting to reshape the pore geometry in a non-uniform way. The resulting framework provides a pore-scale numerical tool for resolving coupled temperature, solute redistribution, and phase-boundary dynamics in mushy layers.

Interscale energy transfer in a propeller-driven homogeneous and isotropic turbulence generation device

Luciano Hergenreder, Serge Simoëns, Cristián Escauriaza, Wernher Brevis

We investigate the interscale energy dynamics of nearly homogeneous isotropic turbulence (HIT) generated in a spherically forced, propeller-driven device. From a spatial perspective, by employing the Kármán-Howarth-Monin (KHM) framework, we analyze the scale-by-scale energy transfer without isotropy assumptions. In contrast to other forcing strategies, where regions with inverse and forward cascades often coexist in scale space, we show that spherical forcing produces a purely forward energy cascade across the entire scale space. While the energy distribution remains highly isotropic and orientation-averaged structure functions align with Kolmogorov's two-thirds law, the energy flux exhibits a directional dependence, suggesting a higher sensitivity to residual forcing asymmetries. Regarding the temporal dynamics, the reduced mean flow generated by this device allows us to investigate the temporal variability in conditions where Taylor's frozen turbulence hypothesis does not apply. Our results provide experimental validation of Tennekes' Eulerian frequency spectrum, where intermediate scales evidence an inertial-convective range with statistics determined by the integral velocity scale and the dissipation rate. Finally, we adopt a global approach to investigate whether the main turbulent scales can be linked to external experimental parameters. We propose a framework that captures the propeller frequency dependence of the main turbulent variables obtained in our measurements and those of previously reported HIT chambers. These findings contribute to the understanding of how external forcing influences the complex interscale energy dynamics of turbulent flows from a spatial, temporal, and global perspective.

Learned Surrogates of Three-Dimensional Magnetohydrodynamic Turbulence

Semih Kacmaz, E. A. Huerta

Three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence, the flow of astrophysical and fusion plasmas, is costly to simulate. We present, to our knowledge, the first learned surrogates of three-dimensional resistive MHD turbulence, and compare three standard backbones in that role: a convolutional UNet, a shifted-window transformer and a Fourier neural operator. They are trained as autoregressive emulators of decaying MHD turbulence at a resolution of $128^3$ and Reynolds numbers of 1000, 3000 and 6000, on 500 simulations per Reynolds number, each with one two-stage procedure, single steps and then short rollouts, and stopped by one rule. Over a window of about one large-eddy turnover time, the UNet and the transformer reach relative velocity errors of 0.018 to 0.053 and keep the current sheets, the enstrophy, the current density and the energy spectra of the simulation; which of the two is the more accurate depends on the Reynolds number, the UNet leading at 3000 and 6000 and the transformer at 1000. Applied with the same backbones and training procedure to two public benchmarks, the models reach the lowest error reported to date on the isotropic turbulence set of FactFormer, 39% below the best published model. On the compressible MHD set of The Well, their rollout errors are a third to a half below those of the best of The Well's baselines. They also match a 1.3 B-parameter pretrained foundation model in its rollout windows, with 20-62 times fewer parameters. The code, the configuration of every model, the evaluation scripts and the data generator with the seed of every simulation will be released upon publication.

A physics-informed neural network method for pressure-driven turbine-blade optimization

Yuan Huang, Francesca di Mare

We develop a physics-informed method for aerodynamic shape optimisation of turbine blades, treating the flow field and blade geometry as coupled unknowns. The method translates prescribed surface-pressure distributions into geometric modifications through pressure-target continuation and alternating flow-geometry updates. A differentiable representation of the blade surfaces allows their positions and orientations to be optimised within the current flow field, followed by flow re-adaptation to the modified boundaries. The inverse formulation uses the two-dimensional steady compressible Euler equations as physics constraints and is evaluated on the LS89 turbine cascade using 28 pressure-target points derived from experimental data. Starting from a modified profile and a warm-started flow network, the final pressure-side and suction-side root-mean-square fitting errors are 1.13% and 2.21% of inlet total pressure, respectively. Independent forward verification with SharC reproduces the principal surface-pressure trends of the experimental measurements, with remaining differences near the trailing edge. These results demonstrate the translation of experimental-origin loading data into geometric blade modifications through coordinated optimisation of the flow field and moving boundaries.

Designing turbulence through hierarchies

Flavio Tuteri, André Freitas

Turbulence is defined by a set of emergent and singular phenomena: a constant-flux energy cascade, intermittency, a dissipation rate that survives the vanishing of viscosity, and the spontaneous loss of determinism of the inviscid limit. Which physical ingredients produce them remains unclear, because in fluids they always come together with incompressibility, spatial structure and vortex dynamics. Here we show that a hierarchy of inertia, coupled by the purely geometric nonlinearity of rigid constraints, is enough. A planar chain of rigid pendula whose lengths and masses decrease geometrically, coupled only through nearest-neighbor constraints, develops a far-from-equilibrium cascade that replaces thermal equipartition, with statistically local and time-irreversible energy transfer despite intrinsically nonlocal coupling. Its fluctuations are intermittent, yet self-similarity is restored by a hidden scale symmetry. As the viscosity vanishes, the dissipation rate saturates at a finite value, and thermal noise consistent with fluctuation-dissipation balance is amplified from the smallest to the largest scales within a fraction of a large-scale period. Because the hierarchy can be tuned and the same dynamics can be realized mechanically or electrically, turbulence becomes a property that can be designed and measured in the laboratory.

Experimental evaluation of Kolmogorov's first similarity hypothesis for shear-dependent viscosity fluids

Luciano Hergenreder, Serge Simoëns, Wernher Brevis

We experimentally investigate the applicability of Kolmogorov's first similarity hypothesis to shear-dependent viscosity fluids. We assess whether dissipative range statistics exhibit a self-similar behavior in nearly homogeneous and isotropic turbulence, using stereo particle image velocimetry in aqueous Carbopol solutions across two concentrations and a range of forcing conditions. We observe that, when normalized by the mean viscosity and mean dissipation rate, second- and third-order structure functions collapse across the different experimental conditions and exhibit functional forms compatible with classical Newtonian results. These observations are in general agreement with the recent numerical results of Rosti [J. Fluid Mech. 1012, R5 (2025)], who proposed that Kolmogorov's similarity hypotheses can be extended to shear-thinning fluids by considering dissipation scales based on the mean viscosity, the mean dissipation rate, and the flow index.

A Drift-Diffusion Approach for Pearson Type IV Heavy-Tail Distributions

Daniele Belardinelli, Simone Benella, Mirko Stumpo, Giuseppe Consolini

Non-Gaussian stationary fluctuations with heavy tails and finite-order moment cutoffs are ubiquitous in non-equilibrium systems. Here, we derive the Pearson type IV distribution as the stationary solution of a standardized Fokker-Planck equation with linear drift and quadratic diffusion. We establish necessary and sufficient conditions for its emergence and show that the linear-quadratic structure, although sufficient, is not necessary. We also give explicit closed expressions for the cumulative distribution function, a moment recurrence relation, skewness, kurtosis, and mode. From a physical perspective, our results reveal that heavy tails and asymmetry originate from distinct features of state-dependent diffusion: its local nonlinear curvature and gradient, respectively. In particular, asymmetric stationary fluctuations can emerge even in the presence of a symmetric linear restoring drift. This framework provides a general stochastic description of asymmetric heavy-tailed fluctuations in non-equilibrium systems, including space plasma turbulence.

Fluid memory beyond creeping flow: a history kernel for a sphere at finite Reynolds numbers

M. D. Łasica, J. G. M. Kuerten, S. K. F. Stoter

Predicting the history force on a sphere beyond the creeping-flow limit remains challenging. Existing formulations have been derived or calibrated for selected flow histories, with limited evidence of their predictive accuracy under more general conditions. We develop a systematic approach to extract the history kernel of a fixed sphere in spatially uniform, unsteady flow from axisymmetric Navier-Stokes simulations of finite-duration acceleration and deceleration ramps at initial Reynolds numbers from 0.2 to 200. The resulting kernel surface recovers the Basset limit at short lags and in the creeping-flow limit and is continued beyond the resolved lags using far-wake asymptotics. At higher Reynolds numbers, the kernel develops non-monotonic behaviour at intermediate lags, associated with delayed adjustment of the boundary layer and wake. For the cases tested, the present kernel substantially improves history-force predictions for finite ramps, oscillatory forcing and non-periodic composite velocity histories relative to the literature formulations considered, without case-specific adjustment. These findings indicate that lag and Reynolds number capture the dominant memory response.

On the role of grazing atmospheric particles in hypersonic boundary-layer receptivity

Sean Dungan, Christoph Brehm

The role of small atmospheric particulates in the initiation of the laminar-turbulent transition is investigated for the HIFLIER vehicle during Mach 6 ascent condition. High-fidelity simulations are used to examine the disturbance wavepackets generated by particles following either grazing trajectories through the boundary layer or trajectories that lead to impact and rebound at the cone surface. Grazing particles that penetrate less than halfway through the boundary layer are found to generate wavepackets with amplitudes comparable to those produced by wall-impinging particles. To identify the receptivity mechanisms responsible for this behaviour, a multiple-scales asymptotic formulation is developed that accounts for the extended streamwise region over which a particle interacts with the boundary layer. The analysis shows that a grazing particle can generate a strong disturbance response when crossing the critical layer, where the streamwise-momentum component of the adjoint mode is largest and the boundary layer is most receptive. Thus, direct surface impact is not required for atmospheric particulates to excite dynamically significant instability waves. Root-mean-square disturbance estimates for submicron grazing particles predict transition-onset locations close to those measured in flight, suggesting they may have contributed significantly to the onset of transition on HIFLIER at the trajectory condition considered.

Readable Is Not Steerable: Pre-Registered Tests of Physical Structure in Learned Fluid Representations

Manvendra Modgil

Self-supervised world models are often said to organize their latents around physical quantities. We test this on 2D decaying Navier-Stokes flow in three pre-registered studies, each tested once on held-out data. In Study 1, a joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) trained to predict the flow fails the pre-registered criteria for organizing its latent around fluid quantities better than an untrained network of the same architecture (not supported); a JEPA trained on non-physical linear dynamics and a static invariance model that never sees time do as well or better. In Study 2, plain autoencoders that render the field make enstrophy prominent in their slowest unsupervised latent directions (best r 0.80-0.86 vs 0.38 untrained; fresh seeds, sealed data). Adding VICReg lowers this prominence in every comparison, though not by the pre-registered margin for vorticity, while raising the autoencoders' linear decodability (partially supported). In Study 3, we build steerable directions ("dials") by routing: the decoder gets overall scale only through a small conditioning code, while its other pathway carries a degraded copy of spectral shape. Label-free rules find a loudness dial in all 8 trained models and a shape dial in 3 of 5 fresh seeds on sealed data, against a pre-registered bar of 4; neither control shows a shape dial (partially supported). Across the studies, what a latent contains and what can be controlled through it come apart. In exploration, a supervised direction reading spectral shape almost perfectly moved nothing when steered. During the build, the loudness dial had to be found by its effect on the output (closest to a pure rescaling), not by data variance; for shape, a variance rule and an effect-constrained rule each worked in 3 of 5 seeds. The program used about 70 logged hours on one consumer GPU; all pre-registrations, deviations, code and outputs are public.
