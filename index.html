Automated Discovery of Physically Interpretable Subgrid Length Scales for Detached Eddy Simulation via Agentic AI

Jiawei Chen, Xiang I. A. Yang, Ping He

Traditional turbulence-model development relies heavily on expert knowledge and is time-consuming. This study develops an agentic AI framework, DEScover (automated Discovery of DES closures), to explore subgrid length scales for detached eddy simulation (DES). The user provides only a modeling idea: the length scale is the grid width normal to the local vortex axis. Four large language model (LLM) agents then propose, review, implement, and evaluate candidate formulations and generate scripts for CFD simulations. The Discovery Agent infers the vortex axis from various vortex-identification methods. The candidates are tested on four cases spanning canonical and engineering flows: a flat plate, a backward-facing step, a free shear layer, and an iced airfoil. For all test cases, the best candidate consistently outperforms the standard length scale based on the maximum cell size. Its accuracy is similar to, or slightly better than, that of the expert-designed shear-layer-adapted length scale. Each agentic discovery run takes about 25 minutes on average, excluding the CFD simulations. These results show that LLM-based reasoning can significantly accelerate the discovery of physically interpretable turbulence models and reduce reliance on manual expert formulation.

A Priori Estimation of the Non-Linear Compression Potential and Quantification of Autoencoder Non-Linearity in Turbulent Flows

Niklas Alexander Pissarski, Juan Pablo Murcia Leon, Søren Juhl Andersen

Data-driven reduced-order modelling (ROM) of turbulent flows is based on an efficient and physical low-dimensional representation of the flow process. Such a dimension reduction can be achieved using proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) or non-linear autoencoders. Capturing the inherent non-linearities in turbulent flows offers the potential for improved compression rates. This work proposes the local tangent residual (LTR) as an a priori estimate of the potential benefit of non-linear dimension reduction for a given dataset. The LTR is based on the local geometry of the data points, requiring neither prior model training nor the definition of a threshold parameter. For all datasets investigated in this work, namely, two vortex street cases and the turbulent wake flow of a wind turbine, the dominant trends in autoencoder compression performance are reproduced by the proposed LTR. The results are further contextualised through the decoder linearisation error (DLE). The DLE quantifies the non-linearity of the trained decoder by comparing it to a linearised surrogate model, which enables physical interpretability of the non-linearities. Together, a large non-linear compression potential in the turbulent flow, as quantified by the LTR, is corroborated by a large DLE, i.e., a pronounced non-linearity captured in the trained autoencoder. The LTR thus enables practitioners in turbulent flow modelling to determine a priori whether non-linear compression is worthwhile, avoiding the substantial computational cost of autoencoder architecture search and hyperparameter tuning in cases where POD already suffices.

Assisted Turbulence Modeling via Data Assimilation and Machine Learning for Turbomachinery Flows

Tom Moussie, Pierric Joseph, Marcello Meldi

This work presents the development and assessment of a hybrid framework coupling numerical simulation, experimental samples, data assimilation (DA) and machine learning (ML) to investigate the flow within an axial compressor stage. The methodology first implements a Multi-Fidelity Ensemble Kalman Filter (MFEnKF) which integrates experimental PIV data with a two-fidelity numerical simulation setup, combining steady-state RANS on a fixed grid with unsteady hybrid RANS/LES simulations on a moving grid. This data assimilation step successfully reduces discrepancies between experiments and CFD realizations. Besides, it provides additional high fidelity samples when compared with the available PIV data. This enriched database is used to train machine learning (ML) tools, with the objective to obtain black-box forcing terms to be included in the discretized form of the Navier-Stokes equations. These models are expected to preserve the accuracy of the DA results while not longer requiring the input of experimental data. The neural network architecture used here maps local physical features such as the velocity, the velocity gradients and the turbulent viscosity, to obtain the volume forcing terms. The analysis of the performance of such ML models shows that global accuracy is preserved but localized increased errors in specific regions of the compressor, such as close to the blade tips, can exceed those observed from the baseline RANS simulations. To resolve this issue, a Mixture of Experts (MoE) method is employed, which classifies and combines the trained ML tools for different flow regions. The discrepancy with experimental data of this method is systematically lower than the one observed for the baseline RANS solution and very similar to the one obtained with the MFEnKF.

Synthetic Dataset Generation, Physical Consistency and Active Learning for Shape-Informed Surrogate Modeling of Hemodynamics

Francesco Romor, Richard Schussnig, Charles A. Taylor, Gerhard A. Holzapfel, Alfonso Caiazzo

Model order reduction has the potential to enable real-time patient-specific assessment of cardiovascular flows. However, it still faces significant challenges stemming from the geometric variability of vascular anatomies. Building upon the SynthAorta dataset (Bosnjak et al., IEEE Trans. Med. Imag. 45(1), 2026) of physiological aortic geometries, we generate an extensive dataset of physiologically consistent computational fluid dynamics solutions through an automatic boundary-condition adjustment procedure. A common reference geometry allows all patient-specific anatomies to be represented by displacement fields, providing point-to-point correspondence without the need for geometric registration. Based on this dataset, we develop Graph U-Net and Transformer U-Net surrogate models to predict velocity and pressure fields at peak systole. We further investigate two strategies to enhance data efficiency and prediction accuracy: active learning and differentiable physical consistency constraints based on the finite element residual. The results show that active learning is strongly regime-dependent: while it offers little benefit over random sampling during early training, it improves performance during fine-tuning through gradient- and subspace-based acquisition strategies. Similarly, physical consistency constraints prove to be substantially more effective as a fine-tuning objective than as a regularizer applied throughout training. Finally, out-of-distribution studies demonstrate that the primary limitation of surrogate models lies in the geometric support of the underlying parametrization rather than in the neural network architectures themselves. The proposed framework combines synthetic dataset generation, geometry-aware surrogate modeling, physics-informed optimization, and active learning into a scalable workflow for inter-patient hemodynamic prediction.

The surrogate enables efficient prediction of mean flow fields downstream of marine hydrokinetic (MHK) turbine arrays at a fraction of the computational cost of high fidelity simulations, facilitating turbine layout optimization in natural waterways where bathymetry, turbulence, and turbine interactions influence the available energy resource. We demonstrate the capabilities of the model at a real world tidal river site under ebb tide conditions. Complex bed bathymetry and planform geometry generate substantial background turbulence that can obscure turbine wake signatures. POD is employed to extract relevant flow structures, improving the ability of the ML model to learn wake dynamics. First, LES is performed at the tidal site to generate training data. POD is then applied to decompose the instantaneous LES fields into spatial modes. Selected modes are used to train a three dimensional convolutional encoder decoder network to predict time averaged velocity fields. This selective filtering reduces the influence of dominant background fluctuations while preserving information relevant to turbine wakes. At equivalent network size and training cost, the POD filtered surrogate reduces the mean squared error by three orders of magnitude compared with the same network trained on unfiltered LES data.

DiffVortex: A quantum-inspired fast solver for incompressible flows

Yu Jun Loo, Silas Alben, Shravan Veerapaneni

Matrix-product-state (MPS) and quantized tensor-train (QTT) representations offer a promising framework for computational fluid dynamics, with logarithmic run time and memory scaling in mesh size when bond dimensions remain bounded. Existing MPS-based solvers are Eulerian: they compress velocity fields and enforce immersed-body boundary conditions through static masks or body-fitted coordinates. Moving or flexible bodies complicate this approach: deformation requires updating MPS masks every time step, while filamentary vorticity in high-Reynolds-number flows may resist low-rank compression. We propose a complementary approach: compress the Biot--Savart kernel rather than the flow state, evolve vorticity using Lagrangian vortex particles, and represent immersed bodies by bound vortex sheets on Lagrangian surface points. Our main theoretical result is a lifting theorem: QTT rank $r$ of the kernel implies matrix-product-operator (MPO) rank at most $2^d r$ for the corresponding convolution operator in $d$ dimensions, independent of grid resolution. This bound is sharp for our interleaved ordering. With quantics tensor cross interpolation, the moment-to-local stage of a single-level fast multipole method becomes a precomputed tensor-network contraction with quasi-linear complexity, retaining Lagrangian flexibility for immersed bodies. Our PyTorch implementation (\texttt{DiffVortex}) is end-to-end differentiable, runs on GPUs, and handles $6.7\times10^{7}$ particles on a single device. We validate against reference vortex-method results for an impulsively started cylinder and exact and reference solutions for inviscid elliptical vortices. We also demonstrate two-dimensional flows past rigidly moving, multiple, and deforming bodies, including gradients propagated through the full time integration.

CARS maps a centerline with per-point lumen radius and a scalar inlet flow rate to pressure and velocity at arbitrary query points. The attention focal set is the case's own centerline, so geometry fusion costs $O(N n_f)$ rather than $O(N^2)$ in the number of evaluation points. Fractional flow reserve derived from the predicted pressure matches the CFD label at Pearson $r = 0.941/0.993$ and reproduces the $\mathrm{FFR}\le 0.8$ decision in $97.7\%/96.3\%$ of held-out cases. The predicted velocity does not satisfy continuity closely enough for derived quantities, and the weights are territory-specific.

Swarmlet: a GPU-resident reformulated vortex particle method for coupled multirotor aerodynamics and flight dynamics

Wyatt James Horne

We present Swarmlet, a GPU solver for the unsteady aerodynamics and flight dynamics of one or many multirotor vehicles. The wake is a meshless, evolving-core vortex particle field in the reformulated (rVPM) large-eddy formulation, coupled to rotor models of three fidelities, six-degree-of-freedom vehicles and flight control. Particles carry a smooth core of compact support whose field outside four core radii is exactly the singular Biot-Savart field, so the multipole far field of the treecode carries no regularization error. The subfilter closure is a dynamic eddy viscosity on a fixed filter width, applied by core spreading with a Lagrangian exchange of its local excess under an explicit stability bound, and the strengths are relaxed by projection; global corrections make both the exchange and the relaxation conserve total vorticity and linear impulse. All rotor models shed connected bound circulation, the disks along the arcs swept in each step however long, so the wake receives the lift impulse. The solver agrees with direct summation to $5\times 10^{-7}$ and converges at third order in time. Without calibration, an actuator-line model that includes its own near sheet predicts the hover thrust of a DJI 9443 propeller within 2%. With calibrated section polars it reproduces a Crazyflie propeller's thrust and torque within 3.2% at three speeds and the near wake of a hovering quadrotor with relative $L^2$ differences of 0.08-0.21 against particle image velocimetry, while the wake keeps the delivered impulse. A closed-loop formation of 100 quadrotors, run at a coarser setting on one GPU, illustrates the coupled method at swarm scale.

Tandem jets in crossflow: the enclosed-recirculation momentum exchange and the unsteady shear-layer dynamics

Xunchen Liu

Twin tandem jets in crossflow are examined by 40\ kHz time-resolved particle image velocimetry (PIV) over nine spacing--velocity-ratio conditions ($L=2d$--$4d$, $r\approx3$--7; 68 twin cases, 11 single-jet records). The front jet is diluted: its trajectory lies below the single-jet reference, with a near-field segment up to 40\% shorter, and its velocity ratio falls to an effective value $r_{\mathrm{eff}}<r$, while the shielded rear jet rises above the reference. This paper identifies the carrier of that dilution and the unsteady dynamics that sustains it. The rear-jet blockage closes a gap recirculation whose reversed flow puts the two gap-side layers in the counter-current shear-layer configuration, the classical route to enhanced entrainment. The measured Reynolds stress and Prandtl mixing length show that the absolute exchange is nearly independent of the velocity ratio and exceeds the single-jet leeward zone like-for-like by up to $1.5\times$ (zone peak, across conditions) and $2.8\times$ (area-integrated) at $r\approx7$. The gain comes from an enlarged mixing scale rather than from a steeper shear, and the front-jet near-field shortening follows the absolute exchange transport rather than the twin/single enhancement ratio. The turbulent term exceeds the pressure term in eight of the nine conditions, and the flux budget reads the dilution as a limited mass addition plus a momentum loss to the crossflow; the evidence remains observational. The gap-side layers remain convectively unstable---DMD and SPOD find no growing mode within the resolvable band and record length---so the exchange is carried by convecting vortex trains rather than by a self-excited global mode.

Extracting target-related flow structures using a time-lagged cross-correlation ranking criterion

Xuran Bao, Zhongkun Jin

Structures with large global contributions to a flow field are not necessarily those that are most closely associated with a specified engineering observable. We propose a time-lagged cross-correlation (TCC) ranking criterion to extract target-related dynamical structures from a fixed modal expansion while preserving existing spatial modes and temporal coefficients. The criterion ranks mode groups by the mean squared norm of cross-responses between their flow contributions and the observable over a lag window. It is tested separately using dynamic mode decomposition (DMD) and proper orthogonal decomposition (POD). TCC-DMD analysis shows that TCC recovers three low-contribution target structures from a dominant background in a synthetic flow. The reconstruction error for the target subfield is 2.15\%. In the cylinder wake, different force observables select structures associated with the vortex-shedding fundamental, slow drag variations, and the second harmonic. In the cavity case, selected modes connect two main frequency bands of the target observable to distinct spatial amplitude and phase distributions. TCC-POD analysis shows that the criterion minimises the weighted truncation error of the correlation operator among equally sized subsets of the fixed orthogonal basis. A comparison with canonical correlation decomposition further reveals differences between fixed-direction selection and correlation-based basis construction in the spatial structure and retention of flow information. Lag aggregation can improve structure identification when zero-lag association is insufficient. The gains depend on the cross-response distribution and window selection. TCC separates target-related ranking from mode-set construction, enabling comparison of spatial structures associated with different observables within the same modal expansion.

Extended-regularized lattice Boltzmann method

Luís Orlando Emerich dos Santos, Diogo Nardelli Siebert

Regularized lattice Boltzmann methods improve numerical stability by projecting the nonequilibrium distribution onto its hydrodynamic second-order Hermite sector and filtering the remaining kinetic modes. We introduce a separate relaxation time for the complete complementary kinetic subspace. The resulting extended-regularized lattice Boltzmann method (E-RLBM) interpolates continuously between the Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook and regularized collision operators while retaining a two-rate implementation. A Chapman-Enskog expansion shows that the isothermal Navier-Stokes equations are recovered with kinematic viscosity $ν=c_s^2(τ-Δt/2)$, independently of the kinetic relaxation time $τ_{\rm nh}$, whereas $τ_{\rm nh}$ changes higher-order truncation errors. For linear transverse shear, its first effect is a fourth-derivative term whose coefficient depends on the direction of propagation relative to the lattice. This fourth-order coefficient is affine in $τ_{\rm nh}$. For linear shear on D2Q9 and $τ_{\rm nh}$ between $τ$ and $Δt$, it lies between the corresponding BGK and RLBM values in every propagation direction. Stability maps for the decaying Taylor-Green vortex and flow past a rectangular obstacle show that an appropriate kinetic relaxation substantially enlarges the stable parameter domain. In a Taylor-Green convergence test with consistent initialization, all three operators converge at second order, and their error differences follow the hyperviscous coefficient for diagonal shear waves: the E-RLBM error is about 6% above the BGK error and about 40% below the RLBM error; in a double shear layer at Re = 80000, the E-RLBM follows the RLBM convergence curve. The proposed collision operator therefore provides a low-cost means of controlling nonhydrodynamic modes without introducing a full multiple-relaxation-time basis.

Neural Operator Splitting for Fluid Dynamics in Complex Geometry

Jianxiang Li, Xi Deng, Hong Chul Nam, Anima Anandkumar

Solving dynamical systems in complex and diverse geometries remains challenging for both numerical solvers and learning-based surrogate models. We propose Neural Operator Splitting, a framework for learning fluid dynamics while preserving the physical structure of fractional-step numerical schemes.
