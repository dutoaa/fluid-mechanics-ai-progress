Singular layer neural networks for the Navier-Stokes equations at small viscosity

Teng-Yuan Chang, Gung-Min Gie, Youngjoon Hong, Chang-Yeol Jung

We develop neural network methods for the two-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equations at a small viscosity $\varepsilon > 0$, whose accuracy is uniform in $\varepsilon$ and whose residual is evaluated on a grid that does not resolve the vorticity boundary layer. In the vorticity-stream function formulation with the slip boundary conditions, a vorticity layer of width $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ forms along the walls. The trial function of the singular layer Physics-Informed Neural Network (sl-PINN) contains the corrector of the asymptotic analysis, together with the law that fixes its amplitude at the wall. The amplitude is tied to the wall trace of the network itself, so that the method requires no a priori knowledge of the limit solution. The sl-PINN keeps the error uniform in $\varepsilon$ over six decades, for the Stokes and the Navier-Stokes equations alike, although in the latter the wall trace travels with the flow and is not known in advance. An enrichment that uses the thin scale but leaves the profile and the amplitude of the layer free deteriorates as $\varepsilon\to0$. Since the corrector carries the layer analytically, the Laplacian in the residual can be discretized by a finite volume flux difference on a coarse grid. The resulting singular layer Finite Volume Neural Network (sl-FVNN) attains the same uniform accuracy as the sl-PINN, to within the scatter of the random seed, at about half the cost of a training step. The combination of the asymptotic corrector with the coarse-grid finite volume discretization is the main result of this article.

Stochastic Representation-Inspired Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Incompressible Fluid Flows

Zihao Guo, Zhongmin Qian

The effectiveness of neural networks in solving partial differential equations depends not only on their approximation capability but also on the suitability of their representations for the underlying solutions. Motivated by stochastic integral representations of incompressible fluid flows and the associated Biot--Savart kernel, we propose a Biot--Savart-informed (i.e. physics-informed) neural network (BS-PINN) for the incompressible Navier--Stokes equations. By extracting a rational profile from a regularized Biot--Savart kernel, we construct a smooth activation function with fixed and learnable kernel scales. Both variants retain the conventional velocity--pressure formulation and physics-informed training framework, allowing the influence of the activation function to be examined independently. We evaluate the method on five benchmarks, including Kovasznay flow, cylinder wake, three-dimensional Beltrami flow, Taylor--Green vortex, and double shear layer. Across 21 benchmark--architecture comparisons, both variants consistently reduce the combined velocity error relative to the conventional $\tanh$-based PINN, achieving mean relative error reductions of 31.7\% and 33.2\%, respectively. Improvements are also observed in reported pressure and vorticity errors. Notably, the double-shear-layer experiments demonstrate improved resolution of localized shear structures and, for BS-PINN-F, qualitatively clearer vortex-roll-up patterns in the long-time simulation. These findings highlight the potential of incorporating analytical flow structures into neural-network representations for incompressible flow simulations.

AeroZoo: A Benchmark of Well-Posed Flow Problems for Foundation Models of Aerodynamics

Peter Sharpe, Carmelo Gonzales, Corey Adams, Mehdi Ataei, Sanjay Choudhry

Neural surrogates for computational fluid dynamics (CFD) are trained and ranked on datasets that each vary one design family in one flow domain. Such datasets cannot test the least that an aerodynamic foundation model must do: predict CFD-quality incompressible flow in seconds for new shapes in new enclosures. On DrivAerML, larger models and longer training barely lower Transolver's error; doubling the training cases cuts it by about 12% up to 400 cases, exhausting the dataset. We introduce AeroZoo, a benchmark and training corpus of 3,815 time-mean solutions to the unsteady Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) equations with a transition model, across seven geometry families, eight enclosure configurations and Reynolds numbers from $10^3$ to $10^8$. Each sample poses a well-posed PDE problem in the form a solver receives it: the boundary meshes grouped by condition type, the prescribed values and the fluid properties. The 2,816 samples with a stable time mean form the canonical benchmark: two tasks, engineering metrics and 22 frozen splits, disjoint in geometry, that hold out geometry families, enclosures, boundary-condition layouts and Reynolds ranges. On the random split, boundary-condition tokens cut GeoTransolver's median velocity error from 76.9% to 8.4% of the inlet speed in single-seed runs (uniform inlet flow: 11.0%). ISLA, a transformer for boundary-driven PDEs, reaches 5.3% over four seeds, about 4 times the change from a finer mesh. On the same test samples, a held-out geometry family barely changes ISLA's error relative to uniform flow, and a held-out enclosure or boundary-condition layout raises it 2.4 to 9.2 times. The data and the scoring code will be released.

$H_2$-optimal riblet profiles for turbulence suppression in channel flows

Daniel W. Hartman, Armin Zare

The drag-reducing properties of streamwise-elongated surface corrugations, commonly referred to as riblets, have been extensively studied through experiments, simulations, and reduced-order modeling. Notably, the linearized Navier-Stokes framework introduced by Ran, Zare, and Jovanovic (J. Fluid Mech., vol. 906, 2021) employs volume penalization to model riblet-induced boundary conditions and predict drag reduction. These approaches typically quantify drag reduction as a function of a single geometric parameter, such as spanwise spacing or groove cross-sectional area, evaluated over fixed riblet shapes. As a result, identifying optimal riblet geometries across a broader design space often necessitates exhaustive parametric studies. To address this problem, we propose an optimization framework for the systematic design of optimal riblet shapes. Accordingly, we reformulate the volume penalization term in the linearized dynamics as a structured state-feedback operator. Targeting maximal suppression of turbulence, we pose a composite optimization problem that minimizes the steady-state variance of velocity fluctuations and riblet-induced dynamical augmentations while maintaining a constant bulk-flux. For a fixed spanwise wavelength (riblet spacing), this framework yields optimal wall-normal profiles that achieve maximal turbulence attenuation.

Direct Numerical Simulation of Vortex-Induced Instability Over a Semi-Infinite Flat Plate with Discrete Roughness

Aditi Sengupta, Tapan K. Sengupta, Vajjala K. Suman

The receptivity of flow over a semi-infinite flat plate to a combination of deterministic free stream excitation and surface roughness, is investigated by solving the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation (NSE). The free stream excitation is in the form of a convecting vortex with fixed strength and sign, while the roughness element is a Gaussian bump. Two-dimensional (2D) and three-dimensional (3D) direct numerical simulations (DNS) are performed to determine whether the nonmodal nonlinear nature of transition, previously established for the vortex-induced instability in [Sundaram et al., Effects of free stream excitation on the boundary layer over a semi-infinite flat plate, Phys. Fluids, 32, 094110 (2020))], holds in the presence of surface roughness. The study aims to capture the fundamental differences arising due to the addition of surface roughness using instantaneous flow features, vorticity dynamics and application of nonlinear theories of receptivity. Roughness accelerates the transition process significantly, while retaining the indicator of nonmodal response, i.e. the spatio-temporal wave front (STWF). The inherently 2D nature of the vortex-induced instability evolves to three-dimensionality much faster in the presence of roughness, necessitating a 3D framework to capture the effects of roughness accurately. The cumulative response to the surface bump and convecting vortex can be separated into four stages, viz. are (i) generation of vortical disturbances at the roughness, (ii) a spatio-temporal amplification of the disturbance field, (iii) emergence and amplification of the STWF marked by sequence of unsteady separation bubbles, and (iv) emergence of turbulent flow due to merging of the unsteady separation bubbles to turbulent spots.

Gradient-Aware AI Agents for Autonomous Turbulence Model Discovery via Differentiable CFD

Yi Liu, Xiantao Fan, Mingqi Xu, Jian-Xun Wang

Turbulence modeling relies on human choices of closure form, physical inputs, and tunable parameters. Training closures a posteriori is efficient only with gradients through long chaotic rollouts, which few solvers provide. Most a posteriori approaches therefore resort to gradient-free optimization, and nearly all fix the closure form in advance, learning only its parameters. In this work, we introduce a gradient-aware agentic framework that poses closure discovery as a bilevel problem: a multi-agent large language model (LLM) system searches over closure forms, while a GPU-native differentiable computational fluid dynamics (CFD) solver supplies gradients, making differentiable a posteriori learning an action of the autonomous AI agent. For each proposal, the agents construct a differentiable training program by embedding the analytical, neural, or hybrid closure in the solver, implementing a differentiable objective, and validating the gradient path. They then decide whether to evaluate the closure, compute its parameter sensitivities, or train it a posteriori, select the optimization settings, and revise subsequent proposals using the simulation outcomes and returned gradients. Truncated-horizon gradients make this training usable under chaotic dynamics for closures with tens of thousands of parameters. Demonstrations include dissipation-law and subgrid-scale closure discovery in two-dimensional turbulence and wall-model discovery for large-eddy simulation of three-dimensional channel flow. With gradient-based training, the agent recovers reference coefficients that forward evaluation alone does not reach within the same budget, and autonomously discovers and trains analytical and neural closures that improve agreement with the targeted reference statistics. These results demonstrate the feasibility of end-to-end autonomous turbulence model development in time-resolved flows.

A high-dimensional spatial-physical coupling operator for generalizable super-resolution of three-dimensional turbulent flows

Zengyuan Zuo, Junjun Jiang, Fei Mu, Xubo Wang, Xianming Liu

Accurate representation of turbulent flows requires simultaneously capturing multi-scale spatial structures and the complex, nonlinear coupling between distinct physical quantities (\textit{e.g.}, velocity components and density). In practice, however, turbulent data are often constrained by low spatial resolution due to hardware limitations and computational costs. While current data-driven super-resolution methods have shown promise by learning mappings via convolutional neural networks (CNNs), they typically treat physical variables as independent channels. This simplified approach neglects intrinsic cross-variable correlations, causing models to struggle with high-fidelity reconstruction, particularly under extremely sparse conditions or in out-of-distribution regimes. To bridge this gap, we propose an architecture-agnostic spatial-physical coupling operator that extends the standard convolution into a joint spatial-variable dimension. Unlike conventional linear channel mixing, our framework constructs a unified spatial-physical representation within CNNs to explicitly capture local inter-variable interactions. By lifting features into a higher-dimensional manifold, this operator significantly enhances the model's representation capacity. Experiments demonstrate that this approach enables strong reconstruction performance and zero-shot generalization, offering a physics-aware inductive bias for super-resolution of three-dimensional turbulence. More broadly, this work suggests that high-dimensional operators offer a promising avenue for modeling intricate multi-scale turbulent flows.

Resolvent-based modeling of turbulent flow over multilayer compliant surfaces

Rutvij Bhagwat, Kourosh Shoele

Compliant coatings offer a passive approach to modifying near-wall turbulence and potentially reducing skin-friction drag, but high-fidelity exploration of the coupled parameter space is costly. We develop a two-way coupled resolvent-Galerkin framework for turbulent channel flow over finite-thickness, layered viscoelastic coatings. The fluid is modeled using the Navier-Stokes equations linearized about a turbulent mean state, while the solid is represented by a through-thickness Galerkin discretization of the elastodynamic equations. The solid response is condensed into a spectral surface-impedance operator at the fluid-solid interface. At $Re_τ= 180$, stiff panels approach the rigid-wall response, whereas more compliant panels modify the low-rank response associated with the near-wall cycle. A Reynolds-number study shows that panels with fixed nondimensional material stiffness can produce different responses depending on whether their thickness scales with inner or outer flow length scales. The influence of viscoelastic damping is examined across low- and high-frequency regimes, where stiffness-proportional damping alters the high-frequency fractional-energy distribution, while the low-frequency distribution remains relatively insensitive to damping. For multilayer panels, the fluid-facing material determines the interfacial wave speed, while the subsurface stiffness distribution modifies the panel's effective thickness. Temporal stability analysis identifies regions of parameter space where a steady harmonic response is relevant and regions where instabilities grow. Finally, a leading-order, covariance-based drag-estimation framework relates the coupled response to changes in the mean flow and to the estimated skin-friction drag. Together, these tools provide a basis for screening coating designs and selecting candidates for high-fidelity simulations and experiments.

Experimental Evidence of Elastic Instability in Viscoelastic Kolmogorov Flow

Christophe Brouzet, Mayank Chopra, Dario Vincenzi, Jason R. Picardo, Atul Varshney

Inertialess elastic instabilities can profoundly affect transport in complex-fluid flows, from mixing in microfluidic devices and displacement through porous media to polymer processing. While such instabilities are well understood in flows with curved streamlines, their origin in rectilinear flows like the prototypical Kolmogorov flow remains unresolved. Here, we report the first experimental evidence of the center-mode elastic instability, at vanishing inertia, in a confined quasi-two-dimensional viscoelastic Kolmogorov flow, driven by electromagnetic forcing. The instability is characterized by the emergence of "arrow-shaped" structures in the velocity field, which fluctuates chaotically as the forcing strength increases. We reproduce these observations in simulations of the Oldroyd-B model and trace the dynamics in the confined flow to the travelling arrowhead structures that emerge from the center-mode instability of unbounded Kolmogorov flow. Our work establishes a generic route to purely elastic instability in rectilinear flows, broadening the conditions under which elastic turbulence and enhanced mixing can arise.

Gas expansion in global models of nanosecond pulsed discharges: a reduced gas-dynamic model coupled to plasma kinetics

Konstantinos Kourtzanidis, Rention Pasolari

Global (zero-dimensional) kinetic models of nanosecond pulsed discharges describe the expansion of the heated gas channel (kernel) with simplified gas-dynamic closures. We show that these closures can lead to errors of tens of percent in the calculated gas density, temperature and species densities, especially in the first microseconds after every pulse. Here, we propose a fast, reduced gas-dynamic model of Lagrangian shells, closed by an approximate cylindrical acoustic radiation condition, with no fitted physical coefficient. The reduced model reproduces the axis density obtained by verified one- dimensional compressible computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations within 2.4% for smooth deposition profiles and axis pressures up to six times ambient, typically a thousand times faster than CFD. We couple it with our in-house global plasma-kinetics solver (SoPlasma-0D), whose energy partition after the pulse agrees well with that derived from measurements and with an independent calculation. Two microseconds after a single pulse in air, and compared with the proposed coupled model, the existing closures overestimate the axis density by up to a factor of 2.8, the atomic oxygen density by up to 2.7 and the temperature by up to 680 K. For repetitive pulses 200 or 430 ns apart, of equal energy per unit volume, no existing closure follows the coupled model at both intervals, in the gas density met by each later pulse and in the energy per molecule deposited by late pulses, which sets how strongly they dissociate and heat the gas. The proposed reduced model should therefore be used to describe the gas evolution during the expansion phase after every nanosecond pulse, and for repetitive pulses above about 100 kHz in kernels of radius up to about a millimetre.

Graph Neural Network based non-local models: Toward application to RANS turbulence modeling

Salvatore Civiello, Davide Oberto, Antonia Larese, Gianluigi Rozza

Most machine-learning closures for the Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) equations are local, predicting the closure quantities at each point solely from local flow features, despite the non-local nature of turbulent interactions. We propose a non-local closure framework based on Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) that exploits the mesh connectivity. Each finite-volume cell becomes a graph node, and cells sharing a face are connected by an edge. Through message passing, each layer aggregates information from neighboring nodes, so that after $k$ layers the prediction at a cell depends on its $k$-hop neighborhood, which naturally accounts for non-local effects. To make training feasible when each simulation yields a single large graph, we also introduce a breadth-first-search $k$-hop subgraph sampling strategy, which acts as data augmentation and cuts the cost per processed graph during training. We compare GCNs with feed-forward networks (FNNs) of equal size on two periodic-hills benchmarks: a laminar advection-diffusion transport problem that mimics the non-local character of RANS closures, and the turbulent eddy-viscosity prediction of the Closure Challenge dataset at $Re = 5600$. In the laminar case, FNNs are accurate only within the training regime and with sufficiently informative inputs, whereas GCNs generalize better to unseen geometries, including out-of-distribution flows, and partly compensate for missing input features. In the turbulent proof-of-concept case, the graph-based models achieve modest but consistent improvements compared to FNNs, supporting GCNs as a promising route to non-local RANS closures.

An LLM-assisted theoretical model for aeroengine installation noise scattering

Xun Huang

A theoretical model is developed for the canonical problem of aeroengine fan noise impinging on, and scattering from, a nearby semi-infinite rigid plate. The model chains two Wiener--Hopf problems in different coordinate systems. The first stage solves the duct-radiation problem analytically in axisymmetric cylindrical coordinates. The second stage expands the field incident on the plate into a double angular spectrum of plane waves: the axial spectrum is inherited from the first stage, while the spanwise spectrum is generated by a Weyl--Sommerfeld expansion of the cylindrical harmonics. Each spectral component is then scattered by the plate. The mathematical crux is the coordinate transformation between the axisymmetric duct frame and the Cartesian plate frame. The core physical insight--that the Weyl--Sommerfeld identity provides an exact spectral bridge between these systems--originates entirely with the authors. The heavy algebra of translating between the two spectral representations, together with the associated numerical verification, was carried out with the assistance of a large language model (LLM), under strict human oversight and independent numerical cross-checking. This division of labour enables the LLM to handle tedious symbolic manipulation without compromising rigour. Results are presented for spinning modes radiating from a cylindrical duct with a subsonic jet into a quiescent ambient. The case study demonstrates that, under systematic verification, LLMs can accelerate tedious algebra in theoretical fluid mechanics, contributing a concrete empirical example to the broader discussion on AI's role in theoretical research.
