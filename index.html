TurtLES: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Turbulent 3D Neural PDE Surrogates

Jarno Platenburg, Armand Kassaï Koupaï, Paola Cinnella, Patrick Gallinari

Machine-learning surrogates are increasingly used to accelerate computational fluid dynamics, yet progress is limited by the lack of benchmarks capturing realistic, time-dependent turbulent flows. We introduce TurtLES, a 14 TB dataset of 3D turbulent cylinder wakes in the shear-layer transition regime, computed via high-fidelity implicit large-eddy simulations. The dataset contains 400 trajectories of 400 time steps each, with 3-11 million points per frame, spanning variations in geometry, Reynolds number, and angle of attack. Unlike existing datasets, TurtLES combines (i) physically realistic turbulence with an active energy cascade driven by vortex stretching, (ii) dense irregular meshes, and (iii) long-horizon temporal dynamics. We benchmark state-of-the-art neural operators on autoregressive rollouts and identify key failure modes, including temporal instability, loss of small-scale energy, and challenges in learning on large irregular domains. TurtLES provides a new benchmark for evaluating surrogate models on 3D turbulent wakes at industrially relevant mesh resolution.

Projection-based segregated, iterative, and multi-stage physics-informed neural network for incompressible Navier-stokes equations

Zhenyu Zhang, Zhixiang Ling, Yinghao Huo, Huayong Liu, Yunde Su

Physics-informed neural networks(PINNs) offer a mesh-free and continuously differentiable approach to fluid simulation. However, standard monolithic PINNs for incompressible Navier-Stokes (NS) equations often suffer from severe constraint coupling and spectral biases, limiting both convergence and accuracy. To address this challenge, we propose a segregated, iterative, and multi-stage PINN (SIM-PINN) inspired by the classical projection method in computational fluid dynamics. In SIM-PINN, the nonlinear NS equations are decoupled into the linearized momentum and pressure Poisson equations. Each is addressed separately using dedicated multi-stage neural networks and coupled through projection-based inner iterations. Each stage of the multi-stage neural networks for the momentum equations corresponds to one iteration of the projection method, in which the velocity fields are progressively refined at targeted frequencies. Upon iteration convergence, solutions to the fully coupled and nonlinear NS equations are recovered. By integrating the segregated and iterative framework with the multi-stage training, SIM-PINN alleviates the optimization difficulties of the incompressible NS equations arising from nonlinearity, constraint coupling, and spectral biases. The improved convergence and accuracy are demonstrated by simulations of steady lid-driven cavity and unsteady Kolmogorov flows.

Bathyspheres, dust devils, lunar plumes and Martian drones: a generalised mathematical model for particle-fluid flow fields generated by static particle bed entrainment across macroscopic scales

S. Langdon, D. J. Needham

We consider a recently derived mathematical model for the mechanism by which a wind driven flow entrains particles into a fluid from an otherwise static particle bed, a process that is fundamental in initiating and driving the phenomenon of particle cloud formation. This model was previously applied to the "helicopter brownout" problem, in which, as a helicopter descends towards a bed of sand, the downwash and swirling flow from the rotors entrain particles into the air, forming a high-velocity particle-laden cloud around the helicopter. Here, we define a new measure of particle cloud formation potential, outline how the model can be incorporated into existing Computational Fluid Dynamics software, and extend the model to scenarios including: formation of particle clouds in the absence of swirl, such as those caused by a bathysphere descending to the ocean bed, or a rocket landing on the lunar surface, in which case we can precisely predict the critical height above the surface at which particle cloud formation is initiated; generation of dust clouds by less powerful downwash and swirling flows caused by drones or other smaller rotorcraft, such as NASA's Ingenuity Mars helicopter, in which case gravitational effects are included in the model; formation of dust devils (dust clouds formed within and around an upwardly circulating columnar air flow), in which case downwash is replaced by updraft. In each case, predictive models are derived through minor adaptations to key modelling assumptions.

Sub-filter-scale stress and heat flux in hypersonic Couette flow and a flat-plate boundary layer

Raagvendra Singh, Sabbir Ahmed, Lian Duan, Carlo Scalo, Takahiko Toki

A comparative study of sub-filter-scale (SFS) quantities in hypersonic Couette flow and flat-plate boundary layer turbulence under strongly cooled-wall conditions is carried out using filtered direct numerical simulation (DNS) data to characterize the differences in SFS dynamics between internal and external wall-bounded turbulence. The two flows exhibit similar near-wall behaviour in the mean velocity profiles and SFS stress components; however, marked differences emerge in the outer layer and thermal structure. The two configurations also exhibit different thermal fields, leading to different temperature-velocity relations. The SFS behaviour shows strong sensitivity to the filtering operation: the top-hat filter yields larger SFS magnitudes, whereas the sharp spectral filter preserves a distinct spectral separation near the cutoff, leading to characteristic SFS features. Decomposition of the SFS terms shows that the residual component provides the dominant contribution to the mean SFS shear stress and heat flux. Differences between the two flows are primarily associated with the interaction terms between the resolved and residual components, which contribute with opposite signs in the two configurations, consistent with their different thermal structures. The different thermal structures also lead to distinct SFS heat-flux behaviour, including a sign change in the flat-plate boundary layer. Instantaneous SFS fields further reveal that local extrema arise from the combined contributions of multiple decomposed terms. Although these terms exhibit large local fluctuations, their net contributions to the averaged SFS quantities are relatively small. The trace of the SFS stress exhibits local negative values, reflecting local energy redistribution across the filter scale rather than a loss of physical energy.

Zhen Zhang, Theo Käufer, Louise Ronglan, Michael S. Triantafyllou, George Em Karniadakis

We present a cross-validated dataset of turbulent wakes behind nine bluff bodies at Reynolds number Re = 10,000: a circular cylinder, a 2:1 elliptical cylinder at 0° and 90°, short and long isosceles triangles with the apex upstream or downstream, and a square cylinder at 0° and 45°. Each body was studied by wall-resolved direct numerical simulation (DNS) with a spectral-element solver and by time-resolved planar particle image velocimetry (PIV) in the MIT Intelligent Towing Tank. The DNS set-up is verified against reference cylinder data at Re = 3900, and DNS and PIV at Re = 10,000 are compared field by field. The dataset supports the development and testing of data-driven turbulence closures, reduced-order models, and wake-stability analyses across geometries with distinct separation behavior.

Boundary Layers, Displacement Flow, and the High-Frequency Response of Porous Media: Beyond the Viscous and Thermal Characteristic Lengths, Order by Order

D. Lafarge, A. Bouchendouka, Z. E. A. Fellah

The Johnson-Koplik-Dashen viscous characteristic length $Λ$ and its thermal analogue $Λ'$ introduced by Champoux and Allard govern the leading $O(ω^{-1/2})$ boundary-layer behaviour of fluid-saturated porous media, but omit higher microgeometric effects associated with interface curvature and field gradients. We develop the asymptotic high-frequency expansions of dynamic tortuosity and dynamic compressibility beyond the leading boundary-layer terms. By resolving the oscillatory Stokes and heat conduction problems in the thin boundary-layer limit, we establish a recursive hierarchy, which can be continued to any order, defining successive microgeometric parameters, given by wall integrals. Each order requires transport equations of WKB type across the boundary layer, closed by a non-secularity condition, together with one Neumann problem for the potential displacement flow that the boundary layer induces. Applied to the values fitted on highly absorbing foams, these expressions show that the negative surface parameter is consistent with strong in-parallel disorder of the pore sizes, whereas the large positive third-order term points to strong constrictions or enlargements along part of the flow paths. The thermal parameters reduce to geometrical quantities: the integral mean curvature of the pore walls, a Willmore-type measure of their departure from umbilicity and, at the next order, this measure weighted by the mean curvature. Using the Avellaneda-Torquato relaxation-mode representation, we show that all these parameters are spectral data of the distribution of relaxation times. For isolated sharp edges across which the fluid flows, the singular terms of the formal expansion cancel, and the description persists with apparent, logarithmically varying values of the parameters.

On the aerodynamics of an accelerating and decelerating propeller in hover at low Reynolds numbers

Manuel Ratz, Christophe Schram, A. Sciacchitano, Miguel A. Mendez

Small unmanned aerial vehicles commonly regulate propeller thrust through rapid changes in rotational speed, yet it remains unclear how the inflow, the wake, and the near-surface velocity respond to these changes at low Reynolds numbers. A commercial two-bladed propeller was tested in hover with custom blade holders at three additional pitch offsets relative to the original blade geometry, $Θ=\,$0, 5, and $10^\circ$, and accelerated and decelerated between 3000 and 5000 RPM with open-loop step responses. The resulting nondimensional acceleration and deceleration numbers, below 0.011 and 0.007, suggest negligible acceleration effects. Phase-locked particle image velocimetry (PIV) at 40, 60, and $80\,\%$ rotor radius and in the meridian plane was combined with load measurements. The thrust coefficient responds quasi-steadily at $Θ= 10^\circ$, but shows hysteresis at the higher pitch offsets, with lower values during acceleration and higher values during deceleration than under steady conditions. This trend opposes expectations from dynamic inflow theory. The effect is largest at $Θ= 5^\circ$, with deviations from the steady values of up to 8\,% during acceleration and $5\,\%$ during deceleration, compared with $4\,\%$ and $3\,\%$ at $Θ= 10^\circ$. PIV reveals that the rotor-disk inflow, blade deformation, and sectional circulation remain largely quasi-steady, whereas velocity fluctuations, blade-wake vorticity, and tip-vortex circulation vary strongly, with the largest deviations at low rotational speeds rather than at peak acceleration or deceleration.

Bayesian Kolmogorov-Arnold networks for trustworthy Reynolds-stress closures

Ali Eidi, Kherlen Jigjid, Tyler Buchanan, Richard P. Dwight

Data-driven turbulence closures can improve Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) predictions, but their use is limited by two issues: learned constitutive relations are often difficult to inspect, and predictions typically lack reliable uncertainty estimates. We develop a Bayesian tensor-basis Kolmogorov-Arnold network (BKAN) for Reynolds-stress anisotropy that addresses both within a single closure. Learnable univariate spline functions on network edges provide an inspectable representation that can, where sufficiently simple, be approximated symbolically. Variational posteriors over spline coefficients yield a predictive distribution separating epistemic uncertainty, due to limited training data, from aleatoric uncertainty, the irreducible scatter of high-fidelity targets at given inputs. Galilean and rotational invariance, symmetry, and tracelessness are enforced through the tensor basis, while physical realisability is treated separately through soft regularisation. A controlled regression benchmark assesses predictive accuracy, calibration, and Bayesian parameterisation. Placing the posterior on spline coefficients gives the best overall balance of accuracy, calibration, parameter cost, and interpretability. Applied to Reynolds-stress anisotropy, the BKAN is accurate in distribution, with calibrated, informative uncertainty that tends to identify larger prediction errors. Under geometric extrapolation, however, the model becomes overconfident: epistemic uncertainty increases but does not fully account for growing prediction errors. Finally, the framework is extended to three-dimensional wind-farm wakes, where the learned correction transfers to unseen layouts and posterior samples are propagated through the RANS solver, with the aleatoric component injected as a spatially correlated field, to obtain uncertainty bounds on the predicted wake flow.

Microdroplet Generator Surrogate Enhancement and Inverse Design Through an Uncertainty-Guided Framework

David Gabriel Harispe, Matías Gerard, Pablo A. Kler

Microfluidic droplet generation is critical for many applications, yet efficient device design in high-dimensional parameter spaces remains computationally prohibitive due to complex multiphase flow dynamics. This work presents a unified methodology that couples adaptive surrogate enhancement with simulation-driven inverse design. High-fidelity Volume-of-Fluid simulations provide ground-truth data, while neural network ensembles predict flow regimes and droplet diameters. Ensemble-based uncertainty quantification identifies epistemic uncertainty using consensus levels for regime classification and the coefficient of variation for diameter prediction, revealing that uncertainty concentrates near regime transition boundaries where physics is highly sensitive to parameter perturbations. A physics-informed enrichment strategy targets these critical zones and addresses class imbalances by deploying additional targeted simulations and retraining the surrogates forming the ensembles. A framework that integrates Particle Swarm Optimization for constrained inverse design, balancing accuracy with predictive confidence and physical consistency is therefore proposed. Validation on independent test datasets demonstrates that the enriched ensembles achieve higher accuracy, with the fraction of predictions within the 0-3% error range on droplet diameter nearly doubling, and high-error cases (above 10%) dropping substantially. The inverse design procedure reliably identifies physically realizable configurations meeting target droplet sizes. This approach significantly reduces computational costs while ensuring reliable predictions, offering a framework with potential applicability to the optimization of a broad range of microfluidic systems.

Frimpong A. Baidoo, Narayana R. Aluru, Thomas J. R. Hughes, Michael R. A. Abdelmalik

We assess the non-linear version of the Entropy Stable Extension to the Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations which were originally derived in arXiv:2407.17334. Using an identity for the derivative of the inverse of linearized collision operator, we show that the Entropy Stable Extension takes the form of a fourth-order quasi-cubic system of equations when written in entropy variables. Furthermore, we find that the quasi-linear contributions to the extension retain the highest order derivative contributions and the entropy stability of the full extension. This quasi-linear approximation is applied to the stationary heat transfer between parallel plates where we find that a specification of the boundary conditions that interpolates between the continuum and collisions limits can yield temperature profiles that better agree with DSMC predictions than Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations with Smoluchowski boundary conditions, while also giving a heat flux prediction that better agrees with the Sherman-Lees heat flux than the Navier-Stokes-Fourier prediction. We conclude with a brief study of dispersion of plane waves according to the linearized equations in one dimension where we show that all frequency modes are stable and relate this to entropy stability.

An Analytical Model for Laser-Activated Two-Phase Choking and Microfluidic Suction on Surface Boundary Layers

D. Tsiklauri

We present a predictive fluid mechanics model to calculate pressure drops (0.25 atm) and fluid velocities (2.50 m/s) governed by localized phase change and choking phenomena in a capillary-constrained, coaxial microfluidic actuator. Driven by near-infrared thermal absorption, the analytical framework describes how energy deposition drives vapor cavity growth and subsequent cavitation bubble collapse, inducing a transient suction force across a curved, two-radii Young-Laplace capillary boundary. Velocity drops are derived from closed-form analytical equations that explicitly couple vapor volume dynamics with non-linear flow area restriction and fluid drag. The model predicts a maximum boundary layer wall shear stress of 30.06 Pa at the target interface. Crucially, this shear magnitude matches the physical threshold required to mechanically strip surface contaminant boundary layers while remaining below the damage threshold of underlying target substrates. Finally, we formulate a closed-form expression for the maximum allowable liquid inlet temperature required to prevent thermal saturation and sustain stable, continuous microfluidic suction cycles. These equations establish direct engineering design rules for optimizing phase-change microfluidic actuators and next-generation coaxial fluidic systems.

FlowReconBench: Accuracy, calibration and cost of learned and classical flow reconstruction from sparse sensors

Nicolas Tricard, Linzheng Wang, Jason Chen, Sili Deng

Reconstructing full flow fields from sparse sensors requires prior assumptions about unmeasured regions and physical variables. Yet it remains unclear when learned models outperform classical estimators and which aspects of their predictions can be trusted. FlowReconBench evaluates ten learned and five classical methods on three regimes: a laminar cylinder wake across Reynolds numbers, two-dimensional Kolmogorov turbulence and a large-scale three-dimensional (3D) isotropic turbulence. Methods share leakage-controlled splits and sensor draws and are scored on per-channel error, ensemble spread, calibration, spectra and cost. The leading method changes for each regime. Classical methods perform strongly in low-order and dense-sensing regimes. On the cylinder wake, linear stochastic estimation and gappy proper orthogonal decomposition lead, including for unobserved pressure. In 3D, inverse-distance weighting matches the best learned methods on observed channels. Data-driven methods become superior at decreasing sensor density or poor sensor observability. On Kolmogorov flow, continuous ranked probability score and error yield different method rankings: generative models offer broader but underdispersed ensembles, while deterministic models prioritize pointwise error. Generative models balance this tradeoff through their sampler budget, with a larger budget improving ensemble quality at higher computational cost. In 3D, scaling tests demonstrate that queryable methods are flat in memory, while full-grid construction methods run out of memory. Observing only 2 of 4 fields leaves the problem kinematically underconstrained, yet a latent model can reproduce structural similarities to the ground truth.

Domain-Adapted Deep Optical Flow for Multiphase Velocimetry in Unsteady Cloud Cavitation

Naga Nitish Chamala, Sanjay Vasanth Kethanur Balasubramaniam, Olivier Coutier-Delgosha

Time-resolved velocimetry inside cloud cavitation remains a fundamental measurement problem. Particle image velocimetry (PIV) requires uniform tracer seeding and undistorted particle images, and cavitating flow violates both, because tracers do not penetrate vapor structures and refractive index mismatch across deforming interfaces produces spurious vectors. We present a domain-adapted deep optical flow framework for two-phase velocimetry, built on a Recurrent All-Pairs Field Transforms (RAFT) architecture fine-tuned on synthetic image pairs rendered from multiphase OpenFOAM simulations (RAFT-CloudCav). It is evaluated against a pre-trained generalist RAFT model, Farnebäck and OpenPIV on 130,000 frames/s recordings of a Venturi test section at 30 and 48 L/min. As no experimental ground truth exists, validation uses three quantities that require none, namely the integrated flow rate against a turbine meter, the liquid-phase velocity divergence, which mass conservation requires to vanish, and the station-to-station spread of the flow rate. RAFT-CloudCav recovers 47.75 L/min against a metered 48.0 $\pm$ 0.5 L/min, the closest of the four methods, and its flow rate varies by 2.0% across four streamwise stations, versus 6-7% for the classical methods. It returns a coherent near-wall reverse flow of -0.71 m/s, the time-averaged signature of the upstream-propagating re-entrant jet, where OpenPIV returns none. In the non-cavitating control, at 51 $μ$m/pixel only three grid points fall inside a 0.68 mm boundary layer and the peak wall-normal gradient is under-predicted more than thirtyfold, while 13 and 19 $μ$m/pixel agree with each other. The mean field is recovered without tracer particles at roughly three times the per-frame uncertainty. Uncertainties are estimated a posteriori from the image residual and propagated conservatively.

Michal Shavit, Yongji Wang, Tristan Buckmaster

We present a computational framework that combines machine-learning-based discovery with rigorous computer-assisted numerical certification and formal verification for strongly nonlinear partial differential equations. We demonstrate the approach for periodic gravity-capillary traveling water waves. Physics-informed neural networks are used as a search mechanism, with the wave profile, wave speed and, in continuation experiments, surface tension learned directly from the governing equations without training data. Through a staged training and refinement procedure, the neural networks achieve near-machine-precision residuals of the governing equations, even in strongly nonlinear regimes. A single formulation recovers high-amplitude Stokes waves, Crapper waves and Wilton ripples. Continuation from a high-amplitude Wilton ripple in surface tension reveals new high-amplitude candidate solutions. We freeze one candidate as an explicit finite representation and rigorously certify a uniform bound on its governing-equation residual using interval arithmetic, accounting for floating-point roundoff, Fourier truncation, aliasing and grid-to-continuum errors. This independently validates the neural-network accuracy. We formalize the certification argument in Lean, which machine-checks the deductions from certified numerical bounds to the residual theorem. Both the interval certification and formalization were developed with substantial assistance from a large language model, while the validity of the result remains independent of the language model: the numerical bounds are rigorously enclosed by interval arithmetic and the subsequent mathematical deductions are checked by the Lean kernel. Together, these steps provide a pipeline from neural discovery to rigorous numerical certification and machine-checked verification with auditable mathematical guarantees.

A Quantum Mechanical Description of Active Matter

Ghansham Chandel, Siddhartha Das

The natural environments habitable to life are inseparable from dissipative and entropic forces, which mandate active matter to operate on constant fluxes of energy and momentum to survive and propagate through spacetime. In contrast to fundamental particles which are characterized by energy and momentum, we postulate that energy expenditure rate and momentum generation rate are the fundamental dynamical quantities driving this evolution of swimming active matter and accordingly construct the corresponding Lagrangian and Hamiltonian of motion of the active matter. Furthermore, living systems broadly employ periodic, non-repeating motion for both locomotion and various biological functions, with meaningful information stored in the periods of these functions. Consequently, analyzing these dynamics in the Fourier space lands us at the familiar tools of the operator based, quantum mechanics. This is accomplished by introducing a complex field to describe the evolving probability distribution of a single unit, as well as the population density of mutually non-interacting active particles under the constraint of scarce resources. The resulting single-unit wavefunction evolves analogously to the Dirac equation with minimal coupling, wherein gauge symmetry reflects the generality of the governing equations across arbitrary resource landscapes. Additionally, we present a Schrödinger-like equation where the effective potential term represents the environmentally mandated, non-motile base metabolic rate. Finally, we formalize the bidirectional coupling between active swimmers and the surrounding fluid via a multipole expansion of the active force with an emphasis on force monopoles and dipoles, where each pole corresponds to a distinct mode of active motility.

A coupled lattice Boltzmann model for pore-scale mushy-layer dynamics with application to sea ice

Nicola Trippella, Dario Maggiolo

A pore-scale lattice Boltzmann model is developed for reactive transport in phase-changing porous media, coupling solute transport in the liquid with conjugate heat transfer across the solid and liquid phases. Two methodological developments are introduced: a novel link-based Volume-of-Pixel formulation for direction-dependent interface motion, and a generalized Robin boundary treatment for solute redistribution at moving interfaces. The phase-change formulation is validated against thermal benchmarks, while the Robin treatment is assessed through literature test cases, exhibiting convergence rates between first and second order. The coupled model is then applied to a local pore-scale segment of a sea ice brine channel in a purely diffusive regime. The simulations recover the expected ideal mushy-layer behaviour under appropriate scale separation. At sea-ice-relevant concentration ratios, thermal perturbations produce relatively large variations in brine salinity, causing freezing rather than melting to reshape the pore geometry in a non-uniform way. The resulting framework provides a pore-scale numerical tool for resolving coupled temperature, solute redistribution, and phase-boundary dynamics in mushy layers.

Orientation and slip statistics of spheroids settling in turbulence

Anubhab Roy

Small spheroids settling through turbulence are turned broadside-on by fluid inertia and tilted away by velocity gradients. We describe their orientation and slip statistics through the crossover from random orientation to alignment, in the overdamped limit. With quasi-static Stokes drag the leading inertial torque is an exact gradient flow on the orientation sphere, its potential quadratic in the cosine of the tilt and only rescaled by mobility anisotropy. The orientation performs an overdamped random walk in a quadratic well, and isotropic Gaussian white noise gives exactly a Boltzmann distribution. For forcing of finite correlation time, a closure keeps this form with its width set by linear response; it recovers the uniform density as settling vanishes and, for fixed-orientation forcing statistics, the aligned coefficient of rapid-settling theory, and errs most in the crossover. The root-mean-square lateral slip peaks where the alignment rate approaches the Kolmogorov rate, as faster settling strengthens the sideways drift at a given tilt but suppresses the tilt; the nearly shape-independent ratio, which tensor-forced simulations reproduce, lets a measured peak constrain the torque coefficient or dissipation rate for given forcing statistics. Dissipation intermittency enters as a lognormal superposition of Boltzmann distributions, which fattens the tails but leaves the leading aligned mean-square tilt unchanged. The superposition reproduces the shape of plate orientation distributions from direct numerical simulations, where a single Boltzmann distribution fails; published moments confirm the predicted spread beyond the weakest torques, and at strong alignment the measured fourth-order tilt moment lies between the two predictions.

In the lattice Boltzmann method (LBM), the momentum-exchange method (MEM) is widely used to evaluate hydrodynamic forces at fluid-solid interfaces due to its computational efficiency and geometric flexibility. However, when mapping complex geometries onto structured Cartesian grids, the physical boundary rarely intersects the lattice links exactly at their midpoints. This discrepancy violates an implicit assumption inherent to the conventional momentum-exchange (CME) formulation. In this study, we systematically analyze this geometric deficiency and demonstrate that it induces significant force inaccuracies, even in elementary flows where the solid boundary deviates from the midway position. To resolve this, we propose a modified momentum-exchange (MME) method that explicitly accounts for the exact boundary location along the lattice links, demonstrating enhanced accuracy for both flat and curved geometries. Furthermore, we address another critical issue with the interpolation-supplemented bounce-back (ISBB) schemes, and that is their inherent violation of local mass conservation. This mass leakage induces unphysical pressure buildup in the system, which compromises the accuracy and stability of the LBM. We propose a mass-conservative remedy for ISBB schemes that eliminates this leakage. Through a series of benchmark simulations of increasing complexity, we demonstrate that combining the proposed MME with the mass-corrected ISBB scheme drastically improves the fidelity of hydrodynamic force evaluations, establishing a robust framework essential for moving boundary problems and fluid-structure interactions.

We construct a divergence-free velocity field $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t)$ on $\mathbb{T}^{n}$ that is a genuine function of position and carries both defining properties of turbulent transport: Richardson pair dispersion and a dissipative anomaly. No synthetic field has carried both, because the frame that gives the first destroys the field the second requires. The key is a dichotomy: deforming a spectrally localised pattern smears it across scales, whereas pushing its generating centres forward and re-convolving leaves the spectral envelope exactly invariant --- so transporting the eddy centres preserves both cascade and frame. In the field the finite-size Lyapunov exponent obeys $ζ(R)=h(R)-1$ threshold by threshold within the scaling range, with a sweeping deficit $Δ_{\rm sw}=+0.038\pm0.019$, consistent with zero, against $+0.091\pm0.019$ for the frozen field on the same data, a gap that doubles at Navier--Stokes intermittency. Companion papers prove that this family shows Richardson dispersion and a dissipative anomaly (Fefferman and Mayboroda 2026), and that a scalar advected by this flow obeys Yaglom's relation (De Lellis 2026), at small Kubo number. Trajectories forget their starting point as the smallest eddy is removed: the median separation of pairs released from one point with independent Brownian kicks varies as $κ_{p}^{0.0333\pm0.0038}$ over 22 decades of particle diffusivity, against $κ_{p}^{1/2}$ for a smooth control. A scalar in the same field closes the exact scale-by-scale variance budget, four independently measured terms balancing the dissipation to 15\% over 1.2 decades with no adjustable constant, and reaches its inertial--convective asymptote $S_{L}=-χr$ to $0.9995\pm0.0018$ once the measured diffusive transfer is restored, while under an imposed mean gradient $χ$ changes by 14\% over a factor 5.8 in $κ$.

A characteristic mapping method for dissipative evolution equations

William Holman-Bissegger, Seth Taylor, Jean-Christophe Nave

The Characteristic Mapping (CM) method is a transport scheme which discretizes the Lagrangian flow map as a composition of spatial interpolants. This adaptive approximation technique has demonstrated novel resolution properties and the ability to efficiently simulate the fine scales of inviscid turbulent flows. In this work, we construct an extension of this approach to the solution of dissipative problems, including the Navier-Stokes equations. The extension lifts a general scalar evolution equation to a path in a group of diffeomorphisms, where the semigroup approach of the CM method can be exploited. We give an interpretation of this lift through the geometric framework of optimal transport. We then introduce a semidirect product evolution technique to perform the time stepping of the map components. The map is evolved based on the semi-Lagrangian CM approach, and its log-Jacobian is evolved explicitly by an exponential integrator. The numerical scheme is demonstrated to be high-order accurate and is tested on a range of canonical nonlinear dissipative equations. By retaining the resolution properties of the CM method, the approach shows a promising ability to simulate turbulent fluid flows across all Reynolds numbers.
